# Voice & ethics guide

This skill simulates real people. Done well, it's a thinking tool. Done carelessly, it's parody or misinformation. These rules keep it on the right side.

## Fidelity

- **Capture the shape of their thinking, not a costume.** A good simulation reaches the same *conclusions for the same reasons* the person would — it isn't catchphrases stapled to your own opinion.
- **Read primary sources first.** Their essays, letters, and long interviews beat someone else's summary of them.
- **Voice means structure, not impressions.** Match sentence rhythm, level of abstraction, what they're blunt vs. careful about, their characteristic moves (a PG essay reasons from a surprising premise; a Buffett letter teaches through folksy analogy). Avoid cartoonish tics.

## Honesty

- **Never fabricate a quotation.** If you present something in quotation marks as their words, it must trace to a real source. Otherwise it's *your paraphrase of their view* and must read that way.
- **Separate documented from extrapolated.** "They have argued X" (sourced) vs. "extending that, they'd probably say Y" (your inference). Make the seam visible.
- **A thin dossier stays thin.** Don't invent depth for reclusive figures. Less material → more hedging, not more confidence.
- **Label every consult as a simulation** of public work, not the real person.

## Ethics

- **No defamation.** Critiques must be real and sourced. Never invent scandal, malice, or private facts.
- **Public material only.** Build only from what people chose to publish. No private communications, leaks, or speculation about their private lives.
- **Counterpoint, not character assassination.** The goal is sharper thinking for the user, not dunking on the advisor.
- **Respect the person.** Simulate them as their best, most rigorous self would argue — that's both fairer and more useful.

## Why this matters for a public repo

This repo is shared. Anyone can read the dossiers and run the consults. Fabricated quotes or invented criticism become *misinformation about real people* the moment someone screenshots them. The bar is: everything here would survive the person reading it.
