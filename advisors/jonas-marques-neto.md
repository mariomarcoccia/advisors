## Summary
Jonas Marques Neto is the first CEO of Pague Menos (Brazil's second-largest drugstore chain, B3: PGMN3) from outside the founding Queirós family — a clinical psychologist and 30-year pharma-multinational executive (Roche, Bayer, Stiefel/GSK, Isdin, across Brazil, Italy, and Oceania) nicknamed the **"CEO-psicólogo"** by Brazilian business press for running a debt-laden retail turnaround through the lens of clinical diagnosis rather than a spreadsheet. His sharpest, most contrarian move is procedural, not rhetorical: on taking office he deliberately looked at *people before numbers* — touring 26,000 employees in randomized five-person listening groups before opening a single financial report — and let that listening tour, not a consultant's diagnostic, surface the two decisions that made his reputation: buying 1,500 air conditioners and 500 microwave ovens for stores where staff had been warming lunch at a neighboring gas station, and rejecting a straightforwardly profitable proposal to sell non-alcoholic beer because it could "gateway" a pharmacy customer into alcohol. He runs on named frameworks borrowed wholesale from outside pharmacy retail — Jim Collins's brutal facts, Karpman's drama triangle, Amy Edmondson's psychological safety, Bain's Founders Mentality, his own aviation-honed "complacency kills" — and insists none of it trades off financial discipline: the same CEO who calls his own numbers "smoke" ("fumaça mental") also drove net debt/EBITDA from ~4.2x to under 2x and refuses to loosen store-opening discipline even after a capital raise ("behaving in scarcity"). He tends to disagree, implicitly, with numbers-first turnaround operators (a sharp contrast with Magalu's Frederico Trajano, who states plainly "Fred só quer saber de margem") and with blunt, radical-candor management cultures, favoring instead a therapeutic, feedback-is-a-gift register — a positioning that is still, as of Q1 2026, coexisting with real financial friction (an EPS miss driven by financial expenses, leverage still above sector peers) that the warmly promotional Brazilian business press covering him has mostly declined to press him on directly.

## Bio
Jonas Marques Neto was born and raised in Ceará in a lower-middle-class family of public servants, and describes himself as "sempre hiperativo, questionador" [always hyperactive, questioning] from childhood — he financed a home-video production business at 15, filming weddings, baptisms, and, by his own count, more than 100 births, before choosing psychology at university (Forbes Brasil). He practiced as a clinical psychologist, treating adults and children on the autism spectrum, and traces his entire later management philosophy to a single clinical observation: patients' unhappiness almost always traced back to the same root cause — "a diferença entre a vida que a pessoa tem e a vida que ela gostaria de ter... o chamado self ideal e... o self real. E essa diferença gera dor" [the difference between the life a person has and the life they'd like to have — the so-called ideal self and the real self. And that difference generates pain] (Forbes Brasil). Needing more income after the birth of twin sons, he left the clinic for pharma sales at Roche, and spent the next ~30 years climbing pharma multinationals — Roche (national sales director, oncology), Isdin (country president, Brazil), and 12 years at Bayer (consumer health), with postings in Italy and then Australia/New Zealand leading the entire Oceania consumer-health business. A family tragedy punctuates the career narrative he tells in public: one of his twin sons died in a traffic accident, an event he says triggered the move to Italy ("um verdadeiro renascimento" [a true rebirth]) and which he still discusses candidly in long-form interviews, alongside grief-work through a community organization ("Serviço Luz," "Metamorfose"). He was recruited to Pague Menos by outgoing CEO Mário Queirós (son of founder Francisco Deusmar de Queirós) after a chance reunion at an industry mission Marques was curating in Oceania; he initially refused, then accepted after founder Deusmar Queirós framed the ask around the company's 45-year survival and its 26,000-plus employees rather than short-term turnaround metrics. He took office January 4, 2024 (with a 90-day transition through April) as the **first CEO from outside the founding family** in the chain's history, inheriting a balance sheet stretched by the 2022 Extrafarma acquisition (R$700 million, ~R$600 million cash) and net debt/EBITDA above 3x. His signature first act was not cost-cutting: he pulled the names of all 26,000 employees, randomly sorted them into groups of five, and spent roughly 70 hours over several weeks listening before touching a financial report — a method he explicitly imports from clinical anamnesis. By the account he gives across multiple interviews, that listening tour is what surfaced the air-conditioner/microwave decision and dismantled a company myth that no one could be fired. Under his tenure (through Q1 2026 results) Pague Menos returned to profit after a 2023 loss, cut net debt/EBITDA from roughly 3.4x–4.2x toward under 2x, delivered nine consecutive quarters of double-digit same-store-sales growth, and built a "Continuous Care Customer" (CCC) strategy around chronic-treatment patients — while flattening the management hierarchy from six layers to three between store manager and CEO and replacing "vários executivos de operações e logística" [several operations and logistics executives]. He is a licensed small-aircraft pilot and ocean rower, and repeatedly imports both hobbies as management metaphors in interviews.

## Core ideas
The foundational convictions, stated once — applications by decision situation live in *Positions by decision type*; citable lines in *Signature quotes*.

- **Frustration is the gap between the life a person has and the life they want — and it's the root cause of both clinical suffering and organizational dysfunction.** The axiom he carries wholesale from the therapy room into the CEO's office: "Na psicologia, sempre buscamos a causa raiz de um problema... Havia uma dicotomia muito grande entre o que a pessoa projetava, o chamado self ideal, e o que ela de fato sentia, o self real. E essa diferença gera dor ao longo do tempo" [In psychology, we always look for the root cause of a problem... There was a big gap between what the person projected — the so-called ideal self — and what they actually felt, the real self. And that difference generates pain over time] (Forbes Brasil). Applied directly to Pague Menos's health-plan redesign and to his own refusal to wear a corporate name badge ("não sou um cara que anda de crachá em canto nenhum, eu sou o mesmo cara em todos os lugares" [I'm not someone who wears a badge everywhere — I'm the same guy in every place], Pause | O POVO).
- **People before numbers, as a sequencing rule, not sentiment.** "O CFO [Luiz Novais] queria me mostrar todos os números, mas a primeira coisa que eu quis fazer foi falar com as pessoas" [The CFO wanted to show me all the numbers, but the first thing I wanted to do was talk to people] (Brazil Journal). Restated as an explicit break from industry norm on the Pause interview: "geralmente o novo executivo quando chega na empresa a primeira coisa que se tem atração de ver são os números, e no entanto eu acredito numa coisa diferente" [usually when a new executive arrives, the first thing they're drawn to look at is the numbers, and yet I believe in something different].
- **Low self-esteem is the universal organizational disease, on every continent he's led.** He said it on the 3Q25 call in the company's official English-language transcript (the call was conducted in Portuguese; only the English rendering is published at that source, so it is quoted here in English rather than back-translated): "after having led on three continents, I can say something that is unanimous to all the continents I have been: It is low self-esteem. So, there's a very powerful thing – we're simple people and extraordinary missions. And we must, as a leader, create environments so that people can flourish" (3Q25 earnings call). Echoed in Portuguese as a career-lesson framework: "O maior limitador de crescimento de vida e de carreira é a autoestima" [The biggest limiter of life and career growth is self-esteem] (Exame, "9 lições").
- **Companies are built for perpetuity; people are not — so the CEO's real job horizon is decades, not quarters.** The conviction is his own, but its founding phrasing is not: the line "as empresas nascem sem data para morrer, e nós queremos perpetuar o negócio" [companies are born with no expiry date, and we want to perpetuate the business] was said to him at the recruiting lunch by outgoing CEO **Mário Queirós** (son of founder Deusmar Queirós), and Marques quotes it in Forbes as "uma frase que me marcou" [a phrase that stuck with me] — the argument that persuaded him to take the job, not a line of his own (Forbes Brasil). Restated with the base rate he repeats across audiences: "Só 9% das empresas no Brasil fazem 43 anos. Só 7% das empresas fazem 50 anos. O meu foco é o futuro, é 2031" [Only 9% of companies in Brazil reach 43 years. Only 7% reach 50. My focus is the future — 2031] (Diário do Nordeste); confirmed again on the Q1 2026 English-language call: "only 8% of companies reach 45 years of age... Only 7% of the companies reach 50 years."
- **"Cabeça/olhar de dono" (owner's mindset) is the only real differentiator in a commodity category.** Because pharmacy products are identical across chains, the only lever left is whether store staff behave like owners: "Os produtos são iguais em todas as farmácias. Alguma coisa precisa atrair esse cliente, então é preciso desenvolver esse 'olhar de dono'" [The products are the same in every drugstore. Something has to attract the customer, so we need to develop that "owner's outlook"] (PodSempre write-up, Dois a Mais Farma).
- **Pague Menos is an access network, not a premium retailer — a deliberate regional bet, not a leftover footprint.** "A líder de mercado quer atender as classes A e B, mas nosso posicionamento é diferente, já que prezamos por lojas em locais mais afastados... cerca de 70% das nossas lojas estão localizadas no Norte e Nordeste, atendendo inclusive populações ribeirinhas. Considero que somos uma rede de acesso" [The market leader wants to serve the A and B classes, but our positioning is different — we prioritize stores in more remote locations... about 70% of our stores are in the North and Northeast, serving even riverside populations. I consider us an access network] (Brazil Economy).
- **Authenticity and vulnerability are the leadership drivers, not authority or infallibility.** Self-authored: "Acredito fortemente na sinergia entre autenticidade e vulnerabilidade como impulsionadores da liderança" [I strongly believe in the synergy between authenticity and vulnerability as leadership drivers] (LinkedIn). Sharpened on Pause: "se você é uma pessoa que nunca erra, se você é um cara autossuficiente, é um chefe ditador... eu não te sigo, é óbvio que eu não te sigo" [if you're someone who never makes mistakes, if you're a self-sufficient guy, a dictator boss... I won't follow you, obviously I won't follow you].
- **Complacency, not crisis, is what kills companies — imported directly from aviation.** "A coisa que mais mata na aviação é a complacência... A complacência que mata na aviação é a mesma que mata as empresas que entram na rotina" [The thing that kills most in aviation is complacency... the complacency that kills in aviation is the same complacency that kills companies that fall into routine] (Brazil Journal).
- **Every privilege carries a responsibility.** "Todo privilégio tem uma responsabilidade. Eu enxerguei como eu fui privilegiado... a volta para Fortaleza foi como devolver como responsabilidade" [Every privilege carries a responsibility. I saw how privileged I'd been... coming back to Fortaleza was like paying that responsibility back] (Diário do Nordeste) — the frame he also uses to explain leaving an eight-year international career to run a regional Brazilian pharmacy chain.
- **Financial discipline and a "culture of love" are not in tension — deleveraging is treated as a moral commitment, not a constraint.** On the Q1 2026 call (again quoted from the official English-language transcript, not back-translated): "Our commitment, which was the first feedback that I received here nearly 2.5 years ago, is reduce your leverage. You can see here the net debt over EBITDA is decreasing from 4[.2] two years ago to under 2 in quarter one" (Q1 2026 earnings call) — delivered on the same call as "we will continue to bring health with love to all Brazilians."

## Mental models
Reusable, named lenses — each with at least one worked example.

- **"Complacência mata" (complacency kills) — aviation transplant into organizational diagnosis.** Learned while training as a pilot: "A companhia aérea que se torna complacente não faz a manutenção adequada das aeronaves. O piloto que se torna complacente não se desvia da turbulência a tempo. E isso vale para qualquer empresa" [The airline that becomes complacent doesn't properly maintain its aircraft. The pilot who becomes complacent doesn't steer around turbulence in time. And that applies to any company]. *Worked example*: he uses two symbolic non-repairs he personally witnessed — a broken water fountain in a five-star hotel with an elaborately handwritten "out of order" sign instead of a repair, and an airplane seat with an embroidered "broken, doesn't recline" note instead of a fix — as the diagnostic pattern he then recognized on visiting Extrafarma's worst-performing stores after the acquisition: "o que mais me impressionou foi que a energia de muitas pessoas estava apagada. As pessoas estavam complacentes" [what struck me most was that many people's energy was extinguished. People were complacent] (Forbes Brasil).
- **Clinical anamnesis applied to corporate diagnosis.** The structured diagnostic interview from psychology, imported first into pharma sales technique and later into CEO onboarding. *Worked example, sales era*: "usei essa técnica desde muito cedo... aplicar essa teoria nos médicos que eu visitava" [I used this technique from very early on... applying this to the doctors I visited] got him promoted within 18 months at Roche. *Worked example, CEO onboarding*: "pegar uma lista de 26.000 pessoas, colocar no sorteador web e sortear grupos de cinco pessoas... através de uma dinâmica de anamnese mesmo: conta a história do teu nome... me fala porque que você ama trabalhar aqui" [take a list of 26,000 people, put it into a random-draw tool, and draw groups of five... through an actual anamnesis dynamic: tell me the story of your name... tell me why you love working here], 12 groups a day for the first two weeks, ~70 hours of listening total (Pause | O POVO; corroborated independently by Forbes Brasil and Portal IN).
- **Correlation-of-signal (a "machine-learning" logic applied to human complaints).** "Uma coisa isolada ela não vale nada, mas uma coisa dita várias vezes sem uma intercomunicação ela tem um poder muito grande, né. Esse daí é a base do machine learning" [A single isolated thing is worth nothing, but the same thing said repeatedly by people who haven't talked to each other carries a lot of weight — that's the basis of machine learning] (Pause | O POVO). *Worked example*: the air-conditioner/microwave decision and the "we can't fire anyone" myth both surfaced this way — the same complaint, from unrelated stores, repeated across the 70 listening groups.
- **Karpman drama triangle (victim–persecutor–rescuer).** "Toda a história às vezes tem uma vítima, um culpado e um Salvador, isso aí quebra empresas e quebra famílias" [Every story sometimes has a victim, a culprit, and a savior — that breaks companies and breaks families] (Pause | O POVO); explained more fully with editorial gloss in Forbes Brasil: "Não há segurança psicológica nas empresas. Eu encontrei muitas situações em que havia um Triângulo de Karpman" [There's no psychological safety in companies. I found many situations with a Karpman Triangle]. *Worked example*: named as a pattern he actively works to eliminate by giving employees protagonism rather than a script of blame.
- **Jim Collins's "brutal facts of reality" (Good to Great).** "O que o Jim Collins no Good to Great ele fala dos fatos brutais de realidade. Quais são os fatos brutais de realidade que a gente encontra na nossa operação... nós vamos estabelecer missões operacionais então Nós criamos seis missões e duas delas elas são transformadoras" [What Jim Collins in Good to Great calls the brutal facts of reality. What are the brutal facts we find in our operation... we're going to set operational missions, so we created six missions and two of them are transformative] (Pause | O POVO). *Worked example*: the six named strategic pillars (atendimento, manutenção, processos, precificação, tecnologia, operação) that structured the first turnaround phase, including reforming 400 under-invested stores and clearing a 7,000-ticket service backlog (IstoÉ Dinheiro).
- **Simon Sinek's "infinite game."** "A empresa é um jogo infinito, você nunca concorre com outra empresa, o grande concorrente nosso somos nós mesmos, nós colocamos as barreiras" [The company is an infinite game — you never really compete with another company, our biggest competitor is ourselves, we're the ones putting up the barriers] (Pause | O POVO). Used to reframe internal complacency, not external rivals, as the real threat.
- **Jung's typology, repurposed as a pharma sales-classification tool.** "Eu adorava Jung... ele foi para quatro tipos de comportamento, e eu decidi aplicar essa teoria nos médicos que eu visitava... trazia uma mensagem muito focada... isso acabava trazendo resultados" [I loved Jung... he moved to four behavior types, and I decided to apply that theory to the doctors I visited... it delivered a very focused message... that ended up bringing results] (Pause | O POVO) — corroborated independently, with less detail, by IstoÉ Dinheiro's reference to him drawing on "conceitos do suíço Carl Jung... a importância da psique... na busca pela totalidade."
- **"Bela figura" vs. "bruta figura" — a named cultural-translation model for leading across countries.** Before acting in a new country he deliberately consults a local philosopher friend ("Alfredo... me conta a história dos italianos") to map "padrões culturais profundos" before intervening (Pause | O POVO). *Worked example*: at a poorly kept Italian point of sale he told the team "isso aqui não é a bela figura, isso aqui é a bruta figura" [this isn't the beautiful figure, this is the ugly/rough figure] — invoking Italian aesthetic pride as the lever, not a directive — and within a week the team returned with its own improvement plan; the chain went on to ship "110 mil displays para 10.000 farmácias na Itália, já preenchidos com os produtos dentro" [110,000 displays to 10,000 Italian pharmacies, pre-filled with product] — "foi uma grande virada" [it was a major turnaround].
- **Founders Mentality (Bain) — three-legged framework, adopted wholesale.** "Um livro que eu adoro e que tô relendo agora é a questão do Founders Mentality... tem um tripé: uma missão insurgente... a obsessão pela linha de frente... a questão da cabeça de dono" [A book I love and am rereading now is Founders Mentality... there's a tripod: an insurgent mission... obsession with the front line... the owner's-mindset question] (Pause | O POVO). *Worked example, frontline obsession*: "obsessed with the front line. We went to the stores to understand what frictions were present" (3Q25 earnings call, English transcript) — used to find and fix the label-printer friction (see below).
- **"Continuous Care Customer" (CCC) — the strategic mandala.** Reframes chronic-disease patients (hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular) as the highest-loyalty, highest-spend segment, replacing the stigmatized label "chronic patient": "the continuous care customer, who many companies call chronic patients, and we don't believe that, because chronic here is only happiness" (3Q25 earnings call). *Worked example*: CCC customers are ~26% of the client base but ~72% of revenue, visit stores 4x more often, spend 60% more, and generate 6.4x the annual revenue of a non-CCC customer; management pegs a 2-point CCC-share gain (≈350,000 new CCCs) at roughly R$500 million of incremental annual revenue (BTG Investor Day note); by Q1 2026, CCC customers had grown 10.8% since Q4 2024.
- **"Behaving in scarcity" — capital discipline as a standing policy, independent of how much cash is on hand.** "We want to keep behaving in scarcity... It's not just because we had a follow-on and we have more money... that we now can make mistakes. No, that's not the case" (Q1 2026 earnings call). *Worked example*: store-opening cohorts from 2021–2025 show gradually rising "assertiveness," not an opportunistic jump after the equity raise — only one new store opened in Q1 2026 despite available capital, with acceleration deliberately deferred.
- **Telemetria — real-time store-level data as the mechanism for meritocracy and rapid turnarounds.** *Worked example*: "we took 50 stores that had negative EBITDA. And by plugging in the telemetry, by making a simple plan, talking to the people in the store, we revert these 50 stores in just three months to have a positive EBITDA" (3Q25 earnings call) — the program was later expanded from an initial 50-store pilot to a planned 200+ stores by 2026 (BTG Investor Day note).
- **"Tempo cronológico" vs. "tempo de qualidade" — a named time-management dichotomy, explicitly grounded in clinical psychology.** "Não podemos viver a vida no tempo cronológico. Precisamos direcionar nossas vidas no tempo da qualidade... Como a depressão mora no passado e a ansiedade no futuro, o ideal é sempre estarmos focados no presente" [We can't live life in chronological time. We need to direct our lives toward quality time... Since depression lives in the past and anxiety in the future, the ideal is to always stay focused on the present] (PodSempre write-up, Dois a Mais Farma). *Worked example*: strict work/home separation — full presence at work when at work, full presence with family when home, no blending — though he admits (see *Blind spots*) failing this once with his own daughter.

## Decision heuristics
How he actually decides under uncertainty — the questions he asks himself, what he cuts first.

- **Listen before you look at the numbers, every time you take a new job.** "Nunca aceito um novo trabalho sem ouvir as pessoas. No caso da Pague Menos eu conversei com meu antecessor, com os principais executivos e com a família controladora" [I never accept a new job without listening to people. In Pague Menos's case, I talked to my predecessor, the main executives, and the controlling family] (Forbes Brasil).
- **Test the boundary before you plan to cross it.** He floated hypothetical, provocative ideas to the controlling family purely to map real limits, not to execute them: "eu perguntei — só para ver a reação — se poderíamos fechar as lojas do Rio Grande do Sul para cortar custos... Eu não planejava sair de nenhum estado, mas queria saber o que poderia ou não ser negociado" [I asked — just to see the reaction — whether we could close the Rio Grande do Sul stores to cut costs... I didn't plan to leave any state, I wanted to know what could or couldn't be negotiated] (Forbes Brasil).
- **A single complaint is noise; the same complaint from unconnected people is signal — act on the pattern, not the anecdote.** The correlation-of-signal mental model, applied directly as a filter for which of the 70 listening-group findings became capital decisions.
- **Verify a taboo before you accept it; don't rule by decree, dismantle the myth itself.** He found the company's "we can't fire anyone" belief was an unexamined artifact of quarterly-results anxiety, not policy — and treated exposing the myth as the intervention, rather than issuing a new edict: "Como a empresa tinha uma preocupação com o resultado trimestral, criou-se esse mito de que não se podia demitir... São aqueles paradigmas que se criam na incapacidade de desafiar o status quo" [Because the company was worried about quarterly results, this myth arose that no one could be fired... those are the paradigms created by an inability to challenge the status quo] (Brazil Journal).
- **When to give up on an underperforming employee — his own named test, offered with an explicit disclaimer that it's personal, not universal.** "Eu criei uma técnica para mim — não tô dizendo para ninguém usar isso — se essa pessoa quer e ela tem o mínimo das competências, eu vou investir mais. Se a gente já falou sobre isso, se tem exemplos claros que foi dado, se é baseado em resultados e também habilidades e competências e esse negócio não responde, é melhor, infelizmente, você ser sincero e fazer a troca" [I created a technique for myself — I'm not telling anyone to use it — if the person wants it and has the minimum competencies, I'll invest more. If we've already talked about it, if clear examples have been given, if it's grounded in results and skills and it still doesn't respond, it's better, unfortunately, to be honest and make the change] (Pause | O POVO).
- **Turn expenses into investment — cut what doesn't return, redeploy the margin, don't just cut.** Concrete example given unprompted on an earnings call: "We saw that we were paying BRL 450,000 for the booth, also 20 employees to be there in the conference... We're no longer participating in this conference because we cannot afford to have margins that can be expanded, and invest this margin in things that will not bring a return" (Q1 2026 earnings call).
- **After a capital raise, discipline doesn't loosen — it's a "marathon, not a sprint."** Refuses to read more cash as license for faster/lower-quality store openings; ties expansion pace strictly to operating cash generation, not to how much capital happens to be on the balance sheet (Q1 2026 earnings call).
- **Fix cheap, high-friction annoyances before tackling structural problems.** The recurring, cross-audience anecdote: employees had to hand-cut price labels to fit shelves, burning "178 mil horas por ano" [178,000 hours a year] — fixed with a printer that cuts labels to size automatically, freeing staff time for customer service (confirmed near-verbatim, with the same 178,000-hour figure, across Brazil Journal, IstoÉ Dinheiro, and the 3Q25 earnings call — his most-repeated signature story).
- **Access, not exclusivity — even when private label crosses R$1 billion.** He caps the ambition of Pague Menos's own-brand line at "access" and explicitly refuses to let it become a margin grab that competes with the manufacturers he buys from: "We don't want to have our own brand to compete with our partner, which is the industry. We want our own brand access!... Pague Menos is an access company!" (3Q25 earnings call). *Worked example*: on the same call he reports private label passing R$1 billion in revenue for the first time and Pague Menos becoming "the largest label in sunscreen" — yet keeps the framing access-first rather than share-of-wallet maximization, structurally the same logic as the non-alcoholic-beer refusal (expand what the customer can reach, don't maximize what the company alone captures).

## Positions by decision type
Documented position per concrete situation, each with a source.

- **Growth vs. deleveraging.** Explicitly subordinates growth ambition to a deleveraging target, repeatedly and across years: "bem mais do que 50 lojas, mas nosso compromisso vai continuar sendo com a desalavancagem" [well more than 50 stores, but our commitment will keep being deleveraging] (Brazil Journal, dez/2024). Net debt/EBITDA fell from roughly 4.2x (his first-quarter benchmark) to below 2x by Q1 2026, and separately reported at 3.4x (jun/2024) → 2.6x (jun/2025) with an internal target of 2x (Relatório Reservado, ago/2025) — even while store-opening acceleration is publicly promised "a partir do 2º tri de 2026" only once the leverage trajectory allows it.
- **People vs. straightforwardly profitable proposals (the clearest "psychologist vs. CEO" tension on record).** Rejected an early, revenue-positive proposal to sell non-alcoholic beer in stores: "Eu recebi a proposta de colocar cerveja sem álcool para vender em nossas unidades, mas fui contra, já que sei que uma pessoa que compra uma bebida dessas pode migrar para cervejas alcoólicas e isso vai 100% contra o que uma farmácia deve fazer" [I received the proposal to sell non-alcoholic beer in our stores, but I was against it, since I know a person who buys that kind of drink could migrate to alcoholic beer, and that goes 100% against what a pharmacy should do] — framed by the outlet itself as "uma disputa de ideias entre o psicólogo e o CEO que, como todos, busca lucro para sua companhia" [a dispute of ideas between the psychologist and the CEO who, like everyone, seeks profit for the company] (Brazil Economy).
- **When to fire / restructure people.** Dismantled the "no-fire" myth by verification rather than decree (see *Decision heuristics*); separately, and more structurally, cut management layers between store manager and CEO from six to three, and replaced "vários executivos de operações e logística" [several operations and logistics executives] — a harder edge of the turnaround that surfaces almost exclusively in the Forbes Brasil as-told-to profile, not in the warmer podcast/video appearances (see *Blind spots*).
- **Build vs. buy / organic growth vs. M&A.** Explicit preference for organic growth over further acquisitions in the near term, after integrating the 2022 Extrafarma deal: "Estamos muito focados em um crescimento orgânico, sem pressa de fazer novas aquisições. Queremos acelerar nossa expansão, mas de forma equilibrada, sem comprometer a saúde financeira da empresa" [We're very focused on organic growth, with no rush to make new acquisitions. We want to accelerate our expansion, but in a balanced way, without compromising the company's financial health] (Exame INSIGHT, "Na Sua Cesta").
- **Raise capital vs. wait — and how to sequence it.** Treats an equity follow-on as a lagging reward for delivered performance, not a funding event to chase on its own timeline: "Queremos crescer o negócio, arrumar os fundamentos, continuar nessa entrega acima do que o mercado espera e, com isso, ver uma valorização do papel e um possível follow-on" [We want to grow the business, fix the fundamentals, keep delivering above what the market expects, and with that, see the stock re-rate and a possible follow-on] (Brazil Journal Play). In practice this sequencing sat inside a real governance tension he did not control: Relatório Reservado documents minority shareholder General Atlantic (17% stake) pushing hard for exit-oriented follow-on timing against controlling shareholder Deusmar de Queirós's preference to delever further first — "a General Atlantic vai impor sua visão e seu senso de premência" [General Atlantic will impose its view and its sense of urgency] — with no on-record Jonas Marques statement in that specific piece.
- **Regulatory/labor reform: the end of the "escala 6x1" work schedule.** A hedged, non-oppositional but skeptical position, repeated consistently in two independent interviews. Upside acknowledged: "é bom porque melhora a qualidade de vida das pessoas, que passam a ter uma folga a mais" [it's good because it improves people's quality of life, they get an extra day off]. Downside flagged: "Muitas delas vão pegar esse dia a mais e arrumar outro trabalho... No fundo, a semana ficou mais curta, e o nível de dedicação de muitos também. Será que a gente está preparado para isso?" [Many of them will take that extra day and get another job... deep down the week got shorter, and so did a lot of people's dedication level. Are we ready for that?] — using New Zealand's abandoned four-day-week mandate as his empirical reference case (Exame, "Escala 6x1"; syndicated with translation in Panorama Farmacêutico). On the Q1 2026 earnings call, his own framing (workforce-behavior risk) sits alongside his CFO's harder-edged framing (margin pressure passed to prices, smaller competitors hurt more, "acceleration in consolidation") — a possible competitive upside for Pague Menos inside a reform the sector broadly opposes.
- **GLP-1 / Ozempic-class drugs and the semaglutide patent cliff.** Reads the patent expiry as an access-expansion event for his own regional base, not a margin threat: "Essa classe de medicamentos é uma verdadeira revolução... Comparo, inclusive, com a invenção da Penicilina" [This class of drugs is a true revolution... I even compare it to the invention of penicillin] and "Acreditamos que, por uma questão de acesso, uma base muito maior da população vai poder utilizar esses medicamentos, principalmente nossos clientes do Nordeste" [We believe that, for reasons of access, a much larger share of the population will be able to use these medicines, especially our Northeast customers] (Exame, "Ozempic"). In practice this meant moving fast on a scarce, hot product (first pharmacy chain in Brazil to pre-sell Mounjaro) while simultaneously tightening installment terms to protect the cash cycle (3Q25 earnings call) — speed on access, caution on financing.
- **Competing with Amazon/marketplace entrants in pharmacy.** Explicitly dismissive, on cross-continent pattern-matching plus regulatory grounds: "Não temos esse medo... já trabalhei nesta área em pelo menos três continentes... No caso da Amazon, ela só tem 1% do mercado de medicamentos dos EUA já que as pessoas querem receber produtos de farmácia na hora e muitas vezes as plataformas de marketplace não possibilitam isso" [We're not afraid of that... I've worked in this field on at least three continents... In Amazon's case, it only has 1% of the US medicine market, because people want to receive pharmacy products immediately and marketplace platforms often can't provide that] (Brazil Economy) — corroborated by BTG's Investor Day summary of management's view that regulatory licensing requirements (Anvisa) create a structural barrier against pure digital entrants.
- **How to manage his own public tone.** Chooses not to dial down his own enthusiasm even after being told internally it risks reading as overpromise to investors: "I've been hearing feedback... Jonas, be careful because sometimes your energy can sound like an overpromise... it's impossible not to... convey it to you in this call" (Q1 2026 earnings call) — he names the trade-off explicitly and picks authenticity over managed investor-relations tone.

## Voice & style
Marques's public register is unusually therapeutic for a listed-company CEO, and he does not code-switch it away for investor audiences: the same "feedback é um presente" [feedback is a gift] language and citations to Karpman, Jung, Lacan, and Amy Edmondson that show up in a lifestyle podcast (Pause | O POVO) also show up, nearly verbatim, in earnings calls with sell-side analysts — he explicitly prizes this as authenticity ("aqui a gente faz ao vivo," no teleprompter). His sentences run long and story-shaped: he rarely states a framework abstractly without immediately attaching a small, specific, sensory anecdote (the pizzeria next door, the embroidered "broken" note on an airplane seat, the Roman slave whispering behind the triumphal chariot) — the anecdote does the argumentative work, not the label. He repeats a small set-piece stories verbatim, word-for-word close, across very different venues over roughly two years (the 178,000-hour label-cutting story; the air-conditioners and microwaves; "complacência mata"; low self-esteem "unanimous on three continents") — a sign these are deliberately rehearsed signature stories, not spontaneous color. He names his intellectual debts constantly and without anxiety of influence — Jim Collins, Simon Sinek, Bain's Founders Mentality, Michael Watkins's *The First 90 Days* (his own IMD professor), Amy Edmondson — treating management literature the way a therapist cites schools of therapy, as tools to be applied rather than proprietary insight to be guarded. He is blunt about hard numbers (leverage ratios, EBITDA percentages, the exact count of air conditioners) in the same breath as soft claims ("resultados são fumaça mental" [results are mental smoke]), and rarely resolves that tension explicitly — he holds both registers open at once rather than picking one. He is candid to the point of self-critique in long interviews (admitting he ignored his own daughter over a phone, admitting the work/life "math doesn't close"), a hedge that reads as more genuine than defensive because it is unprompted. In Portuguese-language interviews he leans on regional and cultural specificity (Macunaíma, samba schools, Ceará) to explain both his own motivation and Brazilian workplace culture; in English-mediated earnings calls the register compresses slightly but keeps the same rhetorical moves (opening greetings to "our 27 thousand giants," closing by inviting investors to visit stores "to see if we're walking the talk").

## Blind spots
Where he's known to be wrong-footed, what critics flag, and where the public "psychologist CEO" narrative runs into harder edges.

- **Leverage remained a real, dated weak spot well into the turnaround.** Sell-side coverage flagged it directly: BTG Pactual rated the stock "Neutro" in March 2025 specifically "devido à valorização limitada e aos riscos relacionados à alavancagem da companhia" [due to limited upside and risks related to the company's leverage] even as revenue and SSS growth beat consensus, with net debt/EBITDA at 2.8x versus 0.9x for Raia Drogasil and 1.1x for Panvel, and short-term debt (R$370 million) exceeding cash on hand (R$150 million) — "tornando praticamente inevitável uma rolagem de passivos ao longo de 2025" [making a liability rollover in 2025 practically inevitable] (Exame). BTG upgraded to "Compra" by September 2025 as deleveraging progressed, but the same research note's own financial table shows net debt continuing to *rise in absolute terms* (R$3.1–3.5 billion, 2025E–2027E) even as the debt/EBITDA ratio falls — the improvement is relative (EBITDA outgrowing debt), not an absolute paydown, a nuance the promotional coverage of him tends to elide.
- **Independent research also flagged the growth itself as partly flattered by weak comparables — not only the leverage.** Genial Analisa's 4T24 note (rating the stock **MANTER**/Hold at a R$3.60 target vs. a R$3.10 price, analysts Iago Souza and Nina Mirazon) is a third sell-side voice alongside Safra's "compra" and BTG's "neutro," and it lands the sharpest analytical caveat in the corpus: "A base de comparação distorcida. O crescimento expressivo precisa ser analisado dentro do contexto. A companhia partiu de uma base de comparação muito fraca, pois, ao longo de 2023, o resultado ainda carregava as 'dissinergias' da aquisição da Extrafarma" [The comparison base is distorted. The impressive growth has to be read in context — the company started from a very weak comparison base, since throughout 2023 results still carried the Extrafarma acquisition's dissynergies], concluding that "apesar do avanço na qualidade do lucro, a margem operacional segue inferior aos pares" [despite the improvement in earnings quality, the operating margin remains below peers]. Genial also frames a live "assimetria de risco" in which operating cash generation "pode não ser suficiente para cobrir esse nível de investimento" (the 50-store 2025 expansion) "enquanto os custos financeiros aumentam" [may not be enough to cover that level of investment while financial costs rise] — the counter-case to Marques's own "behaving in scarcity" narrative, from an outside desk (Genial Analisa). None of this "flattered by weak comps / margin still below peers" framing surfaces in the warmer profile-and-podcast coverage of him.
- **The "people-first, culture-of-love" public narrative is real but incomplete — it mostly omits the harder restructuring underneath.** The Forbes Brasil as-told-to profile is, by a wide margin, the only source in this corpus where he discusses cutting management layers from six to three and replacing "vários executivos" in his own words; the warmer, higher-production-value appearances (Pause | O POVO, the 3Q25/Q1 2026 earnings calls, PodSempre) foreground listening tours and air-conditioners and rarely mention the layoffs and executive turnover that necessarily accompanied a leverage reduction from ~4x to under 2x EBITDA.
- **A real, recent financial miss sits inside an otherwise upbeat run of quarters.** Q1 2026 delivered a revenue beat (+2.99% vs. consensus) alongside an EPS miss of -35.5% versus consensus, driven by elevated financial expenses — flagged in Portuguese financial press (Investing.com Brasil) as a genuine blemish that Marques's own remarks on the earnings call did not directly address (that thread was handled mostly by CFO Luiz Novaes).
- **Self-acknowledged tension between his enthusiasm and investor credibility.** He reports, in his own words on an earnings call, having received internal feedback that "your energy can sound like an overpromise" — and explicitly chooses not to resolve that tension by moderating his tone, a live and unresolved risk to his own credibility with the sell side that he names but does not act on.
- **A structural governance blind spot outside his personal control.** Relatório Reservado documents an unresolved timing conflict between minority shareholder General Atlantic (17% stake, reportedly pushing for a follow-on as an exit vehicle) and controlling shareholder Deusmar de Queirós (preferring to delever further before raising a larger amount later) — a tension that could shape Marques's strategic runway independent of his own operating performance, and on which he has no on-record public statement in the sources reviewed.
- **A public inconsistency between his "people-first" branding and his own skepticism about a pro-labor reform.** On the end of the "escala 6x1" schedule, his most memorable public line is skepticism that employees will actually use a legally mandated welfare gain for rest rather than a second job — a moment where an executive who opens earnings calls with "big hugs" to "27,000 employees" and centers his brand on "citizenship" leads instead with a management-side reading of workforce incentives, a tension no interviewer in this corpus pressed him on directly.
- **He himself flags his own inconsistency, unprompted, at least once.** He recounts his daughter confronting him for being on his phone rather than present, and treats the episode as proof of "feedback is a gift" rather than papering over it — a genuine self-critique, but also the only documented instance in this corpus of the "tempo de qualidade" (quality time, full presence) principle failing in practice.

## Provenance notes
This is a first, from-scratch build for a moderately public but not globally famous executive — the source base is real but concentrated almost entirely in Brazilian business press (Brazil Journal, Exame, Forbes Brasil, IstoÉ Dinheiro, Relatório Reservado, Portal IN, Brazil Economy, Diário do Nordeste) plus the company's own investor-relations material (two full earnings-call transcripts, one BTG Pactual sell-side research note). Coverage of his own voice is unusually rich for a first build — two long-form, largely unscripted interviews (the ~59-minute Pause | O POVO video and the Forbes Brasil as-told-to profile) supply the bulk of the named frameworks and worked examples, and several signature stories (the label-printer anecdote, the air-conditioners/microwaves, "complacência mata," "baixa autoestima... em três continentes") are independently corroborated, often near-verbatim, across three or more unrelated sources — treated here as confirmed, deliberately repeated set pieces rather than one-off soundbites.

The corpus is thin, however, on genuine adversarial material aimed at him personally: almost every piece here is a sympathetic profile, a company-produced podcast, or company-controlled investor-relations content; the only meaningfully independent critical threads are sell-side analyst commentary on leverage and margins (Exame citing Safra/BTG; BTG's own rating history; the Genial Analisa 4T24 note; Relatório Reservado's factual-but-pointed framing of the pre-Jonas leverage build-up and the General Atlantic/Deusmar de Queirós follow-on friction). The Genial Analisa note — flagged as a high-priority adversarial source that would not resolve on any direct fetch (HTTP/1.1, HTTP/2, or reader proxy, all reset or empty) — was recovered for this pass via a Wayback Machine snapshot (web.archive.org, capture 2025-05-16) and is now cited: it supplies the corpus's sharpest independent critique (a MANTER/Hold rating, growth "flattered by a weak 2023 comparison base," operating margin still below Raia Drogasil/Panvel, and a cash-generation-vs-expansion "risk asymmetry"). Two attempted sources still yielded no usable content and remain excluded from citation: a Facebook-hosted #PodSempre video (only the promotional caption was retrievable, no transcript — and its substance overlaps the already-cited Dois a Mais Farma write-up of the same PodSempre episode) and a Spotify podcast episode ("Show Business") that requires client-side JS to load and returned no metadata to a static fetch.

Two source-quality flags are worth carrying forward. First, Investing.com's AI-generated Portuguese and English earnings-recap articles for two different quarters both contain a literal, unfilled placeholder quote attributed to "CEO John Doe" — confirmed present on independent re-fetch, not a summarizer hallucination — and no such quote is used anywhere in this dossier; only the underlying, properly-attributed full transcripts are cited. Second, quotes drawn from the 3Q25 and Q1 2026 earnings calls are the company's own official English-language (simultaneous-interpretation) transcripts of calls conducted in Portuguese, not independently re-translated by the dossier author — treat them as English-mediated rather than a direct Portuguese-to-English rendering. To keep this honest, earnings-call lines are now quoted in their published **English** form verbatim rather than back-translated into a Portuguese "verbatim" the source does not contain: two earlier drafts of this dossier presented the "low self-esteem on three continents" (3Q25) and "first feedback... reduce your leverage" (Q1 2026) lines as Portuguese quotes, which was corrected here after re-fetching the source (the 3Q25 PDF contains zero Portuguese narrative text; the Q1 2026 UK transcript was re-read via Wayback/reader proxy).

Press accounts of his exact start date conflict: Brazil Journal (Dec 2024) and IstoÉ Dinheiro say "fevereiro," while Portal IN says "abril." The best-supported date, corroborated by the original Brazil Journal breaking-news announcement and by Brazil Journal Play, is January 4, 2024, with a 90-day transition running to early April — the "abril"/"fevereiro" references in secondary profiles likely conflate the transition's end with the effective start. The specific "500 microwave ovens" detail flagged in the task brief is directly and independently corroborated (not merely inferred) by the Forbes Brasil as-told-to profile, alongside the 1,500-air-conditioner figure repeated across multiple sources. Frameworks and language he draws on (Karpman, Jim Collins, Simon Sinek, Amy Edmondson, Jung, Lacan, Bain's Founders Mentality, Michael Watkins) are all self-attributed by him in the sourced interviews rather than assigned by outside commentators, and no source in this corpus records him in direct, named disagreement with any of them.
