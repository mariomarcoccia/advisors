## Summary
Caito Maia is the founder and CEO of **Chilli Beans**, Latin America's largest eyewear brand (about 1,000 to 1,400 points of sale depending on the source; R$1.2 bi 2025 revenue per the company), a failed-musician-turned-brand-manager who turned a commodity (200 sunglasses in a California suitcase, 1997) into a fast-collection, pop-culture-licensed, franchise-funded brand. His lens is **brand-as-storytelling over product and aesthetics-as-strategy**, screened through his own investor rubric, *"arroz, feijão, amor e pimenta"* [rice, beans, love and pepper: the accounts, the passion, the differentiator]: *"a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço e parcela"*, *"estética não é detalhe, é ferramenta"*. His sharpest, most contrarian positions: deliberately un-integrate (sold his owned stores to franchisees, manufactures 100% in China, keeps only brand and IP); treat crisis as oxygen management (*"o único oxigênio que você tem é um site"*); name Jobs and Bezos as not his heroes; and tell founders an investor is not a prerequisite (*"eu construí a Chilli Beans sozinho"*). He opposes Buffett's static moat, Arnault's scarcity and Bezos's metric-first logic, and differs from Rony Meisler on story versus purpose. Weak on operational and financial rigor and on the franchisee's downside: three sourced franchise disputes, plus a contested financial-distress episode since 31 Aug 2026 (NeoFeed, citing anonymous sources, reports that a judicial-recovery filing is being studied; the company denies any judicial-recovery discussion and confirms advanced negotiation with creditor banks; outlets report debt figures from R$216 mi to about R$600 mi, unreconciled; on 30 Sept, per NeoFeed, the securitizer declared early maturity of a debenture Caito personally guarantees; a restructuring executive joined on 1 Oct 2026). Strong on brand, licensing, crisis communication, fast collections and franchising as an operating system.

## Bio
Caito Maia (Antonio Caio Gomes Pereira Filho, b. March 25, 1969, São Paulo) is founder and CEO of **Chilli Beans**. His path is unusual: ages ~12 to 22 in the US (Texas, Boston, LA, Miami; "fiz faculdade de música nos Estados Unidos"), lost 50kg at 16 through brute discipline, and chased music for ~15 years across bands (Luzes; Silvia James, signed to Warner; Las Ticas Tienen Fuego, a top-5 finalist among 3,000 bands in an MTV VMA contest) before it collapsed. A prior wholesale eyewear venture (Blue Velvet) blew up when two large clients defaulted after he scaled from importing 200 to 18,000 units, *"levou à falência inesperada"*, the formative lesson being that the brand is the only durable asset (Exame, NEEX 2025; Museu da Pessoa). Chilli Beans began in 1997 with 200 sunglasses in a suitcase from California, sold from a Venice-Beach-style cart; the name came in a dream. He traces his first real lesson in brand-building to selling in São Paulo's **Mercado Mundo Mix** street-fashion market, his own site calls it his *"faculdade de varejo"* [retail college], *"Aprendi a importância de marca no Mercado Mundo Mix."* (ABF, jun/2025; caitomaia.com). The defining move came at the start of the 2000s: he franchised, and over time **sold 100% of his ~130 owned stores to franchisees** to become a pure brand manager, *"gestor de marca, ponto."* 

He sat on **Shark Tank Brasil** as a fixed judge for six consecutive seasons (2017 to 2023 by his own site's count; his LinkedIn Experience page lists the role as continuous, 2017, present, "9 years", the two accounts are in tension, flagged in Provenance), returned for a guest appearance in 2024, and came back as a full-time "veteran Shark" for the 10th season (2025), a program he calls *"mais do que um programa, é uma missão"* [more than a show, it's a mission] (Portal Making Of, set/2025). He has co-authored two books distilling the Chilli Beans playbook: **"E se Colocar Pimenta?"** (with branding journalist Rodolfo Araújo, first published by Elsevier in 2012, re-issued "sem cortes" [uncut] by Editora Alta Books in 2018), structured around a **three-phase company lifecycle: "sobrevivência, decolagem, vida adulta"** [survival, takeoff, adulthood], and **"Se Parar o Sangue Esfria"** (Editora Gente, 2024), organized around three pillars: **Humanização, Ação, Marca** [Humanization, Action, Brand]. He almost went bankrupt during the pandemic and was hospitalized for a week with Covid, an experience he says "destabilized me psychologically... and I still pay some prices for that to this day", a rare, undramatized admission of ongoing personal cost (SBT "The Noite", jun/2025, carried from an earlier research pass with no URL in sources and not re-found by search in this pass; lower confidence). He is a lens on **brand-as-storytelling, aesthetics-as-strategy, fast-collection retail (~10 new models/week, ~1,000 active models, ~4M pieces/year), pop-culture licensing as a moat, crisis leadership, and franchising as an operating system.**

Additional biographical record (this pass). After his resale business went bankrupt, he told the 2019 Fórum audience, the first learning was *"Até então vendia um produto e precisava de uma marca"* [until then I sold a product and needed a brand] (Mercado&Consumo, out/2019), and in a July 2019 Q&A he wrote *"Já tive quebras no início da marca"* [I've had failures early in the brand's life]. Accounts of why Blue Velvet ended differ and are recorded, not reconciled: two large clients defaulting (Exame, NEEX 2025; Museu da Pessoa) versus Startupi (fev/2022), which says the business *"era uma atacadista com um bom volume de vendas, mas não gerava caixa, então precisou ser fechada"* [was a wholesaler with good sales volume that did not generate cash, so it had to be shut down] (the outlet's words, not Caito's). He says his father was a music teacher who did not encourage the career, and that three professional bands had label contracts (Mercado&Consumo, jul/2019). He is the father of four per a corporate blog (MAG Seguros, Potencialize 2024), hosts an entrepreneurship radio program named like his second book on 89FM São Paulo (Martins Fontes listing), and was presented as ABF's "Personalidade do Ano" at the 2025 ABF Franchising Expo (Portal do Franchising blurb, 6 Aug 2025; the video was not transcribed).

Financial distress since 31 August 2026 (contested; see Blind spots for the sourcing). That day NeoFeed reported, citing anonymous *"Fontes do setor"*, that Chilli Beans was *"prestes a entrar com pedido de recuperação judicial"*, with documents that *"já estariam"* on his desk for signature, a conditional claim from unnamed sources. The company's note the same day said *"não há nenhuma discussão sobre eventual processo de recuperação judicial"* and that it is in *"avançada negociação amigável com bancos credores, objetivando alongar prazos de pagamento e reduzir juros"*; it also claimed record Father's Day results and projected 10% growth in 2026 (company claims, not audited). The company has not disclosed total debt. A restructuring executive, André Dela Togna, joins on 1 October 2026 to support Maia operationally while he *"pretende se dedicar cada vez mais aos clientes e franqueados"* (company note, not a Caito quote). Asked by NeoFeed that afternoon, Caito sent an audio message: *"O CEO sou eu, que sigo na luta, com várias coisas para lançar. ## Core ideas
- **Be the brand manager: not the maker, not the retailer.** His identity claim: *"Eu encontrei o meu lugar na cadeia. Eu não sou fabricante, eu não sou varejista. Eu sou gestor de marca, ponto."* He outsources manufacturing (100% China) and stores (franchisees) and keeps only the brand/IP. Proof he cites: *"Eu vendi 100% das minhas lojas. Hoje, nas mãos dos meus franqueados, elas faturam 30% mais."* (Money Times; Seu Dinheiro)
- **Story kills price; I don't sell discount.** *"Eu não vim à Terra para vender desconto, eu vim à Terra para contar história."* A product carrying a story exits price competition: *"todo o produto que eu lanço tem uma história a ser contada com ele. Por isso, a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço e parcela."* He cites a ~2% annual discount rate as proof the narrative does the pricing work. The clearest concrete case: a five-year Harry Potter eyewear line personally design-approved by J.K. Rowling, *"A gente aprendeu a fazer o produto, não é fácil, mas é aquilo que eu falo de não vender apenas um óculos e sim contar uma história"*, expanded via a 2023 Warner Bros. theme-park partnership. (Consumidor Moderno; Money Times; Forbes Brasil 2022)
- **I sell a brand, not glasses: and marca = margem.** *"Eu não vendo óculos. Eu vendo uma marca."* Stated even more structurally on a CNN Brasil podcast: *"Quando você tem marca, você tem margem, você controla o seu negócio. E isso faz toda a diferença. Eu senti a necessidade de criar uma marca para ter futuro."* And the brand is more than a product: *"Uma marca deve ser comunidade, e não apenas um produto. É isso que fazemos, é isso que desenvolvemos há 30 anos."* The public-facing test he applies now: *"Hoje, ou você vende commodities, ou você tem uma marca com algo a dizer."* The "não vendo óculos" line is attributed to Inteligência Financeira by an earlier pass with no URL (not re-found here); a verified variant from December 2020 says the same: *"A gente não vende óculos. A gente vende Chilli Beans; a gente vende histórias"* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, dez/2020). (Inteligência Financeira, unverified; Consumidor Moderno; CNN Brasil "No Lucro" #5)
- **A brand must show its "soul": take a social stance, don't stay silent.** *"Quando você tem um gestor por trás que se posiciona, e esse posicionamento fala com o consumidor e ele se identifica, ele vai lá, consome o seu produto e vira o seu cliente. Hoje, o público quer saber quem está por trás da marca e o que ela pensa. É assim que você mostra a alma da marca, como ela pensa, como ela cuida da sociedade e do planeta."* (CNN Brasil "No Lucro" #5)
- **Aesthetics is strategy and leadership, not decoration.** In his own authored column (not ghostwritten copy, first-person, byline-confirmed): *"Seguinte: antes de abrir a boca, sua marca já falou tudo. O cliente bate o olho e decide em segundos se quer ficar ou se vai embora."* [Here's the thing: before you even open your mouth, your brand has already spoken. The customer decides in seconds whether to stay or leave.] And: *"Não tem meio-termo: ou conquista ou espanta. E isso acontece em milissegundos... Por isso, estética não é detalhe, é ferramenta."* [There's no middle ground: it either wins you over or scares you off... That's why aesthetics isn't a detail, it's a tool.] He names NBA, Camaro, Havaianas, and Volkswagen as brands whose visual identity *is* their strategy, and traces his own method to referencing "arte, música, cinema e moda" rather than retail benchmarks. Closing thesis: *"Mais do que nunca, estética é uma forma de liderança."* [More than ever, aesthetics is a form of leadership.] (Metrópoles, "A estética fala," set/2025)
- **Money/"the first million" is the wrong central goal.** In his earliest public essay he tells young founders to drop the fixation on a first-million target: *"esquece de uma vez por todas essa história do primeiro milhão, esse não deve ser o seu foco... ter somente o primeiro milhão, o dinheiro, como meta central do seu negócio é uma perda de foco."* The substitute motor is something that "touches the heart, involves people, tells stories": *"você precisa, assim como eu, de algo que toque o coração, envolva as pessoas, conte histórias."* He repeated the point a year later on stage, to an audience of franchisors, aimed at the startup culture of 2019: *"Hoje se fala muito em startups e pessoas que rapidamente se tornam milionárias. A pressa pelo milhão é curiosa. No fundo, o importante é a paixão pelo que se faz"* [Today there is a lot of talk about startups and people who quickly become millionaires. The rush for the million is curious. At bottom, what matters is passion for what you do]. (LinkedIn Pulse, ~2018; Mercado&Consumo, 2/out/2019)
- **Irreverence rides on top of a rock-solid base.** The license to be bold is *earned* by operational competence: *"Da mesma maneira que temos irreverência e empenho, temos um arroz com feijão muito bem-feito"* [just as we have irreverence and drive, we have rice-and-beans done very well], the basics done right are *"o que nos dá liberdade para fazer umas gracinhas"* [what gives us the freedom to mess around]. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **The brand must be a living, "mutant" organism: reject "stage entrepreneurship."** His book's thesis, per its own publisher synopsis (not his direct speech, editorial third-person copy, confirmed identically worded across the Amazon eBook page and the Editora Gente product page): *"Atualmente, vivemos em um mundo de empreendedores de palco, que romantizam seus negócios e os resultados, mas no dia a dia estão desconectados do seu time"* [we currently live in a world of "stage entrepreneurs" who romanticize their businesses and results but are disconnected from their teams day-to-day]. Against that current, the synopsis says the book delivers *"insights valiosos sobre a criação de uma marca mutante, que não para nunca de crescer e de conquistar fãs"* [valuable insights on building a mutant brand that never stops growing and winning fans]. (Amazon/Editora Gente synopsis)
- **The cure for stage entrepreneurship: "ir para o jogo" (publisher copy and his own site).** The prescribed move is to stop absorbing theory and *"ir para o jogo — acertando, errando e fazendo as coisas acontecerem na vida real"* [get into the game, getting it right, getting it wrong, and making things happen in real life]. His own site restates the same critique as one of the book's three pillars ("Ação"): *"Parar de se confundir com tantas teorias absorvidas do empreendedorismo de palco e ir para o jogo."* (Amazon/Editora Gente synopsis; caitomaia.com)
- **Self-applied label: "empreendedor de palco".** In a later podcast he applies the label to himself directly: *"Eu não sou empreendedor de palco. O livro mostra a vida real."* [I am not a stage entrepreneur. The book shows real life.] The Flow #457 captions (Sept 2021, machine transcription, paraphrase only) place an earlier use of the same self-label before the 2024 book. (Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025; Flow #457)
- **The brand sits on a fast-fashion engine, deliberately non-seasonal.** Chilli Beans treats eyewear like apparel: constant newness, ~10 new models/week, collections tied to film/music/sport drops, refreshed roughly every 3 months rather than by season, *"Eu não acredito em sasonalidade em um país tropical como o Brasil"* [I don't believe in seasonality in a tropical country like Brazil]. Velocity of novelty, not the product itself, is the moat. (Brand reporting; ABF; Forbes Brasil 2016)
- **Pop culture is the licensing flywheel.** Collaborations (Star Wars/Disney, Marvel, NBA, Harry Potter/Warner Bros., Coca-Cola, The Beatles, Bob Marley, Lenny Kravitz, Anitta, Alok, Ivete Sangalo) borrow other people's stories so each drop arrives pre-loaded with meaning and a fan base. His music years are the source code: he markets eyewear like a label markets releases, and he names **David Bowie / Ziggy Stardust** as the model, a musician who "saw an opportunity" in a persona and "turned that image into one of his brand's main logos," going from singer to "one of the most licensed brands in the world": *"Se é para falar de empreendedor, ele é mestre. De cantor a uma das marcas mais licenciadas no mundo."* (ABF; LinkedIn Pulse)
- **Franchising is the growth machine: but only if the franchisee is "in the game."** *"A gente só tem 1.400 lojas porque se posiciona como franqueador... Não temos lojas próprias. Nós nos especializamos em ser franqueador."* The recurring failure mode is an absentee owner: *"O franqueado precisa estar no jogo."* (ABF; Jornal do Comércio)
- **Balance margin with the franchisee on purpose.** He claims he leaves money on the table to keep the chain healthy: *"Muitas vezes a gente poderia ter aumentado a nossa margem e o dinheiro do franqueado, mas a gente não fez para equilibrar e ter uma franquia legal, saudável, onde a rede inteira está saudável e ganhando dinheiro."* (This line comes from Inteligência Financeira via an earlier pass, with no URL in sources and not re-found by search in this pass; lower confidence.) A 2026 LinkedIn post, on winning the ABF Selo de Excelência and a "5 Estrelas" ranking, restates the same commitment with a fresh citation: *"Na Chilli Beans, a gente acredita que uma rede só cresce de forma sustentável quando o franqueado cresce junto."* (Inteligência Financeira; LinkedIn, 2026)
- **Discipline is the real engine, and it's harder than scaling.** From the weight-loss years. His sharpest line inverts founder bravado: *"A gente tem 854 lojas. Abrir 854 lojas é fácil, agora educar um filho é difícil."* (Museu da Pessoa)
- **Read the next generation or die: and Gen Z lacks the patience and courage his generation had.** *"Se a gente não entender as novas gerações, a gente vai morrer. Simples como isso"* and, on stage at the ABF Franchising Summit: *"Todos nós temos que entender a geração Z. Você tem que entender o mercado, se não, você morre."* Elaborated into an explicit generational critique naming his own idol as the counter-model: *"Vejo uma geração muito startup: quer ser milionária, quer escalar rápido. Mas falta paciência, falta identidade com a empresa."* / *"Cazuza era atitude pura. Hoje as pessoas têm medo de dizer o que pensam, com medo de cancelamento. Mas é isso que faz você sair da curva. É isso que falta: coragem."* (Exame, NEEX 2025; ABF; Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **When the physical channel dies, digital is oxygen, not a backup plan.** His central crisis-leadership idea, forged in the pandemic: *"Na hora em que todas as lojas são fechadas, o único oxigênio que você tem é um site."* See Mental models for the full worked example. (Mercado&Consumo, ago/2021)
- **Practice beats theory, in his own words.** In the December 2020 live reported by ISTOÉ Dinheiro: *"Não sou um cara de teorias, sou de prática"* [I'm not a theory guy, I'm a practice guy]. In the 2024 book-launch coverage he says entrepreneurship should happen *"entre erros e acertos, fazendo as coisas para valer, na vida real"* [between mistakes and successes, doing things for real, in real life] and that the answer to founders' doubts *"virá menos de teorias complexas e abstratas e muito mais da prática"* [will come less from complex, abstract theory and much more from practice]. This is first-person confirmation of the "ir para o jogo" thesis that the earlier version of this dossier had only from publisher copy. (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, dez/2020; Consumidor Moderno, 2024)
- **Respect and participation over hierarchy: "odeio ser chamado de CEO".** In a 2018 leadership Q&A: *"Eu, por exemplo, odeio ser chamado de CEO. Eu não sou um corpo sozinho no topo da pirâmide. Eu trabalho junto com todas as pessoas ali. Então, até a nomenclatura eu questionaria."* [I, for instance, hate being called CEO. I'm not a lone body at the top of the pyramid. I work together with everyone there. So I would even question the title.] And: *"O CEO do futuro é colaborativo e ele é inteligente ao ponto de ter a sensibilidade de usar essas pessoas boas ao redor dele para fazer o negócio crescer."* [Tension with the court record and with his later role as sole CEO: see Blind spots.] (Consumidor Moderno, jan/2018)
- **Against feudal lords: you only win by stimulating the will of your team.** Same 2018 interview: *"O que me entristece, na verdade, é ver pessoas dessa geração agindo como senhores feudais. Agindo como o chefe da matilha. Isso é algo que não pode existir mais. Só é possível ganhar o jogo estimulando a vontade dentro da sua equipe."* [What saddens me is seeing people of this generation acting like feudal lords, like the pack leader. That can't exist anymore. You can only win the game by stimulating the will inside your team.] He adds that younger workers want money but also respect: *"Se você não respeitá-los, eles viram as costas e vão embora."* (Consumidor Moderno, jan/2018)
- **Inclusion as a stated founding rule, with a brand payoff.** In a July 2019 Q&A: *"Em primeiro lugar, respeito. Na Chilli Beans, não nos importamos com a sexualidade, religião, raça, corpo, gênero, ou qualquer característica que possa ser considerada preconceituosa."* [First of all, respect. At Chilli Beans we don't care about sexuality, religion, race, body, gender or any characteristic that could be considered prejudiced.] He says the openness since 1997 shows in the audience, offering as evidence *"mais de 300 fotos de pessoas que tatuam o logo da marca"* [more than 300 photos of people who tattoo the brand's logo]. The book's retailer subtitle carries the same theme: "COMO PAIXÃO, PESSOAS E DIVERSIDADE CONSTRUÍRAM A CHILLI BEANS". (Mercado&Consumo, jul/2019; Martins Fontes listing)
- **The brand is the asset that survives the closed store.** In the December 2020 live, as reproduced by ISTOÉ Dinheiro: *"O que salvou minha vida na pandemia foi o investimento que fiz há 20 anos na marca Chilli Beans"* [What saved my life in the pandemic was the investment I made 20 years ago in the Chilli Beans brand], alongside *"A gente não vende óculos. A gente vende Chilli Beans; a gente vende histórias"* [We don't sell glasses. We sell Chilli Beans; we sell stories]. It reframes brand spending as crisis insurance rather than marketing cost. (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, dez/2020)
- **Eyewear sells a mood, not an object (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 (Sept 2021) he describes, as captioned by machine transcription (no quotation marks used), how people enter a store head down and leave head up with raised self-esteem because of a pair of sunglasses, and says he treats watches as a fashion accessory rather than a time-telling device because people have phones. Treat as paraphrase; not audio-verified. (Flow #457)

## Mental models
Reusable lenses, each with a worked example.

- **"Gestor de marca, ponto." (Brand-manager-only positioning.)** Pick the single highest-leverage link in your value chain and shed the rest. Worked example: he sold all ~130 owned stores and pushed manufacturing to China, keeping brand + design + IP only, and the franchised stores then out-earned his owned ones by 30%. The test: which link, if you let go of it, would let others run it *better* than you? *"Eu vendi 100% das minhas lojas. Hoje... elas faturam 30% mais."* (Money Times; Seu Dinheiro)
- **"Foco de loja": don't mix channels, license instead.** A named companion rule to "gestor de marca": when entering an adjacent category, keep it out of the core retail footprint so the store doesn't lose its identity. Worked example: launching Chilli Beans-branded sneakers (2025) purely as a **licensing deal with Ramarim** (RS), sold only through the footwear channel, not through Chilli Beans stores, *"Está aí o segredo: se você começar a misturar muito, você perde o foco da loja... [Será segmentado] no canal de calçados."* Same logic applied to the ChilliDrinks beverage line and a pepper-sauce partnership with Knorr (2024): licensed-out categories, not shelf space inside the optical stores. (O Tempo, set/2025)
- **Story-first product design.** Don't design a product and market it; design the *story* and let the product carry it, *"A gente conta histórias em tudo o que a gente vende. A gente entende que contar a história do produto faz toda a diferença."* Worked example: the Harry Potter line (five years running, every design J.K. Rowling-approved) and an opportunistic tie-in with the telenovela *Vale Tudo*, while promoting an Ivete Sangalo collection, his team "bateu na porta da TV Globo" [knocked on Globo's door] and got product written into a plot with 50 million viewers per episode: *"Tem coisas que Deus coloca tudo na hora certa: Ivete Sangalo, uma novela, Chilli Beans, moda, óculos... Tem tudo a ver!"* (ABF; Money Times; Forbes Brasil 2022; O Tempo, set/2025)
- **Rejuvenate an old, sleepy category.** The opportunity isn't always a new product, it's a tired one re-dressed. Worked example: *"Há 25 anos eu comecei a vender óculos escuros, só que pus uma roupagem diferente, um mix de produtos diferentes, vendedores diferentes, marca diferente, de um produto que já se vendia há cem anos. Ainda existem oportunidades em setores antigos e velhos que você pode rejuvenescer."* (Agência Sebrae)
- **Licensing as borrowed equity.** Rent the cultural capital of an existing IP so a drop launches with an audience already attached. Worked example: the steady cadence of Disney/Marvel/NBA/Beatles/Anitta drops, each one borrowing a fan base, is how a sunglasses SKU arrives "pre-sold." Worked example, from his own reasoning about musicians-as-brands: David Bowie's Ziggy Stardust persona became a licensable logo; Caito treats collaborators the same way, as pre-built fan bases to rent. (ABF; LinkedIn Pulse)
- **"Oxigenação da rede." (Network refresh as maintenance, not crisis.)** Treat the franchise base like a living system that needs continuous oxygen: replace disengaged operators, remodel tired stores, close the distance between owner and floor. Worked example: *"Percebo que o dono está muito longe da realidade... Quando você corta essa distância, as coisas começam a acontecer."* A related, distinct operational lever from the same 2025 push: a network-wide switch to a **5×2 work schedule**, credited with concrete HR outcomes, *"Os franqueados receberam muito bem a nova escala, a retenção aumentou e os currículos que chegam têm uma qualidade muito melhor."* (Jornal do Comércio; ABF Pinga Fogo, out/2025), note: at the same ABF panel, Tom Moreira Leite (China in Box/Grupo Trigo) used the identical verb "oxigenar a rede" for his own company, suggesting the term may be shared franchising-industry jargon rather than something Caito personally coined.
- **"O único oxigênio que você tem é um site." (Crisis reframing: digital isn't Plan B, it's survival.)** When the physical channel is severed, treat the remaining digital channel as the organism's entire oxygen supply and pour resources into it immediately. Worked example, the fullest crisis narrative in the corpus: on March 17, 2020, 917 stores closed "abruptly"; the website, 5% of sales before the pandemic, was pushed to 18% of sales as physical stores stayed dark, and total e-commerce grew 134% while physical-store sales still grew 10% over 2019 once reopened. (Mercado&Consumo, ago/2021)
- **The oxygen mechanism: virtual try-on and franchisee Instagram as storefronts (2020).** The mechanism wasn't just ad spend: a 3D virtual try-on tool was built from scratch because *"as pessoas estavam em casa e tinham medo de comprar óculos escuros"* [people were home and afraid to buy sunglasses (without trying them on)], click a pair and it renders live on your face, in proportion, matched to skin/hair tone. Franchisees' 798 Instagram pages (already running for years) were converted into a hyperlocal e-commerce network with 2-hour delivery, *"Isso era um projeto que a gente já estava exercitando, mas que foi antecipado de dois anos para dois meses e a coisa funcionou."* [That was a project we were already working on, but it got moved up from two years to two months, and it worked.] (Mercado&Consumo, ago/2021)
- **"Visão de Empreendedor": a named 3-step method, distilled into his first paid course.** After 30 years of "building a brand, making mistakes, course-correcting, deciding amid chaos" ("Eu nunca ensinei isso antes"), he names the method explicitly: *"#01 Entender o que realmente está sustentando o caixa... #02 Crescer o time de forma orgânica e otimizar processos antes de escalar... #03 Planejar com identidade. Ninguém quer a cópia da cópia e o consumidor percebe."* [#01 Understand what's actually sustaining cash flow. #02 Grow the team organically and optimize processes before scaling. #03 Plan with identity, nobody wants a copy of a copy, and the customer notices.] The pattern echoes his own repeated advice against premature scaling and against losing brand identity while expanding. (LinkedIn, 2026)
- **The franchisee due-diligence test (two questions).** Advice to anyone buying *any* franchise: (1) *"pergunte para o franqueador se ele consegue te colocar em contato com o franqueado dele. Se ele não conseguir oferecer cinco números de telefone, já indica que tem algo de errado."* (2) *"converse com o franqueado e entenda a realidade dele. Pergunte como está o estoque... essa é uma pergunta importantíssima."* A rare buyer's-side checklist. Chilli Beans's own internal counterpart, cited by the same event's coverage but as paraphrase, not his direct words, is an AI tool that "montra o pedido para o franqueado" [assembles the franchisee's restock order] to smooth inventory swings; the Forbes US profile independently corroborates an inventory-AI initiative with a concrete claimed result: "a 20% sales increase and a reduction in inventory levels from three months to just two weeks." (Money Times; Forbes US, set/2024)
- **Circle of control vs. circle of worry.** Don't spend energy on what you can't touch. Worked example: *"Coisas que eu não posso pôr a mão, governo, tarifaço, eu não ponho energia. Mas as coisas que eu posso pôr a mão, que estão ao meu alcance, eu não abro mão."* (Exame, NEEX 2025)
- **Hire for the "brilho no olho."** Worked example, applied to both employees and Shark Tank founders: *"Eu não vejo o que a pessoa tem de diferente, como raça ou religião, eu vejo o brilho no olho. O olho é a parte que me pega, porque ele transmite o propósito de cada pessoa."* His own LinkedIn "Experience" entry for the Shark Tank role restates the same filter, now explicitly counter-posed to spreadsheet valuation: *"Investir, incentivar, acreditar em pessoas e no amor que elas têm por seus negócios é mais rentável do que a avaliação exclusivamente numérica. E o brilho nos olhos às vezes fala mais alto que o balanço financeiro."* (Exame, "brilho no olho"; LinkedIn Experience section)
- **Smart money is experience, not cash.** Worked example, from six years investing on Shark Tank, with a concrete season result attached: *"Muita gente fala que está atrás de smart money, mas muitas vezes nem sabe o que é isso. No smart money você não pega dinheiro, você pega a experiência daquela pessoa, que vale mais do que o dinheiro. Nesta temporada dois caras entraram pedindo R$ 1 e saíram com quase R$ 1 milhão."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jul/2024)
- **Get into the game instead of theorizing ("empreendedorismo de palco" vs. real execution).** Named critique of a school of founder behavior: performing entrepreneurship on stage while disconnected from the team, versus his prescribed cure of *"ir para o jogo — acertando, errando e fazendo as coisas acontecerem na vida real."* Worked example: he frames his own 1997 suitcase-and-cart launch and the ~30-year run since as proof of learning by doing rather than by theory. A LinkedIn post makes the same case with a fresher, more tactical framing: *"O mercado não está nem aí para o quanto você trabalhou no seu projeto. Ele só responde a uma coisa: valor... Você não descobre se uma ideia é boa dentro da empresa. Você descobre quando coloca ela na frente de clientes reais. Foi assim que construí a Chilli Beans."* (Amazon/Editora Gente synopsis; LinkedIn, 2026)
- **"Arroz, feijão, amor e pimenta" (his named investment rubric).** What he screens for when a founder pitches: *"Eu busco arroz, feijão, amor e pimenta. Arroz e feijão são as contas, margem, aceitação, vendas, recorrência – o básico do negócio, a “cozinha”. Amor é o brilho nos olhos do empreendedor e a pimenta é o seu diferencial, o que seu produto ou serviço traz de diferente."* [I look for rice, beans, love and pepper. Rice and beans are the accounts, margin, acceptance, sales, recurrence: the basics, the "kitchen". Love is the founder's sparkle in the eye and pepper is the differentiator.] Worked example, per the outlet (paraphrase, not his words): Borracharia do Leandro, a couple who "gourmetizou" the tire-shop visit (waiting room with coffee, décor made of tires); he invested R$250 mil after seeing "brilho nos olhos". It is the most complete named investment framework in the corpus. (Startupi, fev/2022)
- **Sell-out over sell-in (the franchisee's order is built from the store's own sales).** [Flow #457, Sept 2021; auto-caption paraphrase, no quotation.] He separates sell-in (company to franchisee) from sell-out (franchisee to the final consumer) and says decisions are made around the second. Worked example as captioned: the franchisee must take only about 20% of the mix (the collection showcase) and picks the remaining ~80% with an AI tool that assembles the weekly order from that store's sales and location (more sport models for a Rio store, higher ticket for elite malls); he claims sales rose about 30% afterwards, a self-reported figure. The Forbes US profile separately reports an inventory-AI initiative with a claimed 20% sales increase; the two numbers are not reconciled. (Flow #457; Forbes US, set/2024)
- **Short stock as a design constraint, and as a competitive number.** In October 2019: *"Trabalhamos com estoque curto. A gente aprendeu a girar esse produto. A ideia é que a cada duas semanas a gente tenha uma loja nova"* [We work with short stock. We learned to turn this product. The idea is that every two weeks we have a new store]. Per the Flow captions (paraphrase, approximate figures), franchise apparel stock in Brazil runs around 5 months and optical around 6, against about 2.5 weeks at Chilli Beans, while a collection takes around 7 months to produce: the 2020 Olympics collection was shelved by the pandemic and released only in 2021, so tight stock still cost him once. (Mercado&Consumo, out/2019; Flow #457)
- **Plan 18 months ahead, but design for change (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 he says everything to be launched through early 2023 was already defined, with a roughly ten-designer team, weekly drops of about 10 sunglasses, 5 watches and 5 prescription frames and one monthly theme (Marvel in store in Sept 2021, then Harry Potter and an eco line made from sea-floor waste collected by a Chinese company). The outlet's 2019 count differs (10 sunglasses, 5 watches, 7 prescription frames per week), so cadence figures are approximate. (Flow #457; Mercado&Consumo, out/2019)
- **Convention as product (turn a cost center into a loyalty and media asset).** *"Quando você é franqueador, você precisa encantar o seu franqueado. Há redes que punem franqueados que não vão a uma convenção. Nós resolvemos proporcionar algo novo: contratamos o diretor artístico do Cirque du Soleil para fazer uma convenção e acabou virando um show"* [When you are a franchisor you must delight your franchisee. Some networks punish franchisees who skip a convention. We decided to offer something new: we hired Cirque du Soleil's artistic director to do a convention and it became a show]. Per the outlet (paraphrase), it grew into the "Chilli Mob Cruise": three days of music, fashion and art, about 2,000 company people plus 3,000 customers and sponsors. The 2015 cruise convention already in this dossier was a downturn tactic; this is the scaled, productized version. (Mercado&Consumo, out/2019)
- **Store tours as diagnostic, not PR.** At Conarec 2015, on visiting around 200 stores a year (the outlet's number): *"Tem de conversar e ouvir muito"* [you have to talk and listen a lot]. The 2019 coverage paraphrases the same habit as narrowing the distance to franchisees and seeing errors in person. The 2024 version, given as advice to a founder expanding a network: *"Não importa quantas lojas você tem, esteja presente no negócio e disposta a escutar"* [No matter how many stores you have, be present in the business and willing to listen]. (Consumidor Moderno, 2015; Mercado&Consumo, out/2019; Agência Sebrae, jun/2024)
- **"Colocar pimenta": offer more than the obvious, and test whether the stunt paid.** At Conarec 2015: *"Você tem de oferecer muito mais para o consumidor, porque é óbvio para uma loja de óculos vender óculos"* [You have to offer the consumer much more, because it's obvious for an eyewear store to sell eyewear]. Worked example: bringing Iggy Pop to São Paulo Fashion Week to break a giant Chilli Beans pair, *"Tivemos 20% mais retorno com essa ação do que com uma convencional"* [we got 20% more return from this action than from a conventional one]. Also *"Fazer por fazer não adianta nada"* [doing it just to do it is worthless]. (The link to the title of his first book is this dossier's reading; the article does not make it.) (Consumidor Moderno, 2015)
- **Talented or disciplined (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 he describes, in a God-handing-out-two-signs image, that one arrives on Earth either talented or disciplined, says he is the disciplined type rather than especially creative, and that a disciplined person can do whatever he wants while a talented one without discipline does nothing; his job, as captioned, is to surround himself with talented people and teach them discipline. It complements the "discipline is the real engine" idea above. (Flow #457)
- **Hire the competence you lack before you scale (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 he recounts, about 18 years earlier, having an Oscar Freire opportunity and about 1,500 people wanting a franchise, and being tempted to sell franchises fast; he says he hired two experienced operators in their sixties who told him he would be bankrupt in six months without structure. He uses it to coach the hosts on structuring their own company. Self-reported; the one hiring test quoted in the captions is voiced by a host, not by him. (Flow #457)
- **"Plano 3R" and the reality show as method (journalist paraphrase).** ISTOÉ Dinheiro's summary of the 2020 live describes a "Plano 3R" (revelar pessoas, reciclar a marca, recriar histórias) and three methodological pillars (people, stories, brand centrality), all in the journalist's words, not in quotation marks from Caito. The outlet presents his reality show "Sócio por um Dia" (four episodes, going into at-risk businesses) as a vehicle for this plan and also notes it is "também, uma propaganda" for visibility. Treat the 3R label as the outlet's framing. (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, dez/2020)

## Decision heuristics
- **Own the brand, rent everything else.** Default to outsourcing unless it's the brand/IP itself. (Money Times)
- **If it has no story, don't launch it**, because then you'd compete on price, which he refuses. (Consumidor Moderno; Money Times)
- **Earn your irreverence first.** Don't do the *gracinhas* until the *arroz com feijão* is solid. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Pick focus and pay the opportunity cost.** He killed the music dream to go all-in: *"O Brasil perdeu Las Ticas Tienen Fuego, mas ganhou coisa muito melhor."* And: *"Quando você acha o seu lugar na cadeia, o que você ama, tem paixão, ninguém te segura."* (Museu da Pessoa; Agência Sebrae)
- **Chase what "fills your life with pleasure," not a savings target.** His earliest documented public advice to founders: don't run the business as a pure money machine, because every miss against a money target demotivates and drains meaning, *"Só não esquece: acha aquilo que vai encher a sua vida de prazer e o dinheiro com certeza vem. Tira essa pressão do primeiro milhão e… curte!"* (LinkedIn Pulse)
- **Cut the disengaged franchisee.** Absentee or distracted operators are churn targets, not relationships to preserve. (Jornal do Comércio)
- **Expand into adjacency only when demand is already revealed.** He entered prescription eyewear (Ótica Chilli Beans, 2019) only after *grau* was already ~19% of sales. (Exame, óticas)
- **Don't waste energy on the uncontrollable** (government, tariffs); over-invest in what's in reach. (Exame, NEEX 2025)
- **When founders pitch, check the valuation reality before the love.** His top diagnosis of failed pitches: overvaluation, stated with a specific mechanism (not just "high valuation" but a pattern across his entire tenure): *"Em seis anos que eu fiquei como participante fixo tiveram inúmeras oportunidades que eu queria entrar em negócios, mas não entrei porque o valuation era muito alto. Muitas pessoas poderiam ter saído de lá com um investidor se tivessem sido mais conscientes na hora de avaliar o negócio."* And a bluntly stated anti-signal for founders chasing him as an investor: *"Quem chega lá e mostra quer ficar milionário não me brilha os olhos."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jul/2024; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, set/2025)
- **When entering a new geography, size the channel before scaling it.** On international expansion: *"Estamos testando canais de tamanhos diferentes para entender o que funciona melhor."* Don't standardize a format across markets until you've tested which size actually works locally. (Exame, mar/2026)
- **Protect the seeds you already planted before chasing new ones.** On mature international markets (Indonésia, Oriente Médio, Peru): *"A gente tem muita semente que cresceu e que a gente tem que cuidar."* Consolidate and tend existing bets rather than only hunting new ones. (Exame, mar/2026)
- **In an FX shock, change the settlement currency, not just the price.** When the US-China trade war opened a window, he paid a Chinese supplier in Chinese currency for the first time and declared it permanent policy, not opportunism: *"De forma alguma é uma estratégia pontual. Queremos continuar mantendo esta prática. A ideia é que o uso do dólar não volte nunca mais."* [This is by no means a one-off strategy. We want to keep doing this. The idea is that the use of the dollar never comes back.] (Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **In an FX/cost shock, absorb it through volume, not price pass-through: and go renegotiate in person.** During the 2015 real/dollar crisis (70% of the operation in dollars), he cut prices 5 to 8% instead of raising them, betting on turnover, *"Não repassamos os custos [da alta do dólar] para o consumidor e ganhamos no giro"*, spent 15 days personally in China renegotiating with suppliers (cut input costs ~20%), and diversified sourcing to cut China dependency from 90% to 70% within a year. He also cut headcount 10% in the same year and turned the annual franchisee convention into a 4-day cruise (~4,000 attendees) to keep the network engaged through the downturn. (Forbes Brasil, 2016, via web.archive.org)
- **Reframe "crisis" as "opportunity": the word choice is a decision, not just rhetoric.** *"É muito fácil colocar a culpa na crise... Prefiro falar em oportunidade."* (Forbes Brasil, 2016)
- **Be "optimistic but grounded": never let optimism become denial.** Self-description used consistently across interviews years apart: *"Eu sou otimista por natureza. Tem gente até que se irrita comigo. Mas eu estou entre otimista e conservador. Eu estou confiante, mas com o pé no chão."* And, in the same spirit, after the pandemic: *"Não existe momento melhor do mundo para começar um negócio."* (Mercado&Consumo, ago/2021)
- **Lead a crisis with a fixed cadence of honest bad news, not silence.** During the pandemic he called every franchisee on a fixed schedule regardless of what he had to report: *"ter falado com meus franqueados toda semana, toda quinta-feira às 10h, foi importante. 'Desculpe, hoje eu não tenho notícia boa para te trazer.' Ou: 'Hoje eu tenho notícia boa para te trazer.' Olhar no olho e chorar, curtir, comemorar, é isso que faz a diferença."* Backed by concrete relief actions, not just calls: renegotiated franchisee rent, deployed Chilli Beans' legal department to help franchisees access salary-support benefits for their own staff, extended payment deadlines on inventory owed, and arranged a cheap-credit line via Caixa Econômica Federal. (Mercado&Consumo, ago/2021)
- **View your own commitments through cycles: an exit doesn't need a crisis reason.** On leaving Shark Tank as a fixed judge after six seasons, then returning: *"Como a vida é feita de ciclos, decidi encerrar o meu no ano passado."* [Since life happens in cycles, I decided to end mine last year.] A personal decision framework applied to a public role, not just to the business. (ADNews, 2024)
- **Patience is the actual moat; ten years is a normal timeline, not a failure.** *"Tenha paciência. Demorei 10 anos para dar certo. Tomei muita porrada. Não desista em dois anos. E mais: existem setores arcaicos esperando por inovação. Dá para vender até ovo com história e atitude."* (Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **"O varejo é feito de detalhes": the basics are the solidity.** At the 2019 franchising forum: *"O varejo é feito de detalhes. É isso que faz as coisas acontecerem. Eu aprendi que fazer o básico, o arroz com feijão, traz a solidez ao negócio"* [Retail is made of details. That's what makes things happen. I learned that doing the basics, the rice and beans, brings solidity to the business]. It ties the dossier's earn-your-irreverence rule to retail operations and to a franchisor audience. (Mercado&Consumo, out/2019)
- **Open a pitch with the product's story, calm and passionate.** To founders courting investors: *"Fique calmo, tranquilo e mostre sua paixão. Não comece falando de um produto, comece pela história do seu produto. Eu busco por um empresário que tenha uma noção e força muito grande da sua marca e que ela tenha uma cara diferente"* [Stay calm, relaxed and show your passion. Don't start by talking about a product, start with the story of your product. I look for an entrepreneur with a great sense and strength of their brand, and a brand with a different face]. (Startupi, fev/2022)
- **Before taking a partner, structure the business, create desire, get traction.** *"É importante que a pessoa tenha consciência se precisa de um sócio ou não. Se sim, primeiro precisa estruturar o negócio, gerar desejo e ter tração nas vendas"* [It's important to be aware whether you need a partner or not. If so, first structure the business, generate desire and have traction in sales]. See Positions (raise vs. bootstrap) for the claim that precedes it. (Startupi, fev/2022)
- **Reread fear as a sign you are in the game (this dossier's reading of his 2024 statement).** In the book-launch coverage he says his aim is to show *"porque nem todo frio na barriga na hora de empreender é motivo de apreensão"* [why not every butterfly in the stomach when starting a business is a reason for apprehension]. The book's title, "Se Parar o Sangue Esfria" [if you stop, the blood goes cold], fits that reading; the reframing itself is the dossier's inference. (Consumidor Moderno, 2024)
- **Treat the customer with respect, simply, without arguing the problem.** At Conarec 2015: *"O cliente tem de ser tratado com respeito de um jeito simples e fácil e não ficar discutindo problema"* [The customer must be treated with respect in a simple, easy way, without arguing about the problem]; he adds that he has no wheel to reinvent. (Consumidor Moderno, 2015)
- **A business is a plant: it needs time, and heavy outside money can distract from the basics (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 he argues a business needs watering, pruning and a maturation time, says his own took about ten years to make its first real money (after losses from wrong stock and purchases), and warns about founders who start with large venture funding and then spend their energy staying alive; he says he is criticized for this view. Two years, as captioned, is a sprint. The 2025 Exame line in this dossier ("Demorei 10 anos para dar certo") repeats the ten-year point. (Flow #457)
- **"Achar o seu pão francês" (auto-caption paraphrase).** To a listener asking how to build recurring revenue: find the business that is already working, put your energy there, and be patient. His own move into eyewear is the only example given. This label is new relative to the earlier dossier and rests on machine captions only. (Flow #457)
- **Do not stay on the inherited rice and beans: innovate on top of what the family built.** Advice at the Sebrae stand to a 30-year-old taking over a family sewing workshop: *"O que seus pais construíram é muito importante, mas não fique no feijão com arroz"* [What your parents built is very important, but don't stay in the beans and rice] (her reply, per the outlet: "Vou fazer uma feijoada"). It uses the same food idiom as his "arroz com feijão bem-feito" rule in the opposite direction; both uses are recorded. (Agência Sebrae, jun/2024)
- **In a crisis, ask whether the shake-up opens more doors than it closes (auto-caption paraphrase).** Closing Flow #457 he likens the pandemic to a war or revolution that shuts some doors and opens millions of opportunities (repressed demand, new habits). He says in the same conversation that he bought a collector's Elvis guitar cheaply at a New Orleans auction during the 2008 US crisis, the same instinct as the 2020 mall-vacancy buying already in this dossier. Paraphrase; not audio-verified. (Flow #457)
- **Build the base before you grow, in crisis mode (2026).** In the video reported on 3 September 2026: *"Trouxemos um time para solidificar a estrutura, para deixar o financeiro sólido, porque a gente precisa cada vez mais ter uma base sólida para continuar crescendo"* [We brought in a team to solidify the structure, to make the finances solid, because we increasingly need a solid base to keep growing]. It inverts the "mutant brand that never stops growing" register: structure becomes the precondition. He also pointed to the brand's 30th anniversary in 2027 (outlet paraphrase). (Mercado&Consumo, 3/set/2026)

## Positions by decision type
- **Make vs. buy / vertical integration.** Decisively *un-integrate*: sold all owned stores, manufactures 100% in China. The Brazilian factory that once made ~20% of volume closed under fines and taxes, *"foi um volume enorme, quase 500 mil peças. A gente levou tanta multa, tanta multa trabalhista, tanto imposto, que o dono da fábrica desistiu... o imposto destrói as empresas."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro / Click P&G; Money Times)
- **Make vs. buy: offered free factories, the math still fails.** A later, richer interview on the same theme adds detail: he has been offered ~3 free Brazilian factories and turned them down because the unit economics don't clear even at zero acquisition cost, *"Já me ofereceram de presente umas três fábricas, como se eu fosse arrendar. A conta não fecha. O produto se torna mais caro do que o chinês, fabricando no Brasil."*, while explicitly praising the Chinese state's manufacturer-support model (a state-backed factory insurer, so buyers pay the insurer rather than the supplier directly) as what he'd want to see in Brazil: *"Se quisesse, dava. Se desse incentivo, isenção de imposto, a gente ia voar."* His five Chinese factory partners are in Wenzhou (Zhejiang province, ~10M population, 700+ factories, a city he still calls "small" by Chinese standards). (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jun/2025)
- **Make vs. buy: the state-intervention asymmetry (dossier observation).** *(Tension worth flagging: he objects to Brazilian state intervention as destructive but praises Chinese state intervention as a model to emulate, the objection tracks whether the intervention costs or subsidizes him, not intervention per se; not reconciled in either source.)* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro / Click P&G; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jun/2025)
- **Pivot vs. persevere.** He pivoted hard *once*, music → eyewear, then persevered ~30 years inside that choice. Inside the business he doesn't "pivot," he *refreshes* (new collections, formats, verticals) and explicitly frames this as choosing the real "jogo" over theorizing about entrepreneurship. (Museu da Pessoa; ABF; Amazon synopsis)
- **How to price.** Refuse to compete on price for sunglasses; let story carry it (~2% annual discount). *Exception:* in the optical vertical he priced frames *below* market (R$149 to 300 vs. a ~R$400 average), a deliberate penetration move against incumbent opticians, distinct from his sunglasses stance. A second, older exception under FX shock (2015): he cut prices 5 to 8% to defend turnover rather than pass currency cost to the consumer. (Money Times; Exame, óticas; Forbes Brasil 2016)
- **Focus vs. diversify.** Focus at the *level of his role* (brand management only) and at the *level of the store* ("foco de loja", don't mix channels); diversify the *product surface* aggressively via licensing rather than in-store SKUs, sunglasses → watches → prescription eyewear → sustainable lines → marketplace (a Mercado Livre storefront that "dobrou o faturamento online") → sneakers (Ramarim license) → beverages (ChilliDrinks) → pepper sauce (Knorr partnership). Diversify what you license out; never diversify your job, and never let a new category bleed into the core store's identity. (ABF; Exame; O Tempo, set/2025)
- **Raise vs. bootstrap / take a partner.** Bootstrapped and franchise-funded for ~25 years; opened to a minority (~30%) sale only in the mid-2020s, earmarked for the optical expansion, *"estamos negociando parte da venda da empresa para apostar no segmento de óticas."* (Exame, óticas)
- **Margin vs. ecosystem health.** Chooses to leave margin on the table to keep the franchise chain healthy rather than maximize his own take, reinforced in a 2026 LinkedIn post tied to a franchising-excellence award. (Inteligência Financeira, unverified earlier-pass attribution; LinkedIn, 2026)
- **When to fire / replace a partner.** Replace the franchisee who's "out of the game", absentee, distracted, or reinvesting profits into rival franchises, via continuous *oxigenação* rather than waiting for a store to fail. *"Tenho um problema que não consigo conter: o cara ganhando dinheiro na franquia da Chilli Beans e gastando em outras franquias ruins."* (Jornal do Comércio)
- **Entering a new category.** Wait for revealed demand inside the existing base, then attack on price + brand familiarity (prescription eyewear); or license it out entirely to a category specialist rather than build in-house capability (footwear via Ramarim, beverages, sauces). (Exame, óticas; O Tempo, set/2025)
- **Entering a new geography.** Size-test the channel/format locally first, then concentrate resources on markets that already show returns (Indonésia, Oriente Médio, Peru) rather than spreading thin across all fronts at once. On the US specifically, growth has a dated numeric target, not just a direction: a Miami store opened in 2025 as part of a stated goal of *"chegar a 60 pontos de venda nos Estados Unidos até 2030"*, with an explicit caveat that the goal isn't store count for its own sake: *"o importante não é quantidade de lojas. E sim levar nossa cultura, criatividade e jeito de fazer negócio para novos mercados."* (Exame, mar/2026; LinkedIn, ~2025)
- **Small/thin markets that don't support a full-size store.** Rather than skip towns under ~50,000 people where a normal store's unit economics don't work, he designed a cheaper, sustainable, solar-powered container format for gas-station placement, an explicit callback to the brand's original 1997 street-cart format: *"Nós temos muita dificuldade de manter uma loja em cidades com até 50 mil habitantes, a conta simplesmente não fecha. Então, pensamos nessa possibilidade para incluir em postos de gasolina os containers que são sustentáveis e contam com custo mais baixo."* *(Unaddressed in the source: potential channel conflict with existing franchisees whose catchment areas a cheaper company-format channel might now also serve.)* (Forbes Brasil, ago/2022)
- **Real-estate/crisis opportunism.** Read pandemic-era mall vacancy as a buying window rather than a threat: opened ~200 new stores during the pandemic while rents were depressed, *"Nós aproveitamos que os shoppings estavam quase dando as lojas durante aquela incerteza... e aumentamos o nosso portfólio."* (Forbes Brasil, ago/2022)
- **Sustainability as a founder-triggered pivot, not a marketing layer.** Traces the decision to a personal moment, not a market study: *"Há três anos, o meu filho de nove anos me perguntou o que a minha empresa estava fazendo para ajudar o planeta e aquele momento me marcou muito. Foi aí que começamos a investir em sustentabilidade."* Result cited: a 30%-of-production recycled-materials target and 4M recycled-waste protective cases. (Forbes Brasil, ago/2022)
- **AI in production and marketing: use it, but disclose it.** Of ~30 annual ad campaigns, 10 are made entirely with AI, with a self-imposed transparency rule: *"When we do use it [for advertising], it is deliberate and openly [synthetic]."* He pairs heavy AI adoption (inventory-replenishment AI for franchisees; warehouse robotics in Extrema, MG, expected to handle ~150,000 items/month) with an explicit belief that AI doesn't replace human craft in campaigns: *"Just like in music, having a person craft a song, play the guitar in a studio, breathe, sweat, and put emotion into lyrics makes all the difference to the final result, and I believe the same goes for campaigns."* (Forbes US, set/2024, quotes are English-original; the interview was conducted in English, so no Portuguese verbatim exists to pair alongside them)
- **Omnichannel / "phygital": both channels, but locally adapted content.** *"I believe that in order to grow, a brand needs to be both in-person and online, but that needs to be well-executed and connected to the changes in retail... The content [brands] provide for each environment will be different, but the two worlds will need to be blended effectively."* Concrete formats: a **"Store 2.0"** in-store live-commerce module (from ~BRL 220,000 investment per store, projected +20% sales) and, since the pandemic, direct store-fulfilled online orders turning every physical location into a hybrid node. (Forbes US, set/2024; Forbes Brasil, ago/2022, English-original for the Forbes US quotes)
- **Investing (Shark Tank).** Wants both *love and the math*: a founder who has done the accounting and shows real passion (the "brilho no olho"); kills deals on inflated valuation across his entire tenure, not just isolated cases. He frames his own return to the show, after six seasons (2017 to 2023), a 2024 guest spot, and now a full-time comeback for the 10th season, as re-committing to a personal "mission," not a paycheck: *"O Shark Tank Brasil é mais do que um programa, é uma missão."* He also credits the show, and his own exposure as a judge, with directly building his personal brand and the company's: *"Foi durante o Shark Tank que nós percebemos que Caito era uma marca"*, with Chilli Beans' market share against Ray-Ban in Brazil cited (by the outlet, not as his direct words) as roughly doubling, from 13% to 26%, over the decade he's been on air. (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jul/2024; Portal Making Of; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, set/2025; Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **Shark Tank alumni program.** A related program he created for alumni, independent of continued investment: **"Efeito Shark"** [Shark Effect], which follows Shark Tank entrepreneurs whether or not he invested, *"Eu aprendo mais do que ensino. Ver uma hamburgueria bombando dentro de Paraisópolis, com aplicativo próprio, é inspirador. Não é só dinheiro que ajuda. É dividir experiência."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jul/2024; Portal Making Of; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, set/2025; Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **HR / labor policy.** The first documented people-ops lever in the corpus, distinct from the brand/franchising decisions elsewhere: rolled out a network-wide **5×2 work schedule**, crediting it with improved franchisee reception, higher retention, and better-quality job applicants, *"Os franqueados receberam muito bem a nova escala, a retenção aumentou e os currículos que chegam têm uma qualidade muito melhor."* Also created a post-pandemic **"Diretoria de Felicidade"** [Happiness Directorate] focused on listening rather than formal programs: *"As pessoas precisam de cuidado muito mais do que a gente imagina. Abrimos canais para ouvir, sem grandes fórmulas. Só ouvir, olhar para o lado e perguntar: 'Tá tudo bem com você?'"* And an explicit, named rejection of superficial perk culture: *"Não adianta ter uma mesa de ping-pong se o time está estressado. O que importa é respeitar as pessoas como elas são... A gente não abre mão de almoçar junto, rir junto."* (ABF Pinga Fogo, out/2025; Exame/Cazuza, mai/2025)
- **Macro / timing.** Reads Brazil's interest-rate environment as a binding retail constraint, in increasingly specific language across interviews, *"2025 foi um ano muito difícil. O país não sobrevive a juros de quase 20%"*; *"Na minha visão, os juros, do jeito que estão, não é que eles estrangulam, eles sufocam e matam o varejo... Esse juro do jeito que está é insuportável"*; and looking to 2026, *"Eu espero um país de juros mais baixo. Simples como isso"*, but, per his circle-of-control rule, refuses to *spend energy* on the macro he can't change. He also separately reads pandemic-era loan overhangs as compounding the squeeze: *"muitos empréstimos que foram tomados durante a pandemia estão sendo pagos agora."* *(Tension worth noting: he both invokes macro as a constraint and disclaims it as a distraction.)* (Exame, óticas / mar/2026; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jun/2025; ISTOÉ Dinheiro, jul/2024)
- **Make vs. buy: a self-reported origin story for the China supply chain (single source).** On Flow #457 (auto-caption paraphrase) he says that when he had 15 to 30 stores a caller identifying as the Chinese government invited him to China with flights and hotel covered, he attended a factory trade fair, returned with about 30 factory contacts of whom seven were recommended, and has worked with those partners for 17 to 18 years, spending millions of dollars a year. He also says Warner and Disney impose strict standards on suppliers. No corroboration was found; it has the shape of a founder origin story. (Flow #457)
- **Make vs. buy: the 2021 BNDES episode (auto-caption paraphrase).** On Flow #457 he says he asked BNDES to finance a Brazilian factory in exchange for a five-year purchase commitment, was refused, and the Chinese side financed instead, which is why the roughly 20% of his 4 million annual pieces once made in Brazil moved. His prescription: a credit line to import machinery plus tax incentive. This is the earlier, more concrete version of the "conta não fecha" stance in the 2025 interviews. (Flow #457)
- **Raise vs. bootstrap (2022 stance): an investor is not a prerequisite.** *"Hoje tem algo que me preocupa muito, o empreendedor acha que o negócio só decola com investidor. Isso não é verdade, eu construí a Chilli Beans sozinho."* [Today something worries me a lot: entrepreneurs think a business only takes off with an investor. That's not true, I built Chilli Beans on my own.] The claim sits against a franchise-funded model and against the 2025 to 2026 sale process (see Blind spots). ## Voice & style
Warm, extroverted, showman cadence, a frontman who learned to sell from a stage and never stopped. Speaks in punchy, repeatable maxims that end on a hard stop (*"...ponto."*, *"...simples como isso."*), the verbal equivalent of a hook. Heavy on first-person founder myth (the suitcase, the dream-named brand, the bands, the 50kg) and on **story** as both product philosophy and rhetorical method, he *demonstrates* the thesis by telling you a story while explaining that stories sell, and reaches for musicians (David Bowie/Ziggy Stardust) as entrepreneurship case studies rather than business-school examples, explicitly naming Steve Jobs and Jeff Bezos as *not* his heroes. His authored prose (the Metrópoles column) reads noticeably more crafted than his interview cadence, sensory, almost musical sentences ("é aquele primeiro acorde que abre o show e faz a plateia vibrar", it's the first chord that opens the show), while staying in the same register of short declaratives and hard stops. In his written voice more broadly (LinkedIn, book synopses) he's a direct, buddy-to-buddy motivator, *"Cara, eu vou te falar uma coisa"*, that leans into vulnerability (being called "Napoleão" by his team, an earlier-pass detail with no URL in sources; admitting the pandemic "desestabilizou psicologicamente" him "até hoje") before landing the lesson.

Reaches for vivid Brazilian register: *arroz com feijão* for the unglamorous basics, *gracinhas* for the bold moves they earn, *brilho no olho* for talent, *estar no jogo* / *virar o jogo* / *coragem de ter atitude* / *ir para o jogo* for the posture he prescribes, *marca mutante* for a brand that must keep changing to survive, and *oxigênio* for whatever channel keeps the business breathing in a crisis. Treats the brand as a living cultural actor that must keep reading youth culture, and is willing to say plainly that a whole generation is missing something ("falta paciência, falta identidade... falta coragem"). Blunt and unsentimental about partners who underperform (*"nos ferramos"*) and about Brazil's tax/industrial failure (*"dá dó," "o imposto destrói as empresas," "uma judiação"*), yet disciplined about not wasting breath on what he can't change (*tarifaço*). Self-deprecating about scale relative to harder things (raising kids). Bilingual register (US years) surfaces in English asides, and when interviewed directly in English (Forbes US) his cadence compresses into shorter, more conventionally corporate sentences than his Portuguese register, a useful tell that some of his "voice" is genre-specific to Brazilian business press. Where Naval compresses to aphorism for *abstraction*, Caito compresses for *salesmanship*, the line is built to be retweeted by a franchisee, not contemplated.

Register note from the added sources: the 2018 Consumidor Moderno Q&A shows a management-theory register (*"senhores feudais"*, *"chefe da matilha"*, *"célula"*) more reflective than his sales cadence; the 2015 to 2020 event coverage shows the same food and spice idiom (*arroz, feijão, amor e pimenta*; *colocar pimenta*) used consistently as a classification system (the basics, the passion, the differentiator), and he applies *arroz com feijão* in both directions, as the solid base and as the rut to escape. The Flow #457 captions, which are machine transcription and not audio-verified, suggest a joking, informal register with self-deprecation (junior staff calling him tiozinho, per the captions); nothing from them is placed in quotation marks. In the 2026 crisis coverage the only first-person line found is the 3 September video, a short, structural statement (*"base sólida"*), noticeably plainer than his story-driven registers; the longer 2026 statements are company notes in the third person, not his voice.

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Rony Meisler: brand-as-story vs. brand-as-purpose.** Both are Brazilian brand-builders who refuse to compete on price, but the engine differs. Caito's moat is *narrative + novelty + licensed pop culture*, story for its own commercial sake, a fast-collection flywheel. Meisler's is *purpose and community* ("purpose is the greatest driver of value," audience-before-product). Note the seam: Caito *also* says "uma marca deve ser comunidade" and talks about a brand's "alma" caring for "sociedade e do planeta," so the gap is narrower than it looks, but Caito's community forms around the *product's story*, while Meisler's forms around a *cause*. A founder choosing between "give the product a story" and "give the brand a mission" gets genuinely different playbooks.
- **vs. Rony Meisler: keep control vs. sell and hand off.** Caito ran ~25 years founder-controlled and sold only a *minority* stake, ring-fenced to one vertical, and is still operating his company. Meisler sold Reserva outright to Arezzo&Co (~R$715M) and exited, framing departure as "exit at the top of the parabola." Caito's instinct is to retain the brand and keep playing.
- **vs. Rony Meisler: convergent, not opposed: reject "the first million" as the goal.** Both explicitly warn founders off a pure money target. Meisler: *"Não somos uma empresa que pretende vender roupas para pessoas e sim uma empresa de pessoas que vendem roupas."* Caito: *"esquece de uma vez por todas essa história do primeiro milhão... você precisa, assim como eu, de algo que toque o coração, envolva as pessoas, conte histórias."* Where they part again is *what* replaces money: Meisler names a social cause (1P=5P); Caito names story and personal fulfillment, not a cause.
- **vs. Warren Buffett: durable moat vs. perpetual reinvention.** Buffett prizes a wide, *static* moat ("a business that any idiot can run, because someday one will"). Caito's thesis is the inverse: the moat *is* the rate of change, stop launching weekly, stop rejecting seasonality, stop reading Gen Z, and *"a gente vai morrer."* His book's "marca mutante" [mutant brand] concept literalizes this: a brand that survives by never stopping mutation. Buffett would distrust an advantage re-earned every week; Caito would call a static moat in fashion a death sentence.
- **vs. Bernard Arnault: accessible ubiquity vs. scarcity.** Arnault's luxury logic is controlled scarcity, vertical ownership of maisons, and price as a status signal. Caito runs the inverse on his own product: democratized price, mass franchise ubiquity (~1,400 doors, a target of 3,200 in five years), self-service handling, and *more* drops, not fewer. Both monetize desire through brand; one rations it, the other floods the market. Caito's concrete version: about 10 new sunglass models a week, stock turned in about 2.5 weeks (Flow #457, paraphrase), self-service displays without the optician's counter, prescription frames priced at R$149 to 300 against a ~R$400 market average (Exame), and a ~2% annual discount rate defended by story rather than by scarcity (Money Times). Where Arnault protects price by limiting supply, Caito protects price by changing the product faster than it can be discounted. *(Caito's accessibility stance is explicit; the Arnault contrast is inferred from each one's documented model.)*
- **vs. Jeff Bezos: story/relationship-first vs. customer-metric-first: now partly first-party documented, not just inferred.** Bezos starts from the customer and works backward through data and operational obsession. Caito starts from the *story* and trusts narrative to create the customer (*"a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço"*), and now says so about Bezos personally, not just about method: *"Não tenho nada contra... o [Jeff] Bezos, da Amazon, que é um cara incrível. Mas as minhas inspirações são mais do mundo da música e da arte."* His own LinkedIn Experience text draws the contrast even sharper: *"Investir... é mais rentável do que a avaliação exclusivamente numérica. E o brilho nos olhos às vezes fala mais alto que o balanço financeiro."* On a tie-break, Bezos reaches for the metric; Caito reaches for the myth and the "brilho no olho", and he's now on record naming Bezos as a non-model for that reason, not just a different-industry operator.
- **vs. Steve Jobs: genius acknowledged, explicitly not adopted as a model.** A parallel, equally first-party disagreement in the same quote as the Bezos one: *"Não tenho nada contra o Steve Jobs. Eu acho ele um gênio, mas ele não é meu herói."* Where Jobs-style founder mythology centers product obsession and a singular design vision, Caito names musicians and a Brazilian consumer brand (O Boticário) as his actual reference points, a deliberate move away from the Silicon Valley founder canon toward music/art and Brazilian retail culture.
- **vs. Frederico Trajano: narrative-led brand vs. data/platform-led retail.** Trajano's Magalu thesis is digital platform, logistics, and data as the moat; Caito's is brand narrative and licensing, with ops deliberately outsourced. Caito would say a retailer without a story competes on price and dies; a Trajano-style operator would say a brand without owned data and logistics is fragile. The seam is narrower in practice than in rhetoric: Caito's own record includes an AI tool that assembles about 80% of each franchisee's order from that store's sales (Flow #457, paraphrase), an inventory-AI claim of +20% sales and stock cut from three months to two weeks, and warehouse robotics in Extrema (Forbes US, set/2024). The disagreement is about where the moat sits, not whether to use data: Caito treats data as plumbing under the story, Trajano as the platform itself. Both were on the bill of the same 2019 franchising forum (Mercado&Consumo pre-event coverage), but no source records a direct exchange. *(Inferred contrast; both are documented Brazilian retail operators.)*
- **vs. VC-funded startup culture (unnamed counterpart, not an advisor on the council).** Caito: *"o empreendedor acha que o negócio só decola com investidor. Isso não é verdade, eu construí a Chilli Beans sozinho"* (Startupi, fev/2022); and, on Flow #457 per auto-caption paraphrase, a founder who starts with large venture funding risks spending energy on staying alive instead of making the business work. A named VC advisor would hold that early capital buys speed; he holds that maturation time and basics (*arroz e feijão*) come first. The 2025 to 2026 sale process and debt (see Blind spots) complicate the claim.
- **vs. older-generation executives (unnamed): participation vs. command.** In 2018 he says *"É difícil esperar essas mudanças de pessoas que já eram presidentes na época de 2000, 1990. A cabeça é diferente."* [It's hard to expect those changes from people who were already presidents in 2000 or 1990. The mindset is different.] Where hierarchical command-and-control leaders (the dossier's inference; no one is named) would pay for compliance, he argues respect is the price of keeping the new generation. (Consumidor Moderno, jan/2018)

## Blind spots
- **Public register is narrative, not operational.** Across interviews, LinkedIn posts, and even his own book's synopsis he reaches for story, courage, and attitude far more than unit economics, cohort retention, or franchise-level P&L. As a council member he is a strong lens on *brand, storytelling, licensing, fast-collections, crisis leadership, and franchise growth* and a weak source on *finance, supply chain, and the franchisee's downside*, for which Buffett (capital discipline), Bezos (ops/metrics), or the franchisee-complaint and litigation record are better counterweights.
- **The story moat is thin against price at the bottom.** "Story kills price" works for the engaged buyer, but marketplaces, fast e-commerce, and low-cost optical chains pressure exactly the price-sensitive segment, and his own optical pivot *did* compete on price (R$149 to 300), quietly contradicting the sunglasses doctrine. The narrative premium is real but not universal.
- **Franchisee friction is documented across multiple, independent legal cases: not just consumer complaints.** Beyond Chilli Beans's física division carrying ~5,900+ Reclame Aqui complaints (online store ~2,000; company responds to >90%; Ótica Chilli Beans holds an "RA10"-tier reputation), three separate, sourced disputes complicate the harmony narrative: (1) a São José do Rio Pardo (SP) franchisee's suit alleging Chilli Beans' promised operational support never materialized, which the franchisee says drove its 2020 closure; a São Paulo appellate court (TJ-SP, Aug 2024) found the franchise contract's mandatory arbitration clause *abusive* given the power imbalance between franchisor and franchisee, letting the case proceed to trial. (Reclame Aqui; Direito News, ago/2024)
- **Franchisee friction (2): the territorial-exclusivity penalty.** A Catarina Fashion Outlet franchisee penalized under a territorial-exclusivity clause for a different franchisee's store 70km away; a São Paulo judge (Dec 2024) suspended the penalty, with the franchisee's lawyer arguing the clause let Chilli Beans authorize or block other franchisees' expansion at its own "critério exclusivo," and calling it a potential *"abuso de posição dominante"*. (Cerveira Advogados/Folha, dez/2024)
- **Franchisee friction (3): a franchisee's bankruptcy filing, and the unanswered mirror image.** A Cuiabá-based Chilli Beans franchisee (part of a family business group) filed for corporate bankruptcy protection in 2017 with ~R$9M in debt, citing import/logistics disruption (World Cup-era infrastructure works) as a contributing cause. None of these three cases carries a Caito Maia quote in response, they sit as an unaddressed mirror image of his own *"nos ferramos"* admission and the *"oxigenação"* he frames as healthy renewal, which from a franchisee's chair can also read as the franchisor pushing remodel/restock costs and swapping operators while retaining the upside and the contractual leverage. (Jornal do Comércio; Gazeta Digital, mar/2017)
- **His own self-presentation draws a literary-style critique.** A reader review of "E se Colocar Pimenta?" describes the book's language as caught "em cima do muro, entre o jornalístico, o marquetês e o casual" [on the fence between journalistic, marketing-speak, and casual], i.e., the text sometimes foregrounds its own voice/style over the substance of the story it's telling. Thin evidence (one review, paraphrase-only, not independently confirmed verbatim) but a real, sourced data point that his salesmanship-first voice doesn't land uniformly, even with sympathetic readers. (Skoob reviews, via search)
- **Founder-as-brand concentration risk.** The company's identity is fused to Caito's personal myth and cultural instincts ("entender a geração Z ou morrer"); even his second book is built entirely around his own protagonist arc and personality, and a feature film about the brand (announced for 2027) will cast an actor to play him. A strength while he reads the zeitgeist correctly; a single point of failure when he doesn't. Succession and the durability of taste-driven advantage are addressed mostly with charisma, not systems.
- **Macro framing cuts both ways.** He attributes soft retail years substantially to interest rates (*"o país não sobrevive a juros de quase 20%"*, *"eles sufocam e matam o varejo"*), yet also preaches not spending energy on what he can't control. The macro-as-cause framing can externalize execution and competitive issues onto the rate cycle; the two stances sit in unresolved tension. Concrete case: in October 2025 he called 8% growth *"um milagre dentro da atual conjuntura"* and blamed a 14% rate that would kill retail (Meio & Mensagem); eleven months later, per attendees quoted by NeoFeed, he told staff *"Hoje estamos fazendo milagre"* at a moment when holders had already deferred two debenture installments (which, per NeoFeed, then went unpaid by the new 25 September date). Bloomberg Línea's own analysis says *"A crise da rede não vem de retração do setor"*. The rate story and the company-specific leverage story are not reconciled by him in any source read.
- **"Empreendedorismo de palco" is a critique with a self-serving edge.** His book positions itself against "stage entrepreneurs who romanticize their results", but Caito himself is a career stage performer (musician, Shark Tank judge, keynote speaker, paid-course instructor) whose own public register is built almost entirely from stage-ready maxims and myth. He even states the defense directly, *"Eu não sou empreendedor de palco. O livro mostra a vida real"*, which is an assertion, not a demonstration; the line between "doing the real work" and "performing having done the real work" is one he asserts rather than shows with granular operating detail (financials, cohort data, franchisee-level P&L are essentially absent from his own public statements).
- **State-intervention critique is asymmetric.** He condemns Brazilian government taxation as destructive ("o imposto destrói as empresas") while praising the Chinese government's manufacturer-support model (a state-backed factory insurer) as something Brazil should emulate, suggesting the objection is to interventions that cost him money, not to state intervention in industry as such. Not reconciled or addressed by him in any source reviewed.
- **2026 financial distress: what is company-confirmed versus reported.** Company-confirmed (its own notes, as quoted by NeoFeed, Bloomberg Línea, Meio & Mensagem): advanced, friendly negotiation with creditor banks to lengthen terms and cut interest; no discussion of judicial recovery; a restructuring executive joining on 1 October. Reported but not confirmed: NeoFeed's claim, from anonymous *"Fontes do setor"*, that filing was imminent (conditional: documents *"já estariam"* on his desk). Bloomberg Línea's article names no anonymous source, relies on the company note and public documents, and says the negotiation is "considerada uma reestruturação de dívida feita fora do tribunal" and that "A crise da rede não vem de retração do setor" (the outlet's analysis, not the company's). (NeoFeed; Bloomberg Línea, 31/ago/2026)
- **Debt figures differ by outlet and are not reconciled.** R$216 mi (Dec 2023) and R$290 mi (2024, unaudited): NeoFeed, from the debenture prospectus, and Times Brasil, from documents it obtained. "perto de R$ 600 milhões" at the end of 2025: NeoFeed, citing "Fontes ligadas a empresas" (anonymous). Meio & Mensagem, attributing it to NeoFeed, words it "mais de R$ 600 milhões"; Economic News Brasil repeats the NeoFeed figure conditionally and notes it is not in any audited balance. About R$500 mi: Times Brasil/CNBC on 19 Sept, citing Pipeline, with about R$90 mi in debentures and the rest concentrated at Banco do Brasil, Caixa and Santander. The R$100 mi debenture (Mustang 25 Participações, unsecured, unrated) and the R$30 mi BNDES line (Apr 2025, for 235 franchisees and SMEs) are facility sizes from public documents, not balances (Bloomberg Línea). (NeoFeed; Times Brasil; Economic News Brasil)
- **Governance flags reported in the distress coverage.** Per NeoFeed and Times Brasil, the securitizer Vert convened debenture holders in April 2026 to consider early maturity because Mustang had not presented audited 2025 statements, and the company was later notified of a missed installment (May, per NeoFeed). InvestNews (no named source) reports that holders declined early liquidation, gave 45 days and tightened collateral. Times Brasil, citing Pipeline, reports waivers since May, the release of receivables as collateral, talks with the special-situations funds Akron and Vega, and that extrajudicial recovery "também não foi descartada" (distinct from the judicial route; the company did not comment on this). The month of the debenture issue (Feb vs. Mar 2025) and of the Vert call (Apr vs. May) differ across outlets. (NeoFeed; Times Brasil; InvestNews)
- **The debenture default and the personal guarantee (NeoFeed, 30/set/2026).** Per NeoFeed, which says it accessed the Vert material-fact notice: Vert declared early maturity of the R$100 mi debenture on 30 September after the July and August installments (R$16.5 mi together) were not paid, even though holders had voted on 10 August to defer them to 25 September; the debenture (maturity 29 Jan 2029, CDI + 3.9% a year) had an outstanding balance of R$89 mi per *"fontes a par da dívida"* (anonymous); six holder meetings preceded the default (per the data platform Vitrify); in June holders had suspended automatic early maturity despite the missing audited 2025 statements, and in July Vert released 60% of pledged card receivables (R$2.3 mi). NeoFeed writes *"O próprio Caito Maia é o avalista da transação"*. The company called it *"um trâmite legal do processo"* with negotiations *"promissoras"*. This makes the founder-as-brand risk literal: the founder is also a personal guarantor of the holding's market debt. Outcome after the cure period was not checked for this dossier. (NeoFeed, 30/set/2026)
- **Franchisee liquidity and collateral, and the ChilliPay question.** InvestNews says tighter collateral left *"menos liquidez livre para as franqueadas"* until each payment is confirmed; Economic News Brasil says documents show guarantees over credit rights including receivables from franchisee contracts. Economic News also notes ChilliPay (launched in August 2026 with Zoop, possibly extending to credit for franchisees) and adds that there is "Não há evidência pública" that its sales are part of the guarantees or that it was created because of the renegotiation. Read against his claim to balance margin with the franchisee, the sources frame the franchise network as part of the credit structure; none attributes a statement on this to him. (InvestNews, 31/ago/2026; Economic News Brasil, 1/set/2026)
- **Leadership change, and what the executive's history does and does not show.** Dela Togna is described as an executive from restructurings (Galeazzi & Associados 2017 to 2022; running Ledervin since Aug 2023, per Bloomberg Línea and InvestNews citing his LinkedIn), and by NeoFeed and Times Brasil as CEO of Paquetá The Shoes Company in 2022 to 2023. His title is reported inconsistently: vice-president (company note as quoted by Bloomberg Línea and Meio & Mensagem), co-CEO (the note as quoted by NeoFeed; "nas palavras de Maia" per InvestNews), CRO (introduced that way the week before, per NeoFeed). (NeoFeed; Bloomberg Línea; InvestNews; Times Brasil)
