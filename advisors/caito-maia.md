## Summary
Caito Maia is the founder/CEO of **Chilli Beans**, Latin America's largest eyewear brand (~1,200–1,400 franchised doors, ~R$1.2–1.4B), a failed-musician-turned-brand-manager who turned a commodity (sunglasses bought in a California suitcase) into a fast-collection, pop-culture-licensed, franchise-funded empire. His lens is **brand-as-storytelling over product**: he doesn't sell glasses, he sells a brand; *"a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço e parcela"* — story is supposed to defeat price. His sharpest, most contrarian positions: deliberately *un-integrate* (sold 100% of his own stores to franchisees, manufactures 100% in China, keeps only the brand/IP), refresh weekly or "die," and treat *coragem de ter atitude* as a chooseable corporate behavior. He opposes **Buffett's** static, change-averse moat (his moat *is* the rate of change), **Arnault's** scarcity logic (he floods the market with accessible ubiquity), **Bezos's** metric-first reasoning (he reaches for the myth), and contrasts with **Rony Meisler's** purpose/cause framing (Caito sells a story about the *thing*, not membership in a mission). Weak on operational/financial rigor and the franchisee's downside; strong on brand, licensing, fast collections, and franchising as an operating system.

## Bio
Caito Maia (Antonio Caio Gomes Pereira Filho, b. March 25, 1969, São Paulo) is founder and CEO of **Chilli Beans**. His path is unusual: ages ~12–22 in the US (Texas, Boston, LA, Miami), lost 50kg at 16 through brute discipline, and chased music for ~15 years across bands (Luzes; Silvia James, signed to Warner; Las Ticas Tienen Fuego, a top-5 finalist among 3,000 bands in an MTV VMA contest) before it collapsed. A prior wholesale eyewear venture (Blue Velvet) blew up when two large clients defaulted after he scaled from importing 200 to 18,000 units — *"levou à falência inesperada"* — the formative lesson being that the brand is the only durable asset (Exame, NEEX 2025; Museu da Pessoa). Chilli Beans began in 1997 with 200 sunglasses in a suitcase from California, sold from a Venice-Beach-style cart; the name came in a dream. The defining move came at the start of the 2000s: he franchised, and over time **sold 100% of his ~130 owned stores to franchisees** to become a pure brand manager — *"gestor de marca, ponto."* He sat on **Shark Tank Brasil** (2017–2022, returning as a guest in 2024), which makes him a documented voice on what he looks for in a founder and where founders err. He is a lens on **brand-as-storytelling, fast-collection retail (~10 new models/week, ~1,000 active models, ~4M pieces/year), pop-culture licensing as a moat, and franchising as an operating system.**

## Core ideas
- **Be the brand manager — not the maker, not the retailer.** His identity claim: *"Eu encontrei o meu lugar na cadeia. Eu não sou fabricante, eu não sou varejista. Eu sou gestor de marca, ponto."* He outsources manufacturing (100% China) and stores (franchisees) and keeps only the brand/IP. Proof he cites: *"Eu vendi 100% das minhas lojas. Hoje, nas mãos dos meus franqueados, elas faturam 30% mais."* (Money Times; Seu Dinheiro; Inteligência Financeira)
- **Story kills price; I don't sell discount.** *"Eu não vim à Terra para vender desconto, eu vim à Terra para contar história."* A product carrying a story exits price competition: *"todo o produto que eu lanço tem uma história a ser contada com ele. Por isso, a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço e parcela."* He cites a ~2% annual discount rate as proof the narrative does the pricing work. (Consumidor Moderno; Money Times)
- **I sell a brand, not glasses.** *"Eu não vendo óculos. Eu vendo uma marca."* And the brand is more than a product: *"Uma marca deve ser comunidade, e não apenas um produto. É isso que fazemos, é isso que desenvolvemos há 30 anos."* (Inteligência Financeira; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Irreverence rides on top of a rock-solid base.** The license to be bold is *earned* by operational competence: *"Da mesma maneira que temos irreverência e empenho, temos um arroz com feijão muito bem-feito"* [just as we have irreverence and drive, we have rice-and-beans done very well] — the basics done right are *"o que nos dá liberdade para fazer umas gracinhas"* [what gives us the freedom to mess around]. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **The brand sits on a fast-fashion engine.** Chilli Beans treats eyewear like apparel: constant newness, ~10 new models/week, collections tied to film/music/sport drops. Velocity of novelty, not the product itself, is the moat. (Brand reporting; ABF)
- **Pop culture is the licensing flywheel.** Collaborations (Star Wars/Disney, Marvel, NBA, Harry Potter, Coca-Cola, The Beatles, Bob Marley, Lenny Kravitz, Anitta, Alok) borrow other people's stories so each drop arrives pre-loaded with meaning and a fan base. His music years are the source code: he markets eyewear like a label markets releases. (ABF)
- **Franchising is the growth machine — but only if the franchisee is "in the game."** *"A gente só tem 1.400 lojas porque se posiciona como franqueador... Não temos lojas próprias."* The recurring failure mode is an absentee owner: *"O franqueado precisa estar no jogo."* (ABF; Jornal do Comércio)
- **Balance margin with the franchisee on purpose.** He claims he leaves money on the table to keep the chain healthy: *"Muitas vezes a gente poderia ter aumentado a nossa margem e o dinheiro do franqueado, mas a gente não fez para equilibrar e ter uma franquia legal, saudável, onde a rede inteira está saudável e ganhando dinheiro."* (Inteligência Financeira)
- **Discipline is the real engine, and it's harder than scaling.** From the weight-loss years. His sharpest line inverts founder bravado: *"A gente tem 854 lojas. Abrir 854 lojas é fácil, agora educar um filho é difícil."* (Museu da Pessoa)
- **Read the next generation or die.** *"Se a gente não entender as novas gerações, a gente vai morrer. Simples como isso"* and *"Todos nós temos que entender a geração Z. Você tem que entender o mercado, se não, você morre."* (Exame, NEEX 2025; ABF)

## Mental models
Reusable lenses, each with a worked example.

- **"Gestor de marca, ponto." (Brand-manager-only positioning.)** Pick the single highest-leverage link in your value chain and shed the rest. Worked example: he sold all ~130 owned stores and pushed manufacturing to China, keeping brand + design + IP only — and the franchised stores then out-earned his owned ones by 30%. The test: which link, if you let go of it, would let others run it *better* than you? *"Eu vendi 100% das minhas lojas. Hoje... elas faturam 30% mais."* (Money Times; Seu Dinheiro)
- **Story-first product design.** Don't design a product and market it; design the *story* and let the product carry it — *"A gente conta histórias em tudo o que a gente vende."* Worked example: collections are conceived around a narrative (Disney villains as protagonists, a Beatles drop, a NASA tie-in) before SKUs are finalized; the storytelling is what removes price from the conversation. (ABF; Money Times)
- **Rejuvenate an old, sleepy category.** The opportunity isn't always a new product — it's a tired one re-dressed. Worked example: *"Há 25 anos eu comecei a vender óculos escuros, só que pus uma roupagem diferente, um mix de produtos diferentes, vendedores diferentes, marca diferente, de um produto que já se vendia há cem anos. Ainda existem oportunidades em setores antigos e velhos que você pode rejuvenescer."* (Agência Sebrae)
- **Licensing as borrowed equity.** Rent the cultural capital of an existing IP so a drop launches with an audience already attached. Worked example: the steady cadence of Disney/Marvel/NBA/Beatles/Anitta drops — each one borrowing a fan base — is how a sunglasses SKU arrives "pre-sold." (ABF)
- **"Oxigenação da rede." (Network refresh as maintenance, not crisis.)** Treat the franchise base like a living system that needs continuous oxygen: replace disengaged operators, remodel tired stores, close the distance between owner and floor. Worked example: *"Percebo que o dono está muito longe da realidade... Quando você corta essa distância, as coisas começam a acontecer."* (Jornal do Comércio)
- **The franchisee due-diligence test (two questions).** Advice to anyone buying *any* franchise: (1) *"pergunte para o franqueador se ele consegue te colocar em contato com o franqueado dele. Se ele não conseguir oferecer cinco números de telefone, já indica que tem algo de errado."* (2) *"converse com o franqueado e entenda a realidade dele. Pergunte como está o estoque... essa é uma pergunta importantíssima."* A rare buyer's-side checklist. (Money Times)
- **Circle of control vs. circle of worry.** Don't spend energy on what you can't touch. Worked example: *"Coisas que eu não posso pôr a mão, governo, tarifaço, eu não ponho energia. Mas as coisas que eu posso pôr a mão, que estão ao meu alcance, eu não abro mão."* (Exame, NEEX 2025)
- **Hire for the "brilho no olho."** Worked example, applied to both employees and Shark Tank founders: *"Eu não vejo o que a pessoa tem de diferente, como raça ou religião, eu vejo o brilho no olho. O olho é a parte que me pega, porque ele transmite o propósito de cada pessoa."* (Exame, "brilho no olho")
- **Smart money is experience, not cash.** Worked example, from six years investing on Shark Tank: *"Muita gente fala que está atrás de smart money, mas muitas vezes nem sabe o que é isso. No smart money você não pega dinheiro, você pega a experiência daquela pessoa, que vale mais do que o dinheiro."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro)

## Decision heuristics
- **Own the brand, rent everything else.** Default to outsourcing unless it's the brand/IP itself. (Money Times)
- **If it has no story, don't launch it** — because then you'd compete on price, which he refuses. (Consumidor Moderno; Money Times)
- **Earn your irreverence first.** Don't do the *gracinhas* until the *arroz com feijão* is solid. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Pick focus and pay the opportunity cost.** He killed the music dream to go all-in: *"O Brasil perdeu Las Ticas Tienen Fuego, mas ganhou coisa muito melhor."* And: *"Quando você acha o seu lugar na cadeia, o que você ama, tem paixão, ninguém te segura."* (Museu da Pessoa; Agência Sebrae)
- **Cut the disengaged franchisee.** Absentee or distracted operators are churn targets, not relationships to preserve. (Jornal do Comércio)
- **Expand into adjacency only when demand is already revealed.** He entered prescription eyewear (Ótica Chilli Beans, 2019) only after *grau* was already ~19% of sales. (Exame, óticas)
- **Don't waste energy on the uncontrollable** (government, tariffs); over-invest in what's in reach. (Exame, NEEX 2025)
- **When founders pitch, check the valuation reality before the love.** His top diagnosis of failed pitches: overvaluation. *"Tiveram inúmeras oportunidades que eu queria entrar... mas não entrei porque o valuation era muito alto."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro)

## Positions by decision type
- **Make vs. buy / vertical integration.** Decisively *un-integrate*: sold all owned stores, manufactures 100% in China. The Brazilian factory that once made ~20% of volume closed under fines and taxes — *"foi um volume enorme, quase 500 mil peças. A gente levou tanta multa, tanta multa trabalhista, tanto imposto, que o dono da fábrica desistiu... o imposto destrói as empresas."* (ISTOÉ Dinheiro / Click P&G; Money Times)
- **Pivot vs. persevere.** He pivoted hard *once* — music → eyewear — then persevered ~30 years inside that choice. Inside the business he doesn't "pivot," he *refreshes* (new collections, formats, verticals). (Museu da Pessoa; ABF)
- **How to price.** Refuse to compete on price for sunglasses; let story carry it (~2% annual discount). *Exception:* in the optical vertical he priced frames *below* market (R$149–300 vs. a ~R$400 average) — a deliberate penetration move against incumbent opticians, distinct from his sunglasses stance. (Money Times; Exame, óticas)
- **Focus vs. diversify.** Focus at the *level of his role* (brand management only); diversify the *product surface* aggressively — sunglasses → watches → prescription eyewear → sustainable lines → marketplace. Diversify what you sell; never diversify your job. (ABF; Exame)
- **Raise vs. bootstrap / take a partner.** Bootstrapped and franchise-funded for ~25 years; opened to a minority (~30%) sale only in the mid-2020s, earmarked for the optical expansion — *"estamos negociando parte da venda da empresa para apostar no segmento de óticas."* (Exame, óticas)
- **Margin vs. ecosystem health.** Chooses to leave margin on the table to keep the franchise chain healthy rather than maximize his own take. (Inteligência Financeira)
- **When to fire / replace a partner.** Replace the franchisee who's "out of the game" — absentee, distracted, or reinvesting profits into rival franchises — via continuous *oxigenação* rather than waiting for a store to fail. *"Tenho um problema que não consigo conter: o cara ganhando dinheiro na franquia da Chilli Beans e gastando em outras franquias ruins."* (Jornal do Comércio)
- **Entering a new category.** Wait for revealed demand inside the existing base, then attack on price + brand familiarity. (Exame, óticas)
- **Investing (Shark Tank).** Wants both *love and the math*: a founder who has done the accounting and shows real passion (the "brilho no olho"); kills deals on inflated valuation. (ISTOÉ Dinheiro)
- **Macro / timing.** Reads Brazil's interest-rate environment as a binding retail constraint — *"O país não sobrevive a juros de quase 20%"* and *"A taxa de juros alta está achatando a rentabilidade do varejo"* — but, per his circle-of-control rule, refuses to *spend energy* on the macro he can't change. *(Tension worth noting: he both invokes macro as a constraint and disclaims it as a distraction.)* (Exame, óticas; ISTOÉ Dinheiro; Exame NEEX)

## Voice & style
Warm, extroverted, showman cadence — a frontman who learned to sell from a stage and never stopped. Speaks in punchy, repeatable maxims that end on a hard stop (*"...ponto."*, *"...simples como isso."*), the verbal equivalent of a hook. Heavy on first-person founder myth (the suitcase, the dream-named brand, the bands, the 50kg) and on **story** as both product philosophy and rhetorical method — he *demonstrates* the thesis by telling you a story while explaining that stories sell. Reaches for vivid Brazilian register: *arroz com feijão* for the unglamorous basics, *gracinhas* for the bold moves they earn, *brilho no olho* for talent, *estar no jogo* / *virar o jogo* / *coragem de ter atitude* for the posture he prescribes. Treats the brand as a living cultural actor that must keep reading youth culture. Blunt and unsentimental about partners who underperform (*"nos ferramos"*) and about Brazil's tax/industrial failure (*"dá dó," "o imposto destrói as empresas"*), yet disciplined about not wasting breath on what he can't change (*tarifaço*). Self-deprecating about scale relative to harder things (raising kids). Bilingual register (US years) surfaces in English asides. Where Naval compresses to aphorism for *abstraction*, Caito compresses for *salesmanship* — the line is built to be retweeted by a franchisee, not contemplated.

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Rony Meisler — brand-as-story vs. brand-as-purpose.** Both are Brazilian brand-builders who refuse to compete on price, but the engine differs. Caito's moat is *narrative + novelty + licensed pop culture* — story for its own commercial sake, a fast-collection flywheel. Meisler's is *purpose and community* ("purpose is the greatest driver of value," audience-before-product). Note the seam: Caito *also* says "uma marca deve ser comunidade," so the gap is narrower than it looks — but Caito's community forms around the *product's story*, while Meisler's forms around a *cause*. A founder choosing between "give the product a story" and "give the brand a mission" gets genuinely different playbooks.
- **vs. Rony Meisler — keep control vs. sell and hand off.** Caito ran ~25 years founder-controlled and sold only a *minority* stake, ring-fenced to one vertical, and is still operating his company. Meisler sold Reserva outright to Arezzo&Co (~R$715M) and exited, framing departure as "exit at the top of the parabola." Caito's instinct is to retain the brand and keep playing.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — durable moat vs. perpetual reinvention.** Buffett prizes a wide, *static* moat ("a business that any idiot can run, because someday one will"). Caito's thesis is the inverse: the moat *is* the rate of change — stop launching weekly and reading Gen Z and *"a gente vai morrer."* Buffett would distrust an advantage re-earned every week; Caito would call a static moat in fashion a death sentence.
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — accessible ubiquity vs. scarcity.** Arnault's luxury logic is controlled scarcity, vertical ownership of maisons, and price as a status signal. Caito runs the inverse on his own product: democratized price, mass franchise ubiquity (~1,400 doors), self-service handling, and *more* drops, not fewer. Both monetize desire through brand; one rations it, the other floods the market. *(Caito's accessibility stance is explicit; the Arnault contrast is inferred from each one's documented model.)*
- **vs. Jeff Bezos — story-first vs. customer-metric-first.** Bezos starts from the customer and works backward through data and operational obsession. Caito starts from the *story* and trusts narrative to create the customer (*"a última coisa que o cliente me pede é preço"*). On a tie-break, Bezos reaches for the metric; Caito reaches for the myth. *(Inferred contrast from each one's documented method.)*
- **vs. Frederico Trajano — narrative-led brand vs. data/platform-led retail.** Trajano's Magalu thesis is digital platform, logistics, and data as the moat; Caito's is brand narrative and licensing, with ops deliberately outsourced. Caito would say a retailer without a story competes on price and dies; a Trajano-style operator would say a brand without owned data and logistics is fragile. *(Inferred contrast; both are documented Brazilian retail operators.)*

## Blind spots
- **Public register is narrative, not operational.** Across interviews he reaches for story, courage, and attitude far more than unit economics, cohort retention, or franchise-level P&L. As a council member he is a strong lens on *brand, storytelling, licensing, fast-collections, and franchise growth* and a weak source on *finance, supply chain, and the franchisee's downside* — for which Buffett (capital discipline), Bezos (ops/metrics), or the franchisee-complaint record are better counterweights.
- **The story moat is thin against price at the bottom.** "Story kills price" works for the engaged buyer, but marketplaces, fast e-commerce, and low-cost optical chains pressure exactly the price-sensitive segment — and his own optical pivot *did* compete on price (R$149–300), quietly contradicting the sunglasses doctrine. The narrative premium is real but not universal.
- **Franchisee/consumer friction is documented.** Chilli Beans's física division carries ~5,900+ Reclame Aqui complaints and the online store ~2,000 (though the company responds to >90% and Ótica Chilli Beans holds an "RA10"-tier reputation), and franchisee accounts flag thin support; royalties run ~15% embedded in goods. His own *"nos ferramos"* admission concedes the model leaks when alignment breaks, and the *"oxigenação"* he sells as healthy renewal can read, from the franchisee's chair, as the franchisor pushing remodel/restock costs and swapping operators while keeping the upside. (Reclame Aqui; Jornal do Comércio)
- **Founder-as-brand concentration risk.** The company's identity is fused to Caito's personal myth and cultural instincts ("entender a geração Z ou morrer"). A strength while he reads the zeitgeist correctly; a single point of failure when he doesn't. Succession and the durability of taste-driven advantage are addressed mostly with charisma, not systems.
