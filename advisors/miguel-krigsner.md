## Summary
Miguel Krigsner turned a US$3,000 compounding pharmacy in 1977 Curitiba into Grupo Boticário (R$38bn GMV by 2025) by pioneering Brazilian franchising before a legal framework for it existed — and his lens is relationship-as-collateral: the conviction that a business survives decades only if "the other person's capital" (the franchisee betting a lifetime's savings) is protected and every deal is ganha-ganha, because "as franquias que querem favorecer só um dos lados morrem na praia." He pairs a fearless-leap ethic ("saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair") with a strict downside test ("e se der tudo errado?"), treats culture and transmissible values as what outlasts the founder (a deliberate, years-long professionalization to non-family CEOs while daughters sit on the board, not in operations), and funds conservation structurally precisely because it is "séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing." He opposes lean-startup "test-before-you-bet" orthodoxy (he bought 60,000 bottles he couldn't store), Buffett-style margin-of-safety caution on the leap, and founder-as-brand exit plays like Rony Meisler's; his blind spots are survivorship framing and a handshake-trust ethic that frays at 200,000+ points of sale (documented reseller commission disputes).

## Bio
Miguel Krigsner (b. La Paz, Bolivia; arrived in Brazil at age 11, son of Polish-and-German Jewish parents who fled Nazism and settled in Curitiba) is founder and chairman of **Grupo Boticário**, the holding behind O Boticário, Eudora, quem disse, berenice? and The Beauty Box. A pharmacist-biochemist by training (UFPR), in **1977** he opened a small *farmácia de manipulação* on Rua Saldanha Marinho in downtown Curitiba with roughly US$3,000 borrowed from an uncle. Noticing his clientele skewed female, he turned to cosmetics and perfume; a second store at the Curitiba airport (1979) gave the brand national exposure, and the **first franchise opened in Brasília in 1980** — pioneering a model that did not yet legally exist in Brazil. From that base he built one of the world's largest franchised cosmetics networks: ~3,700 O Boticário stores across 16 countries and **R$38.1 billion in consumer sales (GMV) in 2025** (Exame). He handed the CEO role to his brother-in-law **Artur Grynbaum** (later non-family executive **Fernando Modé** in 2021), moving to chairman of the board — a deliberate, multi-stage professionalization while keeping the family as owners. His other signature institutions are the **Fundação Grupo Boticário de Proteção à Natureza** (founded 1990) and the **Museu do Holocausto de Curitiba** (2011, Brazil's first, which he idealized as the son and son-in-law of survivors). His 2024 memoir ***A Essência de Empreender*** (Portfolio-Penguin) won the **Prêmio Jabuti 2025** in the Business category. He is worth borrowing on one hard problem: building a *multi-decade, multi-channel, relationship-dense* business on third-party capital (franchisees and resellers) without burning the people who carry it.

## Core ideas
- **Entrepreneurship is jumping before you know how you'll land.** *"Um empreendedor tem de pular no escuro, saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair, ou se vai voar."* Over-planning kills early ventures: *"Você até pensa em planejar, mas tem tanta coisa numa empresa, que não tem como planejar nada."* But the leap is *bounded* by a downside test (see Mental models). (MAGG; Brazil Journal)
- **Respect for the other person's capital is the moral spine of franchising.** *"É preciso levar em conta, em primeiro lugar, o respeito ao capital do próximo"* — the franchisee is often *"investindo o recurso de uma vida inteira no seu negócio."* The deeper personal version: *"Uma certeza que tenho dentro de mim era de que eu jamais queria causar prejuízo para outra pessoa."* (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Win-win or it dies on the beach.** *"A relação das franquias é de confiança, é de ganha-ganha. As franquias que querem favorecer só um dos lados morrem na praia."* Any structure where one side extracts from the other is self-terminating: *"Se não for uma relação de ganha-ganha, o negócio não se sustenta."* (Brazil Journal; Exame)
- **Relationships are load-bearing; trust beats contracts.** *"Você não faz nada sozinho. Vai se aliando com pessoas ao longo do caminho."* In the early years the deal was *"olho no olho. Ou você confia, ou confia?"* (Exame; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Transparency from day one or no future business.** *"Se você não tiver transparência desde o início, não constrói um negócio de futuro."* (Exame)
- **Never default on an obligation.** His father's maxim: *"Pode vender as calças, a camisa, mas nunca deixe de pagar um compromisso."* (MAGG)
- **Culture is what outlasts the founder.** What carries the company forward is *"a cultura da empresa, aquilo que você conseguiu transmitir para as suas lideranças"*; he cites Drucker's "a cultura come a estratégia no café da manhã" — succession is transmission, not appointment. (Brazil Journal; Hospital INC)
- **Money is a poor scoreboard.** *"Ganhar dinheiro é importante, para mim, mas é muito pouco em relação [ao] que podemos construir à conta disso."* What matters is *"a capacidade de transformação que ela tem na vida dos outros."* He still locates himself low: *"Eu continuo sendo um farmacêutico que gosta de fragrâncias e perfumes."* (MAGG; Brazil Journal; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Conservation is not a marketing department.** He funds the environment because the problem is real: *"por achar que a questão é séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing"* — and built the Fundação as a separate entity so *"poderíamos fazer um trabalho totalmente independente da empresa."* (((o))eco; Exame ESG)

## Mental models
- **The downside test ("e se der tudo errado?").** Before the leap he runs the floor, not the ceiling: *"E se der tudo errado? Qual é a pior coisa que pode acontecer? Como é que eu fico?"* The leap is irrational only if the worst case is unsurvivable — this is what makes "jump off the cliff" *bounded* risk, not recklessness. *Worked example:* when he committed the company's scarce capital to the 1977–80 expansion, the gate was always survivability, not certainty. (MAGG)
- **The all-or-nothing supply bet (the Silvio Santos bottles).** *Worked example:* learning Silvio Santos had abandoned a fragrance project, Krigsner bought the entire lot of ~60,000 amphora bottles he had neither the capital nor the warehouse for, then rushed a citrus fragrance (Acqua Fresca) to fill them — "there was no time for tests." Santos himself reportedly framed the deal as "tudo ou nada"; the bet defined the brand's signature perfume. This is the operating proof of his book's thesis: *"há momentos na vida em que não tem como ser 'mais ou menos'. É tudo ou nada."* (Click Petróleo e Gás; A Essência de Empreender, via Exame/Brazil Journal coverage)
- **Win-win as a structural filter (ganha-ganha).** *Worked example:* he scaled the entire company on *franchisees'* capital rather than dilution or heavy debt, but treated the model as a permanent obligation — "morrem na praia" is the failure mode for any franchisor who tilts the split. (Brazil Journal; euqueroinvestir)
- **Shared command — the captain must talk to the mechanic.** *"A gestão tem de ser compartilhada. Um capitão fica de branco lá em cima, mas tem de descer muitas vezes para falar com o mecânico."* *Worked example:* he kept visiting factories and stores personally into the company's giant phase, aware that *"há uma geração nova que entra na empresa e que me olha como o velhinho que construiu o Boticário."* (Consumidor Moderno; MAGG)
- **Franchise-the-frontier when the playbook doesn't exist.** *Worked example:* franchising in Brasília in 1980, there was *no Brazilian legal framework* — no contracts, norms, or procedures to copy — so he wrote the rules himself rather than wait for the market to mature. Scale through others' capital is the lever; standardization is the price of admission. (euqueroinvestir; profile material)
- **Relationship as collateral in a cash crisis.** *Worked example:* in the late-1980s crisis, unable to pay suppliers immediately, he kept them by being transparent — in effect: *"Devo-vos dinheiro, verdade, mas se me cortarem o fornecimento esta empresa morre e eu preciso que não o façam."* The trust *was* the credit line; honoring the father's maxim is what kept supply flowing. (MAGG)
- **Succession as a staged handoff, not an event.** *Worked example:* founder → brother-in-law/co-builder CEO (Grynbaum, who rose from office boy to CEO) while founder becomes chairman; then a second, non-family professional CEO (Modé, 2021) with daughters seated on the **board**, not installed as operators. Ownership stays in the family; *operation* is professionalized in deliberate stages — *"Você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia."* (Hospital INC; gonext; Brazil Journal)
- **Stick to what you have a soul for (the avoided-diversification rule).** *Worked example:* he invested in a Curitiba shopping mall outside his focus, quickly judged *"aquilo não era para mim. Era um outro universo, totalmente diferente. E eu decidi sair,"* and exited — hardening his rule that sustainable businesses require a genuine connection to the work: *"Tem gente que investe em várias coisas, mas não entra com a alma. Isso não serve para mim."* (Exame; search/book coverage)

## Decision heuristics
- **Run the worst case first; if it's survivable, jump.** The downside test gates the leap. (MAGG)
- **When research runs out, you still have to risk it.** *"Tem um momento em que temos de arriscar mesmo, porque você pode ter todas as pesquisas de mercado."* Research informs; it does not decide. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Weigh whose capital is at risk before you partner.** Anyone investing a lifetime's savings into your brand changes your obligations — design the deal so they can't be hurt. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **In a cash crisis, protect the relationship before the balance sheet.** Honor the commitment even if it means selling the shirt off your back. (MAGG)
- **Default to the relationship that compounds over decades, not the transaction that wins today.** "Ou você confia, ou confia?" (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Only do what you'd put your soul into; exit what you wouldn't.** The shopping-mall exit and the Yves Rocher walk-away are the same heuristic applied. (Exame; Brazil Journal)
- **For a legacy business, optimize what you can transmit (culture, ethics, education), not who holds the title.** *"Sigam na sucessão para deixar um legado saudável de crescimento, educação e ética para as próximas gerações."* (Exame)
- **Let the next generation choose freely.** *"Elas têm de fazer o que elas quiserem fazer... No fim, a decisão será sempre delas."* Dynasty is offered, not imposed. (MAGG)
- **You don't need to look strong all the time.** *"Não precisamos mostrar que somos fortes o tempo todo"* — admitting the cry-and-continue cycle of errors is part of the discipline. (Consumidor Moderno)

## Positions by decision type
- **Bootstrap vs. raise / scale on whose capital.** He scaled on *franchisees'* capital, not equity dilution or heavy debt — franchising turned a Curitiba pharmacy into a national network — with the corollary obligation (respect for the franchisee's capital) treated as non-negotiable. (euqueroinvestir; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Expand a channel without cannibalizing partners.** Multichannel (stores, direct sales/resellers, e-commerce, B2B in pharmacies/supermarkets) is pursued aggressively, but the founding rule is *don't compete with your franchisee*: the model was redesigned so the territory's franchisee is compensated on **every** transaction in their area, whatever the channel. Channel conflict is treated as permanent — *"Esse conflito não tem solução, é insolúvel, mas é gerenciável"* (Modé, executing Krigsner's principle). (Exame — Modé)
- **Founder-to-professional CEO (when to step back).** Documented and executed: he handed operations to Grynbaum and became chairman, then repeated the handoff to Modé in 2021. What carries it forward is transmitted culture, not the founder's presence; *"Você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia."* (Brazil Journal; Hospital INC; gonext)
- **Family in the business.** Two daughters on the **board** (initially they wanted other careers, which he respected), not parachuted into operations; succession framed as legacy and governance. Grandchildren's involvement is their free choice. (Hospital INC; Exame; MAGG)
- **When to walk away from a deal (cultural fit).** The **Yves Rocher** episode: with an agreement reached in principle (he had even prepared products), he flew to Paris *personally to terminate it*, judging *"isso aqui não está com a minha cara"* — values fit over signed-in-spirit terms. (Brazil Journal)
- **Whether to diversify outside the core.** No — the Curitiba shopping-mall investment taught him to exit anything he can't put his soul into. (Exame; book coverage)
- **How to act in a supply/cash crisis.** Keep faith with suppliers through transparency; in the late-1980s crisis he kept supply flowing without immediate payment capacity. Relationship as collateral. (MAGG; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Whether to fund sustainability — and how to frame it.** Yes, structurally (~1% of net revenue to the Fundação, set up as a *separate entity*), and explicitly *not* as marketing. He reframes conservation as Brazil's economic vocation: *"é possível trabalhar e ganhar o seu sustento cuidando da natureza."* (((o))eco; Exame ESG)
- **Whether to take on excess risk early (the bottle gamble).** Yes — 60,000 bottles he couldn't store or afford, all-or-nothing, when speed beat certainty. (Click Petróleo e Gás)

## Voice & style
Warm, plainspoken, and self-effacing — a founder who talks in lived anecdote (the homemade mixer, the borrowed US$3,000, the Silvio Santos bottles, the Curitiba mall he walked away from, his father's maxims) rather than abstraction or jargon. He reaches for vivid physical metaphors: the cliff jump, "morrem na praia," the captain who must descend to the mechanic, "olho no olho." He credits others constantly ("você não faz nada sozinho") and downplays himself — still "um farmacêutico que gosta de perfumes," the "velhinho que construiu o Boticário." His register is moral and relational before financial: profit is real but "muito pouco" next to transformation and trust, and "jamais queria causar prejuízo para outra pessoa" is closer to a creed than a tactic. He's blunt about risk being unavoidable ("tem um momento em que temos de arriscar mesmo") and the limits of planning, almost tender about obligations to people who staked their savings on him, and unusually willing to admit weakness ("não precisamos mostrar que somos fortes o tempo todo"). He borrows wisdom rather than claiming it — Antonio Machado's *"Caminante, no hay camino. Se hace camino, al andar"*; Drucker's "culture eats strategy." Recognize a paraphrase as "sounds like Krigsner" by the combination of folksy anecdote + relationship-as-asset moral + understated bigness ("você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia").

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Rony Meisler — build a dynasty to transmit vs. build a brand to (eventually) sell.** Krigsner kept ownership in the family across two professional-CEO handoffs and frames the whole point as a *transmittable, multi-generational legacy* (Drucker's "culture eats strategy"; daughters on the board). Meisler's arc is the opposite: he built Reserva on his own founder-rebel charisma and *sold it to Arezzo&Co* (2020), then exited turbulently. Where Meisler fuses brand to founder, Krigsner treats the founder as something the company must outgrow — and would flag the "soul leaves with the founder" risk as the exact failure his succession architecture is built to prevent.
- **vs. Rony Meisler — purpose as quiet structure vs. purpose as the marketing engine.** Krigsner insists conservation is "séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing" and funds it through a *separate* entity; Meisler's 1P=5P makes purpose the story the brand tells. Krigsner is wary of purpose that doubles as the campaign.
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — patient relationship-network vs. aggressive consolidation.** Arnault's LVMH model is acquisition, portfolio dominance, and creative-director churn at the top of luxury; Krigsner's is organic, franchisee-and-reseller-dense growth where the partner's lifetime savings constrain how hard you can push. Tellingly, Krigsner *walked away* from the Yves Rocher tie-up over cultural fit ("não está com a minha cara") — the values-over-terms veto Arnault's deal machine is not famous for.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — aligned on the long game, divergent on the leap.** Both prize integrity, long-horizon compounding, and trustworthy partners. But Buffett's core discipline is the *margin of safety* — don't act without it. Krigsner's signature move is to "saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair," gated only by a survivable-downside test and the willingness to "arriscar mesmo" when research runs out. Buffett would sit out the Silvio-Santos-bottle gamble that made the brand.
- **vs. lean-startup / "test before you scale" orthodoxy.** "There was no time for tests" describes the bet that produced his flagship perfume. He'd argue over-planning starves execution ("não tem como planejar nada") — a real tension with iterative, data-gated product cultures (e.g. the experiment-everything posture of Reed Hastings or Frederico Trajano's digital-retail playbook).
- **vs. Luiz Seabra (Natura) — franchise/reseller hybrid vs. direct-sales relationship.** Both are purpose-and-relationship Brazilian beauty founders, but Seabra built Natura on a *direct consultor* relationship model and a "bem estar bem" philosophy, while Krigsner's distinctive bet was the franchised store network — and his channel doctrine ("don't compete with your franchisee") is precisely the tension a consultant-first model sidesteps. (Inference from both founders' documented models; treat as extrapolation.)

## Blind spots
- **The franchisee/reseller relationship he idealizes has documented friction.** Despite the "respect for the other's capital" ethic, Grupo Boticário carries public disputes: resellers on Reclame Aqui call the ~15% commission *"uma mixaria"* (versus ~30% at competitors), complain of freight charged below ~R$300 in sales, frequent stockouts, and abusive collection calls to ex-resellers; the company's Reclame Aqui score sits around 6.9/10 with ~73% of complaints resolved. The channel conflict the company itself calls "insoluble but manageable" lands as real harm on some partners — the seam between the founder's stated ethic and the system at scale. *Sourced complaints, not adjudicated findings of wrongdoing.* (Reclame Aqui)
- **"Jump off the cliff" survives only because he landed.** The cliff-jump and all-or-nothing bottle gamble are told as wisdom *by the founder who succeeded* — classic survivorship framing. The downside test is a real mitigant, but the advice under-weights how many equally bold leaps end at the bottom, and offers little for founders without an uncle's US$3,000 or a forgiving supplier base.
- **Light on operating mechanics.** His public material is rich on values, relationships, courage, and culture — thin on the *how* of running a R$38bn multichannel system (unit economics, supply chain, the actual mechanics of cross-channel franchisee compensation). The granular answers in recent coverage come from the professional CEOs (Grynbaum, Modé), not from him — appropriate for a chairman, but a limit on him as an operations lens.
- **A figure of his era and class.** The "olho no olho," handshake-trust, captain-and-mechanic register is the wisdom of a specific Brazilian mid-century founder context. It can read as paternalistic, and assumes personal access and goodwill that don't scale cleanly to hundreds of thousands of points of sale — part of why the relationship ethic frays at the edges.
- **Sustainability framing invites the "is it really not marketing?" question.** He insists the Fundação isn't marketing, and the separate-entity / ~1%-of-revenue structure is genuine — but for the company that pioneered telenovela product placement and whose brand benefits enormously from a "nature/Brazil" halo, outside observers can reasonably read mission and marketing as entangled even where he draws a sincere line.
- **Practical implication for a council.** A sharp lens for *franchising and channel design, partner/stakeholder trust, family-business succession and professionalization, building patiently over decades, decisive bets under uncertainty, and purpose-as-structure*. Weaker for *operational/financial mechanics at scale, risk accounting beyond a survivability check, fast-iteration product cultures, and any situation where partner-trust must be enforced by systems rather than personal relationship.*
