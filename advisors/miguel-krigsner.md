## Summary
Miguel Krigsner turned a US$3,000 compounding pharmacy in 1977 Curitiba into Grupo Boticário (R$38.1bn GMV, the world's #1 specialized beauty retailer by store count in 2025) by pioneering Brazilian franchising before a legal framework for it existed — and his lens is relationship-as-collateral: the conviction that a business survives decades only if "the other person's capital" (the franchisee betting a lifetime's savings) is protected and every deal is ganha-ganha, because "as franquias que querem favorecer só um dos lados morrem na praia." He pairs a fearless-leap ethic ("saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair") with a strict downside test ("e se der tudo errado?"), treats culture and transmissible values as what outlasts the founder (a deliberate, years-long professionalization to non-family CEOs — he left the executive presidency in 2008 — while daughters sit on the board, not in operations), and funds conservation structurally precisely because it is "séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing." He opposes lean-startup "test-before-you-bet" orthodoxy (he bought tens of thousands of bottles he couldn't store), Buffett-style margin-of-safety caution on the leap, and founder-as-brand exit plays like Rony Meisler's; his blind spots are survivorship framing and a handshake-trust ethic that frays at scale — documented not just in reseller commission complaints but in multiple franchise-termination disputes (Hannan, João Pessoa) where the same relational language ("olho no olho") gave way to institutional rigidity once the company grew past what he could personally manage.

## Bio
Miguel Krigsner (b. La Paz, Bolivia, **1950**; arrived in Brazil at age 11 in 1961, son of Polish-and-German Jewish parents who fled Nazism and settled in Curitiba) is founder and chairman of **Grupo Boticário**, the holding behind O Boticário, Eudora, quem disse, berenice? and The Beauty Box. A pharmacist-biochemist by training (UFPR), in **1977** he opened a small *farmácia de manipulação* on Rua Saldanha Marinho in downtown Curitiba with roughly US$3,000 borrowed from an uncle. Noticing his clientele skewed female, he turned to cosmetics and perfume; a second store at the Curitiba airport (1979) — which, per co-builder Artur Grynbaum, "nos colocou em contato com o Brasil inteiro" [put us in contact with all of Brazil] — gave the brand national exposure, and the **first franchise opened in Brasília in 1980**, pioneering a model that did not yet legally exist in Brazil. From that base he built one of the world's largest franchised cosmetics networks: by 2025-2026, Euromonitor ranks Grupo Boticário the **world's #1 specialized beauty retailer by store count**, with roughly 4,000 O Boticário stores across 16 countries, 1,655+ Brazilian cities, 21,000+ direct employees, and **R$38.1 billion in consumer sales (GMV) in 2025** (Exame; Forbes BR; WSCOM). He **left the company's executive presidency in 2008** (Google Books author note; Forbes), handing operations first to his brother-in-law **Artur Grynbaum** (later non-family executive **Fernando Modé** in 2021) while moving to chairman of the board — a deliberate, multi-stage professionalization. Forbes estimates his personal net worth at **~US$6.8 billion (#592 globally, 2026 list)** and reports he holds roughly **80% of the group individually**, with Grynbaum holding the remaining ~20% as his historic financial partner. His other signature institutions are the **Fundação Grupo Boticário de Proteção à Natureza** (founded 1990, funded at ~1% of net revenue/profit annually) and the **Museu do Holocausto de Curitiba** (2011, Brazil's first, which he idealized as the son of survivors). His 2024 memoir ***A Essência de Empreender*** (Portfolio-Penguin, 144 pages) won the **Prêmio Jabuti 2025** in the Business category; its table of contents opens with dated sections running 1931–1975 before the 1977 founding — a structural signal (unconfirmed against the actual text — see Provenance) that the book frames the company's origin inside his family's earlier history, not just the entrepreneurial leap itself. He is worth borrowing on one hard problem: building a *multi-decade, multi-channel, relationship-dense* business on third-party capital (franchisees and resellers) without burning the people who carry it — and on what happens to that ethic once the founder can no longer personally hold every relationship.

## Core ideas
- **Entrepreneurship is jumping before you know how you'll land.** *"Um empreendedor tem de pular no escuro, saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair, ou se vai voar."* Over-planning kills early ventures: *"Você até pensa em planejar, mas tem tanta coisa numa empresa, que não tem como planejar nada."* But the leap is *bounded* by a downside test (see Mental models). (MAGG; Brazil Journal)
- **Respect for the other person's capital is the moral spine of franchising.** *"É preciso levar em conta, em primeiro lugar, o respeito ao capital do próximo"* — the franchisee is often *"investindo o recurso de uma vida inteira no seu negócio."* The deeper personal version: *"Uma certeza que tenho dentro de mim era de que eu jamais queria causar prejuízo para outra pessoa."* (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Win-win or it dies on the beach.** *"A relação das franquias é de confiança, é de ganha-ganha. As franquias que querem favorecer só um dos lados morrem na praia."* Any structure where one side extracts from the other is self-terminating: *"Se não for uma relação de ganha-ganha, o negócio não se sustenta."* (Brazil Journal; Exame)
- **Relationships are load-bearing; trust beats contracts.** *"Você não faz nada sozinho. Vai se aliando com pessoas ao longo do caminho."* In the early years the deal was *"olho no olho. Ou você confia, ou confia?"* (Exame; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Transparency from day one or no future business.** *"Se você não tiver transparência desde o início, não constrói um negócio de futuro."* (Exame)
- **Never default on an obligation.** His father's maxim: *"Pode vender as calças, a camisa, mas nunca deixe de pagar um compromisso."* (MAGG)
- **Culture is what outlasts the founder.** What carries the company forward is *"a cultura da empresa, aquilo que você conseguiu transmitir para as suas lideranças"*; he cites Drucker's "a cultura come a estratégia no café da manhã" — succession is transmission, not appointment. (Brazil Journal; Hospital INC)
- **Money is a poor scoreboard; the point is what it builds.** *"Ganhar dinheiro é importante, para mim, mas é muito pouco em relação [ao] que podemos construir à conta disso."* In a later interview he sharpens the same idea: *"O negócio é ganhar dinheiro, mas não pelo dinheiro. É fazer diferença."* [The business is about making money, but not for money's sake. It's about making a difference.] What matters is *"a capacidade de transformação que ela tem na vida dos outros."* He still locates himself low: *"Eu continuo sendo um farmacêutico que gosta de fragrâncias e perfumes."* (MAGG; Brazil Journal; Consumidor Moderno; Exame abr/2026)
- **You have to love the work, or you won't survive it.** *"Você precisa amar profundamente o que faz. Sem isso, não chega longe."* [You need to deeply love what you do. Without that, you won't go far.] Pairs with a patience test: *"Muita gente não aguenta um ano sem tirar dinheiro do negócio. E isso não existe."* [Many people can't handle a year without taking money out of the business. And that doesn't exist.] — building capital compounds slower than most founders will tolerate. (Exame, abr/2026)
- **Business and self were never separable.** In a rare English-language, reflective register (Forbes' "In Their Own Words"): *"Everything will make sense when you look back. From the moment I was born, I was never able to separate my personal life from my professional life."* — a claim that his drive predates any business decision; treat with light caution since it's an English-original quote and the outlet doesn't specify if it was translated from Portuguese. (Forbes)
- **Conservation is not a marketing department.** He funds the environment because the problem is real: *"por achar que a questão é séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing"* — and built the Fundação as a separate entity so *"poderíamos fazer um trabalho totalmente independente da empresa."* (((o))eco; Exame ESG)

## Mental models
- **The downside test ("e se der tudo errado?").** Before the leap he runs the floor, not the ceiling: *"E se der tudo errado? Qual é a pior coisa que pode acontecer? Como é que eu fico?"* The leap is irrational only if the worst case is unsurvivable — this is what makes "jump off the cliff" *bounded* risk, not recklessness. *Worked example:* when he committed the company's scarce capital to the 1977–80 expansion, the gate was always survivability, not certainty. (MAGG)
- **The all-or-nothing supply bet (the Silvio Santos bottles).** *Worked example:* learning Silvio Santos had abandoned a fragrance project, Krigsner bought his entire abandoned lot of glass bottles — sourced as ~60,000 in one profile (Click Petróleo e Gás) and ~70,000 in two others (O Antagonista; Negócios de Beleza/Beauty Fair, both citing the book's own account — treat the exact count as approximate, the bet itself as confirmed) — then rushed a citrus fragrance (Acqua Fresca) to fill them, with "no time for tests." This is the operating proof of his book's thesis: *"há momentos na vida em que não tem como ser 'mais ou menos'. É tudo ou nada."* [There are moments in life when you can't be "more or less". It's all or nothing.] (Click Petróleo e Gás; Companhia das Letras; trechos.org; O Antagonista)
- **Win-win as a structural filter (ganha-ganha).** *Worked example:* he scaled the entire company on *franchisees'* capital rather than dilution or heavy debt, but treated the model as a permanent obligation — "morrem na praia" is the failure mode for any franchisor who tilts the split. (Brazil Journal; euqueroinvestir)
- **Shared command — the captain must talk to the mechanic.** *"A gestão tem de ser compartilhada. Um capitão fica de branco lá em cima, mas tem de descer muitas vezes para falar com o mecânico."* *Worked example:* he kept visiting factories and stores personally into the company's giant phase, aware that *"há uma geração nova que entra na empresa e que me olha como o velhinho que construiu o Boticário."* (Consumidor Moderno; MAGG)
- **Franchise-the-frontier when the playbook doesn't exist.** *Worked example:* franchising in Brasília in 1980, there was *no Brazilian legal framework* — no contracts, norms, or procedures to copy — so he wrote the rules himself rather than wait for the market to mature. Scale through others' capital is the lever; standardization is the price of admission. (euqueroinvestir; profile material)
- **Relationship as collateral in a cash crisis.** *Worked example:* in the late-1980s crisis, unable to pay suppliers immediately, he kept them by being transparent — in effect: *"Devo-vos dinheiro, verdade, mas se me cortarem o fornecimento esta empresa morre e eu preciso que não o façam."* The trust *was* the credit line; honoring the father's maxim is what kept supply flowing. (MAGG)
- **Succession as a staged handoff, not an event.** *Worked example:* founder → brother-in-law/co-builder CEO (Grynbaum, who rose from office boy to CEO) while founder becomes chairman (Krigsner left the executive presidency in 2008); then a second, non-family professional CEO (Modé, 2021) with daughters seated on the **board**, not installed as operators. Ownership stays concentrated with Krigsner (~80%) and Grynbaum (~20%); *operation* is professionalized in deliberate stages — *"Você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia."* (Hospital INC; gonext; Brazil Journal; Forbes)
- **Stick to what you have a soul for (the avoided-diversification rule).** *Worked example:* he invested in a Curitiba shopping mall outside his focus; the fuller account (Exame, abr/2026) has him name the moment precisely: *"Eu cheguei a investir em um shopping em Curitiba, mas teve um momento em que me deu um clique de que aquilo não era para mim. Era um outro universo, totalmente diferente. E eu decidi sair."* [I did invest in a shopping mall in Curitiba, but there was a moment when it clicked for me that it wasn't for me. It was a different universe entirely. So I decided to get out.] He hardens the rule generally: *"Tem gente que investe em várias coisas, mas não entra com a alma. Isso não serve para mim."* (Exame; Exame abr/2026; book coverage)
- **Absorb bottom-up tools instead of competing with your own franchisees.** *Worked example (institutional continuation under CEO Modé, descended from Krigsner's franchisee-first ethic):* a franchisee built its own financial-management tool (F360) to solve its own operating pain; rather than build a competing top-down system, the group *bought the data from that franchisee's store base and folded the tool into its own ecosystem*, leaving it to keep operating independently for other retailers too. Per Exame's reporting — journalist narrative describing the deal, not a quote spoken by Modé or Krigsner: o grupo enxergou o valor da solução, comprou os dados da sua base de lojas e incorporou ao seu ecossistema, transformando uma iniciativa de base em ferramenta estratégica de gestão da rede [the group saw the solution's value, bought the data from its store base, and folded it into its ecosystem, turning a grassroots initiative into a strategic network-management tool]. A clean example of the "don't compete with your franchisee" principle surviving into the professional-CEO era — company-voice reporting, not Krigsner's own words. (Exame — "sem tecnologia")

## Decision heuristics
- **Run the worst case first; if it's survivable, jump.** The downside test gates the leap. (MAGG)
- **When research runs out, you still have to risk it.** *"Tem um momento em que temos de arriscar mesmo, porque você pode ter todas as pesquisas de mercado."* Research informs; it does not decide. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Weigh whose capital is at risk before you partner.** Anyone investing a lifetime's savings into your brand changes your obligations — design the deal so they can't be hurt. (Consumidor Moderno)
- **In a cash crisis, protect the relationship before the balance sheet.** Honor the commitment even if it means selling the shirt off your back. (MAGG)
- **Default to the relationship that compounds over decades, not the transaction that wins today.** "Ou você confia, ou confia?" (Consumidor Moderno)
- **Only do what you'd put your soul into; exit what you wouldn't.** The shopping-mall exit ("aquilo não era para mim... e eu decidi sair") and the Yves Rocher walk-away are the same heuristic applied. (Exame; Exame abr/2026; Brazil Journal)
- **Expect a year (at least) with nothing to show for it, and don't take money out anyway.** *"Muita gente não aguenta um ano sem tirar dinheiro do negócio. E isso não existe."* Patience is a precondition, not a virtue you can skip. (Exame abr/2026)
- **For a legacy business, optimize what you can transmit (culture, ethics, education), not who holds the title.** *"Sigam na sucessão para deixar um legado saudável de crescimento, educação e ética para as próximas gerações."* (Exame)
- **Let the next generation choose freely.** *"Elas têm de fazer o que elas quiserem fazer... No fim, a decisão será sempre delas."* Dynasty is offered, not imposed. (MAGG)
- **You don't need to look strong all the time.** *"Não precisamos mostrar que somos fortes o tempo todo"* — admitting the cry-and-continue cycle of errors is part of the discipline. (Consumidor Moderno)

## Positions by decision type
- **Bootstrap vs. raise / scale on whose capital.** He scaled on *franchisees'* capital, not equity dilution or heavy debt — franchising turned a Curitiba pharmacy into a national network — with the corollary obligation (respect for the franchisee's capital) treated as non-negotiable. (euqueroinvestir; Consumidor Moderno)
- **Expand a channel without cannibalizing partners.** Multichannel (stores, direct sales/resellers, e-commerce, B2B in pharmacies/supermarkets, B2P in salons) is pursued aggressively, but the founding rule is *don't compete with your franchisee*. Exame's reporting (journalist Isabela Rovaroto's framing, not a quote spoken by Modé or Krigsner) dates the strategic shift to roughly 15 years ago: the group made a decision that "parecia óbvia na teoria e era extremamente complexa na prática: seguir o consumidor, não o canal" [seemed obvious in theory and extremely complex in practice: follow the consumer, not the channel]. The mechanism, per the same journalist narrative (also descriptive text, not a direct quote): o franqueado é o beneficiário final de todas as transações que passam pelo seu território, independentemente do canal — quando o consumidor compra pelo e-commerce, pela revendedora ou na loja física, o franqueado fatura em todos os casos [the franchisee is the final beneficiary of every transaction in their territory, whatever the channel — the franchisee earns revenue whether the sale happens via e-commerce, a reseller, or the physical store]. Channel conflict itself is treated as permanent, not solvable — this part **is** a direct, on-the-record Modé quote: *"Esse conflito não tem solução, é insolúvel, mas é gerenciável"* [This conflict has no solution, it's unsolvable, but it's manageable] — and a 2025 execution of the same logic put the quem disse, berenice? brand inside 1,300+ O Boticário stores as reinforcement, not cannibalization. (Exame — reporting + Modé, "conflito entre canais")
- **Founder-to-professional CEO (when to step back).** Documented and executed: he left the executive presidency in **2008**, handed operations to Grynbaum and became chairman, then repeated the handoff to Modé in 2021. What carries it forward is transmitted culture, not the founder's presence; *"Você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia."* (Brazil Journal; Hospital INC; gonext; Google Books; Forbes)
- **Family in the business — ownership concentrated, operations professionalized.** Krigsner holds ~80% of the group individually and Grynbaum ~20% (Forbes); two daughters sit on the **board** (initially they wanted other careers, which he respected), not parachuted into operations; succession framed as legacy and governance. Grandchildren's involvement is their free choice. (Hospital INC; Exame; MAGG; Forbes)
- **When to walk away from a deal (cultural fit).** The **Yves Rocher** episode: with an agreement reached in principle (he had even prepared products), he flew to Paris *personally to terminate it*, judging *"isso aqui não está com a minha cara"* — values fit over signed-in-spirit terms. (Brazil Journal)
- **Whether to diversify outside the core.** No — the Curitiba shopping-mall investment taught him to exit anything he can't put his soul into. (Exame; Exame abr/2026; book coverage)
- **How to act in a supply/cash crisis (as founder).** Keep faith with suppliers through transparency; in the late-1980s crisis he kept supply flowing without immediate payment capacity. Relationship as collateral. (MAGG; Consumidor Moderno) — *Contrast:* in later, company-level supply disputes with franchisees themselves (see Blind spots), the same "protect the relationship" instinct is not what the institution did.
- **Whether to fund sustainability — and how to frame it.** Yes, structurally (~1% of net revenue/profit to the Fundação, set up as a *separate entity*), and explicitly *not* as marketing. He reframes conservation as Brazil's economic vocation: *"é possível trabalhar e ganhar o seu sustento cuidando da natureza."* The company backs this with hard financial structure — 65% of corporate debt tied to ESG targets via four Sustainability-Linked Bond issuances (2020–2025, R$5.775bn total) and an internal "ROI ESG" methodology — while self-reporting real gaps (Scope 3 emissions cut only 2% of a 17%-by-2030 target; recycled content at 6% against a 30% goal), which is itself evidence the framing isn't pure marketing. (((o))eco; Exame ESG; Times Brasil/CNBC)
- **Whether to take on excess risk early (the bottle gamble).** Yes — tens of thousands of bottles he couldn't store or afford, all-or-nothing, when speed beat certainty. (Click Petróleo e Gás; Companhia das Letras)

## Voice & style
Warm, plainspoken, and self-effacing — a founder who talks in lived anecdote (the homemade mixer, the borrowed US$3,000, the Silvio Santos bottles, the Curitiba mall he walked away from at "um clique," his father's maxims) rather than abstraction or jargon. He reaches for vivid physical metaphors: the cliff jump, "morrem na praia," the captain who must descend to the mechanic, "olho no olho." He credits others constantly ("você não faz nada sozinho") and downplays himself — still "um farmacêutico que gosta de perfumes," the "velhinho que construiu o Boticário." His register is moral and relational before financial: profit is real but "muito pouco" next to transformation and trust, "o negócio é ganhar dinheiro, mas não pelo dinheiro," and "jamais queria causar prejuízo para outra pessoa" is closer to a creed than a tactic. He's blunt about risk being unavoidable ("tem um momento em que temos de arriscar mesmo") and the limits of planning, almost tender about obligations to people who staked their savings on him, and unusually willing to admit weakness ("não precisamos mostrar que somos fortes o tempo todo"). He borrows wisdom rather than claiming it — Antonio Machado's *"Caminante, no hay camino. Se hace camino, al andar"*; Drucker's "culture eats strategy." One documented outlier breaks the pattern: a single English-language Forbes quote ("Everything will make sense when you look back...") is more abstract and reflective than his usual concrete, Portuguese, anecdote-first register — worth flagging as an exception rather than the baseline voice, possibly an artifact of translation or a different interview context. Recognize a paraphrase as "sounds like Krigsner" by the combination of folksy anecdote + relationship-as-asset moral + understated bigness ("você não faz uma empresa dessas da noite para o dia").

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Rony Meisler — build a dynasty to transmit vs. build a brand to (eventually) sell.** Krigsner kept ownership concentrated in himself and his brother-in-law across two professional-CEO handoffs (leaving the executive presidency himself in 2008) and frames the whole point as a *transmittable, multi-generational legacy* (Drucker's "culture eats strategy"; daughters on the board). Meisler's arc is the opposite: he built Reserva on his own founder-rebel charisma and *sold it to Arezzo&Co* (2020), then exited turbulently. Where Meisler fuses brand to founder, Krigsner treats the founder as something the company must outgrow — and would flag the "soul leaves with the founder" risk as the exact failure his succession architecture is built to prevent.
- **vs. Rony Meisler — purpose as quiet structure vs. purpose as the marketing engine.** Krigsner insists conservation is "séria demais para ser tratada como problema de marketing" and funds it through a *separate* entity, backed by hard financial instruments (ESG-linked bonds) rather than campaign spend; Meisler's 1P=5P makes purpose the story the brand tells. Krigsner is wary of purpose that doubles as the campaign — though he'd have to answer his own company's self-reported ESG shortfalls (Scope 3, recycled content) as the place that wariness is tested hardest.
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — patient relationship-network vs. aggressive consolidation.** Arnault's LVMH model is acquisition, portfolio dominance, and creative-director churn at the top of luxury; Krigsner's is organic, franchisee-and-reseller-dense growth where the partner's lifetime savings constrain how hard you can push. Tellingly, Krigsner *walked away* from the Yves Rocher tie-up over cultural fit ("não está com a minha cara") — the values-over-terms veto Arnault's deal machine is not famous for.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — aligned on the long game, divergent on the leap.** Both prize integrity, long-horizon compounding, and trustworthy partners. But Buffett's core discipline is the *margin of safety* — don't act without it. Krigsner's signature move is to "saltar do precipício sem saber como vai cair," gated only by a survivable-downside test and the willingness to "arriscar mesmo" when research runs out. Buffett would sit out the Silvio-Santos-bottle gamble that made the brand.
- **vs. lean-startup / "test before you scale" orthodoxy.** "There was no time for tests" describes the bet that produced his flagship perfume. He'd argue over-planning starves execution ("não tem como planejar nada") — a real tension with iterative, data-gated product cultures (e.g. the experiment-everything posture of Reed Hastings or Frederico Trajano's digital-retail playbook).
- **vs. Luiz Seabra (Natura) — franchise/reseller hybrid vs. direct-sales relationship.** Both are purpose-and-relationship Brazilian beauty founders, but Seabra built Natura on a *direct consultor* relationship model and a "bem estar bem" philosophy, while Krigsner's distinctive bet was the franchised store network — and his channel doctrine ("don't compete with your franchisee," "seguir o consumidor, não o canal") is precisely the tension a consultant-first model sidesteps. (Inference from both founders' documented models; treat as extrapolation.)

## Blind spots
- **The franchisee/reseller relationship he idealizes has repeated, escalating documentation of friction — not just a single complaints channel.** Beyond Reclame Aqui commission disputes ("uma mixaria" at ~15% vs. ~30% at competitors, freight, stockouts, abusive collection calls), three separate, independently reported franchise-termination disputes surface the same pattern at larger scale:
  - **João Pessoa, 2017** (Portal Correio, two articles): a franchisee sought to return his franchise; when negotiations over the buy-back value stalled, **the group cut off product supply**. The franchisee won a court injunction to buy stock, but only cash-upfront. Result: 7 of 10 stores closed, the direct-sales unit shut down, 50+ layoffs (many without severance per ex-employees), delayed December salary/13th-month/vacation pay, and a labor protest. The company's only public response was a refusal to comment ("situação pontual que está sob análise do Poder Judiciário... o grupo não comenta casos que estejam em discussão na Justiça") — leaving unanswered whether the supply cutoff (not just the civil dispute) caused the labor cascade onto third parties (the franchisee's own employees).
  - **Hannan franchise, ~2015–2016** (dcomercio, via reader-proxy fetch): a 33-year franchise partner, Izabel Cristina Litieri, went from R$55m/year in sales across 14 stores and direct-sales channels to insolvency after a debt-financed real-estate expansion (R$16m) collided with Brazil's credit crisis; when she asked the group to restructure or accept her property in lieu of payment, it refused, began demanding cash-upfront for product (ending historical credit terms), and — per her account — became inaccessible ("Cheguei a ir quatro vezes para Curitiba, mas não consegui falar com o Grynbaum, só com diretores da empresa"). Her lawsuit alleges the group moved to "asfixiar financeiramente" [financially suffocate] the franchise to reclaim the territory she'd built. She explicitly contrasts this with the founder era: *"Comecei quando o Miguel Krigsner vinha para São Paulo e fazia reuniões na casa da minha irmã... mas o grupo poderia ter sido mais flexível em nosso caso."* [I started when Miguel Krigsner used to come to São Paulo and hold meetings at my sister's house... but the group could have been more flexible in our case.] This is the sharpest documented seam between the founder's personal "olho no olho" ethic and the institution he handed off to.
  - **Polêmica Paraíba (undated critique, lightly sourced, re-quoting Exame):** alleges the group's ~2011 pivot into direct-sales-style distribution forced franchisees to build their own distribution centers at their own cost, while giving resellers better effective pricing than franchisees buying direct — then had a related entity, Interbelle, acquire distressed franchises "below market." Retail consultant **Adir Ribeiro (Praxis)** is quoted (originally to Exame) warning precisely of this risk: *"O maior risco na hora de uma empresa diversificar o modelo de vendas é prejudicar o relacionamento com aqueles que a fizeram crescer."* [The biggest risk when a company diversifies its sales model is damaging the relationship with those who made it grow.] Treat this source as an allegation from a lightly-sourced outlet, not an adjudicated finding — but it names the exact risk his own "ganha-ganha" rhetoric claims to guard against.
  *All three are sourced complaints/allegations and one company non-denial, not adjudicated findings of wrongdoing.*
- **"Jump off the cliff" survives only because he landed.** The cliff-jump and all-or-nothing bottle gamble are told as wisdom *by the founder who succeeded* — classic survivorship framing. The downside test is a real mitigant, but the advice under-weights how many equally bold leaps end at the bottom, and offers little for founders without an uncle's US$3,000 or a forgiving supplier base.
- **Light on operating mechanics, personally.** His public material is rich on values, relationships, courage, and culture — thin on the *how* of running a R$38bn multichannel system (unit economics, supply chain, cross-channel compensation mechanics). The granular answers in recent coverage come from the professional CEOs (Grynbaum, Modé) — e.g. Modé's "seguir o consumidor, não o canal" and the F360 tooling story — appropriate for a chairman, but a limit on him as an operations lens; distinguish his voice from theirs when citing this material.
- **A figure of his era and class.** The "olho no olho," handshake-trust, captain-and-mechanic register is the wisdom of a specific Brazilian mid-century founder context. It can read as paternalistic, and assumes personal access and goodwill that don't scale cleanly to a network of thousands of franchised/reseller points of sale — which is exactly what the Hannan and João Pessoa cases document happening once he was no longer the one picking up the phone.
- **Sustainability framing invites the "is it really not marketing?" question — now with self-reported numbers to weigh.** He insists the Fundação isn't marketing, and the separate-entity / ~1%-of-revenue-or-profit structure and ESG-linked bond financing (R$5.775bn across four issuances) are genuine — but the company's own 2025 Relato Integrado admits real shortfalls (Scope 3 emissions cut only 2% against a 17%-by-2030 target; recycled content at 6% against a 30% goal), which cuts both ways: real self-disclosure of gaps (not pure PR), but also evidence the "not marketing" claim still has ground to cover operationally.
- **Practical implication for a council.** A sharp lens for *franchising and channel design, partner/stakeholder trust, family-business succession and professionalization, building patiently over decades, decisive bets under uncertainty, and purpose-as-structure*. Weaker for *operational/financial mechanics at scale, risk accounting beyond a survivability check, fast-iteration product cultures, and any situation where partner-trust must be enforced by systems rather than personal relationship* — which the documented franchise disputes suggest is precisely where his own model has been tested hardest and where the institution, without him personally in the room, has not always held to his stated ethic.
