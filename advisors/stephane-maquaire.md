## Summary
Stéphane Maquaire is the French executive who has run Carrefour Brasil (B3: CRFB3) since September 2021 — arriving straight from turning around Carrefour Argentina's hyperinflation-era operation into a country that handed him, within months, the R$7.5 billion Grupo BIG acquisition (40,000 new employees, 374 stores), then a pandemic-scale digital-acceleration mandate, then the unresolved aftermath of the November 2020 killing of João Alberto "Beto" Freitas — a Black man beaten to death by outsourced security at a Carrefour store in Porto Alegre, ten months before Maquaire's own arrival. His sharpest documented instinct is procedural rather than ideological: split simultaneous crises into separate workstreams ("poucas rotinas," a named anti-bureaucracy heuristic even at 130,000 employees), work the commercial and institutional channels of a crisis in parallel, and reverse a costly integration bet (keeping all acquired banners) once eighteen months of data said otherwise — closing 150 stores to consolidate around Atacadão, Carrefour, and Sam's Club. He is a useful lens on wholesale/atacarejo portfolio strategy, capital-light franchise expansion, and disaster-response logistics (the May 2024 Rio Grande do Sul floods, where Carrefour froze prices statewide and deliberately sourced flood donations from *outside* the affected state to avoid depleting local shelves). He is a considerably weaker lens on personal crisis accountability: across every source in this corpus, his own public language about the Porto Alegre killing softened over time — from calling it directly "a tragédia de Porto Alegre" in his first Brazilian interview (2022) to an unnamed, victim-less "episódio grave" three years later (2025) — while three fresh racism incidents surfaced in a single week in April 2023, eighteen months into his tenure, and the case remains without a trial date five years on. He also inherited, and has not personally addressed on the record, a 2025 French-parent-led delisting of the Brazilian subsidiary that two independent proxy advisors and multiple sell-side banks said undervalued minority shareholders — a governance pattern in which the hardest questions are consistently left to corporate statements and third-party analysts rather than his own voice.

## Bio
Stéphane Maquaire is a French national who joined the Carrefour group globally in 2019, took his first international post as CEO of Carrefour Argentina (2019–2021) — where he ran a digital-transformation plan while managing runaway inflation — and arrived in Brazil as CEO of Carrefour Brasil in September 2021, becoming the first Carrefour Brasil CEO from outside the market to lead the post-BIG-acquisition era. By his own account, he spent "anos e anos" resisting international mobility, telling his wife for two decades that leaving France would "nunca, nunca vai acontecer" [never, never going to happen], before eventually working across Germany, Argentina, and Brazil and having to learn German, Spanish, and Portuguese along the way (YouTube, CNN Brasil "É Negócio"). He insisted on conducting his first Brazilian press interview (Exame, April 2022) in Portuguese, already fluent at the time. His tenure has been defined by three overlapping, self-described "grandes desafios" [major challenges]: absorbing Grupo BIG (ex-Walmart Brasil, owner of Sam's Club) for R$7.5 billion — 40,000 new employees onto a base of ~100,000, 374 stores, later reduced to three consolidated banners after 150 store closures; accelerating e-commerce, which he says grew fivefold since his 2021 arrival to 10% of sales; and managing the reputational fallout of the November 2020 killing of João Alberto "Beto" Freitas, which predates his appointment by roughly ten months but which he has led the company through for the entirety of its unresolved legal aftermath (Experience Club, 2025). He has also overseen the RS flood crisis response of May 2024, a 2025 debt-deleveraging push via real-estate sale-and-leaseback, and — as of early 2025 — the French parent's move to take Carrefour Brasil private, delisting it from B3's Novo Mercado.

## Core ideas
The convictions restated across the corpus; situational applications are in *Positions by decision type*, citable lines in *Signature quotes*.

- **Growth breeds bureaucracy, and bureaucracy must be actively fought, even at 130,000 employees.** "Um espírito de startup é um espírito de ficar muito ágil, não ter muitos controles, test and learn muito — [é] o que nós estamos [tentando] para manter na nossa empresa." [A startup spirit is a spirit of staying very agile, not having a lot of controls, testing and learning a lot — that's what we're trying to maintain in our company.] He names the failure mode explicitly: scale produces "linhas de produtos, business units, CEOs que estão funcionando de forma separada," which then requires "outra organização transversal para cuidar, olhar e controlar" — a self-defeating cycle. (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **A crisis has two tracks — the commercial relationship and the institutional/state relationship — and both must be worked simultaneously.** On the 2024 Bompard/Mercosul-beef statement crisis: "Lidamos com essa crise de porte relevante nos aproximando de nossos parceiros frigoríficos para ouvir as dores que criamos com o comunicado. Também ficamos perto do governo brasileiro, através de seu embaixador." [We handled this crisis of relevant scale by getting close to our meatpacking partners to hear the pain we caused with the statement. We also stayed close to the Brazilian government, through its ambassador.] (InfoMoney, 10/12/2024)
- **Leadership requires visible presence and personal ownership of unpopular calls.** "Um líder tem que ser presente. Não é possível liderar de 'fora'." [A leader has to be present. You can't lead from 'outside'.] On closing the "Todo dia" chain: "não foi uma decisão fácil... mas tínhamos que fazer, porque era a decisão correta. Era a minha responsabilidade." [it wasn't an easy decision... but we had to do it, because it was the right call. It was my responsibility.] (InfoMoney, 10/12/2024)
- **The wholesale/atacarejo format has two distinct customers, not one.** "Temos que lembrar que [o atacarejo] tem dois clientes: o comerciante e o consumidor, e cada vez mais consumidor [vai] para [lá]." [We have to remember that wholesale has two customers: the small merchant and the end consumer, and increasingly the consumer is going there too.] (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **Retail economics are inherently thin-margin, high-volume — say so plainly.** "Varejo é um negócio de muito volume e de margem de taxa muito pequena." [Retail is a business of a lot of volume and a very small margin rate.] (LÍDER.INC/LIDE, 13/06/2024)
- **Don't let a single political statement redirect a multi-decade investment thesis.** Responding to Economy Minister Paulo Guedes telling a Brasília business audience that France was irrelevant to Brazil: "Com a relevância que temos, não podemos fazer qualquer coisa porque uma pessoa disse algo. Estamos há 47 anos aqui, e não é uma frase que vai mudar tudo." [With the relevance we have, we can't just do anything because one person said something. We've been here 47 years, and it's not one sentence that's going to change everything.] (Mercado&Consumo, 17/08/2022)
- **Political neutrality in an election year, stated in advance.** "Vamos seguir atendendo os nossos clientes qualquer que seja a decisão do povo brasileiro." [We will keep serving our customers whatever the Brazilian people decide.] (Mercado&Consumo, 17/08/2022)
- **Ecosystem cross-subsidy: a healthy financial arm buffers retail margin pressure.** "Temos um ecossistema robusto que nos traz vantagens. Se o banco vai bem, conseguimos ter oportunidades no varejo, e assim por diante." [We have a robust ecosystem that gives us advantages. If the bank does well, we get opportunities in retail, and so on.] (Exame, 14/04/2022)
- **Transparency is the primary crisis-leadership doctrine, because scale makes crises inevitable.** "A transparência... é a principal forma de lidar com situações que fogem do controle," tied explicitly to ~2 million daily customers across stores and digital channels, which is why leadership must be prepared to respond "com agilidade." (Experience Club, 06/05/2025)

## Mental models
Named, reusable frameworks, each with the situation where he applied it.

- **"Poucas rotinas" (few routines) — anti-bureaucracy antidote to post-M&A scale.** Explicit framework offered in answer to an entrepreneur's question about preserving startup culture while growing: "eu diria poucas rotinas, por favor, pouca organização... obviamente uma empresa precisa de rotinas e organização, mas não organizar demais." [I'd say few routines, please, little organization... obviously a company needs routines and organization, but not too much.] Worked example: he frames the entire post-BIG "simplification phase" (2024) as an application of this — reducing redundant banners and converting stores into the two winning formats (Atacadão and Sam's Club) rather than layering new cross-cutting controls onto the merged organization. (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **Organizational separation under simultaneous-crisis load.** He kept the digital-acceleration team structurally apart from the BIG-integration team, calling the choice "acertada" [correct] specifically because running two high-complexity transformations through the same leadership bandwidth was a risk he wanted to avoid. Worked example: digital sales scaled 5x since 2021 to 10% of revenue while, in parallel, the BIG-integration/store-closure decisions ran through a separate stream. (Experience Club, 06/05/2025)
- **Store-format segmentation by social class, treated as a complementary portfolio, not a single optimized banner.** Atacadão covers classes C/D, the classic Carrefour hypermarket covers class B, and Sam's Club (retained from the BIG deal) opens access to classes A/AB — "Esse formato tem alguns elementos únicos, como os produtos importados e exclusivos para os sócios. E nos dá acesso a clientes das classes A e AB. Até então, nós cobríamos mais as classes C e D, com o Atacadão, e B, com o hipermercado." Worked example: of 374 acquired BIG stores, dozens were converted to hypermercados Carrefour, others to Sam's Club (target 60 units, from 2.1 million to 3 million members), and Maxxi-banner stores to Atacadão. (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023; Mercado&Consumo, 17/08/2022)
- **"Maturidade em três anos" (three-year maturity curve) for converted stores.** Explicit ramp model: any store recently converted between banners needs roughly three years to reach the target format's full sales potential — used to explain why the "100%-complete" integration milestone (129 stores converted, R$1.9bi capex) didn't mean immediate capture of the R$2 billion synergy target. "Precisamos de três anos para que essas lojas atinjam uma maturidade. A boa notícia é que ganhamos seis meses nessa jornada." (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023)
- **Sale-and-leaseback as deleveraging without selling the underlying business.** Rather than cutting costs to reduce ~2.4x net-debt/EBITDA leverage post-BIG, he opened a parallel lever: monetize the group's owned real estate (Carrefour Brasil owns "muitas das nossas lojas") via partnership, alongside organic ramp-up cash flow. Worked example: the Barzel Properties deal (R$1.3 billion, five distribution centers plus five stores) — "Além do sale and leaseback, temos o ramp up das lojas convertidas e o fluxo de caixa que vamos gerar com essa base." (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023)
- **Three-type conversion taxonomy as a live earnings-call communication tool.** When Q1-2023 results disappointed analysts, he segmented outcomes rather than owning a blanket miss: "Quando falamos em conversões de mesmo formato, temos três tipos de conversões: atacarejo para atacarejo, varejo para varejo e varejo para atacarejo. Nesse último tipo, de hipermercado para Atacadão, vemos uma melhora forte das vendas." — isolating the one conversion type performing well from the two performing below plan. (InfoMoney, 03/05/2023)
- **"Combo" co-located store format for capital-efficient expansion.** Two banners (e.g., Sam's Club plus Atacadão, or Sam's Club plus Carrefour) share one lot, splitting land, energy, and tax costs — "Há muitos ativos em que podemos dividir custos como energia, estacionamento e impostos." Worked example: 10 combo units running by mid-2024, explicitly used to accelerate Sam's Club's rollout with less capex per new location. (NeoFeed, 26/05/2024)
- **Own-brand price freeze as a portable, but not copy-pasted, inflation tool.** Carried over from his Argentina posting but explicitly adapted, not transplanted: "Passar de 2% a 10% de inflação pode ser uma dificuldade para os clientes tanto quanto passar de 20% a 50%, como vimos na Argentina... Mas a Argentina é outro país e tem outra cultura, não podemos pensar da mesma forma." Worked example: Brazil's own-brand prices frozen 4 November–10 January (specific, bounded dates), contributing to two years of 50% own-brand sales growth. (Exame, 14/04/2022)
- **Representation-depth framework for DEI — headcount diversity is not leadership diversity.** "Não basta, por exemplo, contratar negros; eles precisam estar em todos os níveis hierárquicos da companhia." [It's not enough, for example, to hire Black people; they need to be at every hierarchical level of the company.] Worked example, self-reported: 61% of the 100,000-person workforce self-declared Black or Brown in 2021, versus 54.7% of leadership — cited as evidence of a narrowing but persisting representation gap, alongside a forward commitment of ≥50% Black hires. (Exame, 14/04/2022) — *see Blind spots* for the tension between this self-assessment and the Coalizão Negra Por Direitos's rejection of the underlying remediation package as "compliance theater."

## Decision heuristics
How he actually decides under uncertainty — what he does first, what he minimizes.

- **In a supply panic, communicate to prevent panic-buying, freeze prices in the affected zone for a bounded window, and source aid from *outside* the crisis zone so as never to deplete local shelves.** During the May 2024 RS floods: "a primeira medida que tomamos é congelar todos os nossos preços... durante todo o mês de maio," paired with a direct customer ask — "estamos pedindo aos nossos [clientes], por favor, limitem a compra adicional de arroz" — and 500 tonnes of donated supply deliberately sourced "com abastecimento fora do Estado porque não queríamos... ter o risco de desabastecer as nossas lojas." He generalizes the panic-psychology pattern cross-nationally: "isso aqui, como em todos os países que eu trabalhei, esse sentimento de pânico vai muito rápido... na França, por exemplo... os franceses compram muito açúcar." (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **When pressed publicly to name a career mistake, redirect from the corporate thesis to a personal story.** Asked on live TV about mistakes in the BIG integration, he pivoted to a decades-long personal resistance to international mobility instead — a deliberate choice not to expose fragility in the investment thesis he was actively selling to the same audience. (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **When leverage draws scrutiny, minimize rather than concede stress — even as the number moves against you.** In June 2023, with net debt at R$13.8bn and leverage at 2.44x: "isso não tira o nosso sono" [it doesn't cost us any sleep]. A month earlier, in the same quarter's earnings call, leverage had actually printed at 3.1x net-debt/EBITDA — a figure four independent sell-side banks (JPMorgan, Bradesco BBI, Genial, Itaú BBA) flagged as a genuine risk the same week. A year later, describing the same metric on camera, he rounds it down again: "um pouco mais de duas vezes o nosso EBITDA... é sustentável esse endividamento." (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023; InfoMoney, 03/05/2023; CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **Don't sell a strategic asset under time pressure just to relieve a balance-sheet headline.** On the real-estate carve-out during the same debt scrutiny: "não temos urgência" — explicitly refusing to accelerate a sale-and-leaseback deal merely because the market was anxious about leverage. (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023)
- **Face a direct competitor's land-grab with patience and execution detail, not a price war.** With Assaí opening 240 stores in twelve months, his response was schedule, not discount: "Não tenho dúvida de que vamos conquistar mais clientes com essas novas lojas. Mas precisamos de tempo e vai ser um trabalho de detalhe para consolidar essas novas operações. E a concorrência terá o mesmo desafio." (NeoFeed, 30/06/2023)
- **When a governance move (delisting) draws hard questions, frame it as a time-horizon shift, not a strategy change, and don't engage the harder specifics.** On the 2025 go-private process: "[O fechamento de capital] pode agilizar nossos planos de expansão para os próximos anos, mas não estou vendo uma mudança muito forte [na estratégia]." He attributes any change to the parent's longer time horizon replacing "public-company short-termism," while leaving the minority-shareholder-value questions entirely to sell-side commentary (BTG, Santander, Itaú BBA) rather than addressing them himself. (Seu Dinheiro, 19/02/2025)
- **Precommit publicly to a dated milestone for a hard integration call, then use that date as the accountability anchor.** "Estou vendo um período de 18 meses para fazer todo esse trabalho e poder dizer, em uma conferência de imprensa em fevereiro de 2024: integração está já atrás de nós." (ISTOÉ/Reuters, 07/06/2022)

## Positions by decision type

- **Pivot vs. persevere (post-BIG multi-banner strategy).** Initial decision: preserve every acquired brand to minimize disruption. Reversed at end of 2023 once integration data was in: "Foi uma decisão bem difícil de tomar. Fechamos 150 lojas e decidimos manter apenas as marcas Carrefour, Atacadão e Sam's Club." He names this explicitly as the hardest of his three "grandes desafios." (Experience Club, 06/05/2025)
- **When to close a store chain / own the call personally.** Closing the underperforming "Todo dia" banner: framed not as a portfolio-optimization footnote but as a personally-owned, painful decision — "nunca é [fácil], para um varejista, tomar uma decisão como essas — mas tínhamos que fazer, porque era a decisão correta. Era a minha responsabilidade." (InfoMoney, 10/12/2024)
- **Pricing under inflation.** A combined toolkit rather than a single lever: own-brand price freezes (bounded dates), smaller pack sizes for lower-frequency shoppers, direct supplier renegotiation, and in-house interest-free financing via the group's bank — "como há um banco dentro do Carrefour Brasil, temos a oportunidade de apoiar a venda financiada, parcelando em três vezes sem juros." (Mercado&Consumo, 17/08/2022)
- **List vs. delist — the 2025 go-private decision.** Carrefour S.A. (France), already the ~70% controlling shareholder, moved in February 2025 to absorb Carrefour Brasil's remaining minority stake and delist it from B3's Novo Mercado, exiting the CVM's "A" listing category. Maquaire's own framing treats it as continuity, not a strategic pivot (see *Decision heuristics*); the harder governance question — whether the offer (three exchange classes, a 32.4% premium over prior-month VWAP) fairly priced minorities out — was contested by Glass Lewis and ISS (both recommended voting AGAINST) and by Santander (R$10 target vs. a ~R$7.70 cash option), with a Península stake reduction from 7.3% to 4.9% just below the disclosure threshold, timed ahead of the vote, flagged by Santander analysts themselves as likely coordinated to improve the odds of the controller's offer passing. Maquaire is not on record engaging any of this substance directly. (Money Times, 03/04/2025; Seu Dinheiro, 19/02/2025)
- **Focus vs. diversify — banner portfolio by income segment.** Deliberately multi-banner by design (Atacadão/C-D, hipermercado/B, Sam's Club/A-AB) rather than optimizing one format; a contrarian bet on hypermarkets specifically, doubling down on the "proximity hypermarket" format in interior cities as digital-fulfillment infrastructure at the same time competitor Grupo Pão de Açúcar was closing its Extra hypermarket chain. (Exame, 14/04/2022)
- **Crisis response — natural disaster (RS floods, May 2024).** Statewide price freeze on all products/banners for the month of May; explicit customer messaging to prevent rice-panic-buying; 500 tonnes of donated goods sourced from outside RS to protect local supply; a store in the São Leopoldo/RS region converted into a shelter housing 700+ displaced people. (CNN Brasil/YouTube, 26/05/2024)
- **Crisis response — racialized violence (the Beto Freitas killing and its aftermath).** Institutional response inherited and continued: a 2021 Termo de Ajustamento de Conduta (TAC) worth R$115 million (per the Coalizão Negra Por Direitos's own figure) signed with MPRS/MPF/MPT/DPE-RS/DPU and civil-society groups, an External Diversity and Inclusion Committee, a R$144 million fund (created 2020, predating his arrival) for employability/education/anti-racism programs, and expanded "racial-literacy" training reaching 130,000 employees. In his most candid on-record moment (his first Brazilian interview, April 2022), he named it directly in his own answer — "Depois de fortes ondas de covid-19, e da tragédia de Porto Alegre, tivemos meses duros" — and admitted "ficamos com uma imagem machucada" [we were left with a damaged image]. - **Crisis response — supply-chain/ESG scandal (Amazon-deforestation beef).** After a May 2022 exposé linking two JBS meatpacking plants (Vilhena, Pimenta Bueno) to illegal cattle grazing inside the Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau indigenous territory, Carrefour announced blocking purchases from those two plants — but Repórter Brasil's own tracking found product from those plants' output still reaching Carrefour shelves in Pernambuco and São Paulo months later (July and November 2022). The company's stated policy (satellite monitoring, direct-farm auditing since 2016) does not cover indirect/intermediary suppliers, the layer where the investigation found the actual laundering ("gado lavado") occurring. No personal Maquaire statement appears in this source; the company's defense is institutional. (Repórter Brasil, 14/02/2023)
- **Governance reform after a landmark acquisition.** Board expanded from 10 to 13 seats (3 independent) alongside the BIG deal's close; Carrefour S.A. CEO Alexandre Bompard became Brazilian-board chairman, Abilio Diniz vice-chairman; Cade approved the ~R$7.5bn deal conditioned on divesting 14 of 374 acquired stores. Maquaire treated the divestment as a minor, parallel workstream rather than a gating issue. (ISTOÉ/Reuters, 07/06/2022)
- **Disclosure under a material, unexplained price adjustment.** When a ~R$1 billion downward price adjustment on the R$7.5bn BIG deal was announced (April 2023) with no detail on the underlying seller obligations that triggered it, four sell-side banks (JPMorgan, Itaú BBA, Goldman Sachs, XP) all flagged the same problem — insufficient disclosure, added noise about the acquired operations' true condition — concurrent with the Banco Carrefour CEO's own departure. No Maquaire quote or personal explanation appears in this source; the silence itself, letting the market absorb the news via press release, is a recurring pattern in this corpus. (InfoMoney, 12/04/2023)

## Voice & style
Diplomatic and process-oriented, even under direct challenge: he answers hostile or skeptical questions (a minister's insult, a shareholder-value critique, a leverage worry) by widening the frame — company tenure ("47 anos"), a metric he can round favorably ("um pouco mais de duas vezes"), or a procedural description of what the organization is doing — rather than by directly rebutting the premise or naming an adversary. He never attacks a named critic, analyst, or politician in his own quoted words, even when the interviewer's question does (Paulo Guedes, sell-side banks, proxy advisors are referenced by journalists, not contested by him directly). He is comfortable with personal, humanizing color — two decades of resisting a move abroad, learning three languages, a first triathlon at his daughters' request — used to soften an otherwise data-and-synergy-heavy register (leverage ratios, EBITDA, capex, store counts). His crisis vocabulary is consistently procedural and calm: "agilidade," "transparência," "test and learn," "poucas rotinas" — startup/consulting-adjacent terms applied to a 130,000-employee, R$115-billion-revenue company. He is willing to name a hard number when it's favorable (5x e-commerce growth, 3 million Sam's Club members) but reaches for euphemism or omission on the hardest number or name: leverage is minimized even as it moves the wrong way, and the one proper noun that would anchor the Porto Alegre story — João Alberto Freitas's name — never appears in his own quoted words anywhere in this corpus; only the place name does, and only in 2022 ("a tragédia de Porto Alegre"), after which he has no on-record first-person reference to the killing at all — by 2025 the "episódio grave / crise de imagem" framing belongs to the Experience Club recap's own narration, not to a Maquaire quote. His Portuguese, per YouTube auto-captions, carries a detectable French accent that ASR tools mis-transcribe (his own name rendered "Stefane maer"), consistent with a leader who insists on speaking the local language directly rather than through an interpreter, even years into the role.

## Where they disagree
How Maquaire's documented positions diverge from other named advisors in this corpus. (Simulation from public material on both sides.)

- **vs. Bernard Arnault (LVMH) — what a controlling shareholder owes minority investors.** Arnault, also running a French-controlled, family-majority conglomerate, argues explicitly that staying *publicly listed while family-controlled* is a deliberate discipline, not a tolerated cost: "Vous avez ainsi plus de responsabilité par rapport à votre marché et même vis-à-vis de votre management. Vous êtes constamment obligés de vous remettre en question" [You have more accountability to your market and to your own management. You're constantly forced to question yourself] — a private company, in his telling, lets leaders dodge exactly that discipline. Maquaire's own French parent did the opposite in Brazil: in 2025 it moved to *delist* Carrefour Brasil precisely to escape public-market short-termism, at a price two independent proxy advisors (Glass Lewis, ISS) and Santander argued undervalued minorities near the stock's historical lows — and Maquaire, as the CEO of the entity being taken private, offered no personal defense of the pricing, leaving that fight to sell-side analysts. Where Arnault treats public accountability as a feature worth keeping even with majority control, the Carrefour Brasil case shows the opposite instinct exercised on a subsidiary: strip the accountability once control is secure enough to do so cheaply. (Money Times, 03/04/2025; cf. Bernard Arnault dossier, *Decision heuristics*)
- **vs. Frederico Trajano (Magalu) — founder/controller governance vs. an installed expatriate executive answering to a distant controller.** Trajano argues explicitly that founder- or controller-led companies outperform those run by hired "executivos do mercado," because founders "têm foco no longo prazo" — a thesis he embodies as third-generation family leadership. Maquaire is the live counter-case Trajano's thesis warns about: an installed executive (not a founder, not a family member) running a subsidiary whose every major capital-structure decision (the BIG deal's financing, the 2025 delisting) is set by a distant controlling parent in France, with the local CEO's public role largely limited to explaining continuity rather than shaping the structure. Both executives, notably, respond to weak-quarter skepticism the same way — externalizing to macro conditions rather than owning execution — but Trajano's version comes with an ownership stake in the long-term outcome that Maquaire's does not.
- **vs. Paulo Corrêa (C&A) — naming the adversary.** Both CEOs consistently attribute soft results to macro conditions rather than execution, and both reach for a favorable, rounded framing of a worrying metric rather than a direct concession. But Corrêa, when publicly challenged on Shein/Shopee, engages competitors by name at length across multiple interviews ("Por que as condições são diferentes?"), campaigning openly for regulatory parity. Maquaire's corpus shows no equivalent instance: he does not name a single competitor, critic, analyst, or politician adversarially in his own quoted words anywhere in these twenty-one sources — even the minister who insulted France, and even the sell-side banks who called his leverage a real risk, remain unnamed by him personally, referenced only by journalists. (cf. Paulo Corrêa dossier, *Core ideas* — "compete, but insist the rules be the same for everyone")

## Blind spots
Where the corpus itself, or named critics, documents a gap between Maquaire's narrative and outcomes — including the controversy this dossier was explicitly asked not to soften.

- **The Beto Freitas killing, its unresolved five-year aftermath, and a softening personal narrative.** João Alberto Silveira Freitas, a Black man, was tortured and beaten to death by outsourced security guards (Grupo Vector) at a Carrefour store in the Passo D'Areia neighborhood of **Porto Alegre**, Rio Grande do Sul, on 19 November 2020 — ten months before Maquaire's arrival as CEO (September 2021), under predecessor Noël Prioux. The killing triggered nationwide protests and comparisons to George Floyd's death the same year. Five years on (as of a November 2025 CNN Brasil retrospective), no trial date has been set for the six defendants; all were released from preventive custody in December 2024 on grounds of excessive detention length; and the Public Prosecutor's Office is still fighting, at the STJ, to restore "racism" as an aggravating factor after Rio Grande do Sul's appellate court (TJRS) stripped it — a removal that contradicted both the original police investigation and the indictment. The victim's widow's lawyer voices the family's explicit fear that slow process combined with news-cycle turnover will erode public memory before trial. Maquaire's own public language about the episode has documented a clear softening arc — one that is specifically about *his own quoted words*, not about how the outlets narrated it. In his first Brazilian interview (April 2022) he named it directly, unprompted, inside his own answer: "Depois de fortes ondas de covid-19, e da tragédia de Porto Alegre, tivemos meses duros," and admitted "ficamos com uma imagem machucada." By a May 2025 friendly executive-network recap (Experience Club), he is no longer quoted on the killing at all — his own on-record words in that piece are confined to strategy, e-commerce growth and "transparência." The Experience Club article does *not* euphemize the death in its own prose (its narration states plainly that the episode "culminou na morte de um homem negro" involving outsourced security, and frames Maquaire's task as "a gestão de uma crise de imagem, após um episódio grave"); the point is the reverse — the outlet supplies the gravity the CEO's own 2025 quotes no longer do. Across all twenty-one sources, the one proper noun that would anchor the story — João Alberto Freitas's name — never appears in a single quote attributed to Maquaire; only the place name does, and only in 2022. Company-level responses (the 2021 TAC, external diversity committee, R$144M fund, expanded training) are real and substantive, but the Coalizão Negra Por Direitos — in the note that gave this dossier its clearest adversarial source — rejected the entire remediation package as "compliance theater": "o que na verdade se caracterizou como uma estratégia de compliance empresarial, com a triste participação de organizações e lideranças negras a endossar a narrativa da empresa racista," and objected that none of its three demanded pillars (criminal/civil accountability, direct family dialogue and reparation, territorial reparation) were honored in the eventual settlement. Individual criminal exposure fell entirely on frontline employees and an outsourced contractor — no executive faced charges. (Coalizão Negra Por Direitos, 18/06/2021; CNN Brasil, 20/11/2025; Exame, 14/04/2022; Experience Club, 06/05/2025)
- **A documented, recurring institutional pattern predating the fatal case — including a genuine Osasco incident, distinct from the 2020 killing.** Two independent sources — the Coalizão's own note and Brasil de Fato's contemporaneous "sete vezes em que o Carrefour atuou com descaso e violência" (20/11/2020) — name the same prior racialized-violence incidents at Carrefour Brazil stores before the fatal 2020 case. In **August 2009**, roughly five outsourced security guards beat Januário Alves de Santana (then 39, a security-technician) at the **Osasco (SP)** unit on Avenida dos Autonomistas after mistaking him for a car thief as he opened the door of his own EcoSport — he was taken to a back room, punched, kicked and pistol-whipped while hearing racial slurs, left with a broken face and teeth; Carrefour later fired those involved, cut the security contractor, and paid an indemnity. In **October 2018**, security beat Luís Carlos Gomes at a São Bernardo do Campo (SP) store over an alleged open beer can. The Coalizão's assessment, verbatim: "A situação de Beto não se tratou de exceção. violência racial é regra na rede de supermercados Carrefour." — *Note on the task brief's "2020 Osasco incident":* every source in this corpus consistently and specifically places the *fatal* 2020 killing in **Porto Alegre**, not Osasco; Osasco is both the site of the real 2009 (non-fatal) incident above and separately the location of Carrefour Brasil's corporate headquarters, which is the likely source of the conflation. This dossier uses "Porto Alegre" for the 2020 killing and documents the real, distinct 2009 Osasco incident here — corroborated across two live sources rather than resting on a single note — instead of either fabricating a false 2020-Osasco narrative or silently dropping the genuine controversy the brief was pointing at. (Coalizão Negra Por Direitos, 18/06/2021; Brasil de Fato, 20/11/2020)
- **Three fresh racism incidents in a single week, eighteen months into his tenure.** In April 2023 (7–11 April), three separate incidents surfaced within days of each other: a Black professor, Isabel Oliveira, was followed by security and stripped to her underwear at a Curitiba Atacadão to "prove" she wasn't stealing; a Black man, Vinícius de Paula (husband of Olympic volleyball champion Fabiana Claudino), says he was barred from a "caixa preferencial" at a Carrefour Alphaville store while a white customer who didn't qualify used it unchallenged; and Pedro Freire alleges he was forced to empty his bag by security at an Olinda (PE) Atacadão. President Lula personally condemned the Curitiba case in a cabinet meeting: "O Carrefour cometeu mais um crime de racismo... É a segunda vez que o Carrefour faz isso... nesse país aqui não vamos admitir o racismo que essa gente tenta impor ao Brasil." Carrefour's written response denied any wrongdoing ("não identificou indícios de abordagem indevida") while reasserting a "tolerância zero" policy — a denial-plus-policy-restatement pattern, not a personal response from Maquaire. (Brasil de Fato, 11/04/2023 and 22/04/2023)
- **A supply-chain ESG gap between stated policy and actual practice.** Despite a socio-environmental beef-sourcing policy dating to 2016 (satellite monitoring, direct-farm audits), Repórter Brasil documented Carrefour stores nationwide selling beef traceable to Amazon-deforestation zones and indigenous land (Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau territory), including from JBS plants Carrefour claimed to have blocked in May 2022 — product from those same plants kept reaching shelves in July and November that year. Mighty Earth estimated deforestation linked to Carrefour's beef sales at up to 50,000 hectares in 2022. Idec's Rafael Arantes: "A partir do momento que [os varejistas] comercializam produtos, é de responsabilidade deles procurarem e pressionar os seus fornecedores por maior transparência" — an implicit rebuke of the direct-farm-only monitoring approach, since the investigation's core finding was laundering through untracked intermediary suppliers. No Maquaire quote addresses this directly. (Repórter Brasil, 14/02/2023)
- **A pattern of personal silence on the hardest governance and disclosure questions.** Across three separate episodes — the ~R$1 billion unexplained BIG-deal price adjustment (April 2023, concurrent with the Banco Carrefour CEO's departure), the 3.1x-leverage quarter that triggered a 9.4% single-day stock drop and convergent criticism from JPMorgan, Bradesco BBI, Genial, and Itaú BBA (May 2023), and the 2025 delisting's minority-shareholder pricing dispute — Maquaire's own quoted commentary is thin and generic ("agilidade," "não estou vendo uma mudança muito forte") while the substantive critical scrutiny comes entirely from third parties. He does not appear, in any of these twenty-one sources, to personally and directly engage the harder critical questions in his own words; corporate statements or analyst commentary consistently carry that weight instead.
- **Implication for a board.** A strong lens for *disaster-response logistics and price-freeze discipline under supply panic, multi-banner post-M&A portfolio segmentation, capital-light franchise and combo-store expansion, and running simultaneous transformations through deliberately separated organizational tracks.* A weak lens for *personal accountability under reputational crisis (his own language about Porto Alegre demonstrably softened rather than sharpened over time), transparency toward minority shareholders under a controlling-parent's governance moves, supply-chain traceability enforcement beyond stated policy, and direct personal engagement with hostile analyst or civil-society critique.*
