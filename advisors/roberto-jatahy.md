## Summary
Roberto Jatahy is the co-founder of Animale (with his sister Claudia Jatahy) who built Grupo Soma into Brazil's dominant multi-brand fashion platform — Farm, Animale, NV, Maria Filó, Cris Barros, and (2021) Hering — on a doctrine he calls the **"aceleradora de marcas"** (brand accelerator): buy brands with an "alma," pay partly in equity to keep founders as partners, touch the invisible back-office first, and explicitly refuse layoffs or organic in-house brand creation ("a gente não acredita" in building from scratch). He is patient and discipline-obsessed on paper — idle cash over a rushed deal, a ~20% portfolio-overlap ceiling, years-long "courtships" before a deal closes — yet the 2024 merger of Soma with Arezzo&Co into **Azzas 2154** put that autonomy-preserving philosophy under real stress: Jatahy became the subordinate co-founder (Chief Brand Officer, not CEO) alongside the more centralizing Alexandre Birman, and by 2026 had to seek a court injunction against his own partner just to keep his own position and portfolio intact. His sharpest tension as an advisor is between the M&A gospel he preaches (preserve founders' autonomy, never impose a boss) and the diagnosis several market sources level at him directly: **"o Roberto não quer ter chefe."** Best read against Alexandre Birman (centralization vs. autonomy, the same merger from the other side), Bernard Arnault (discreet, non-luxury roll-up vs. LVMH's aggressive luxury exposure), and Rony Meisler (the founder whose brand's fate hung on exactly the tension Jatahy fought to control).

## Bio
Roberto Luiz Jatahy Gonçalves (b. 1969) studied economics at PUC-Rio (Robb Report cites Universidade Cândido Mendes instead — a discrepancy the corpus does not resolve) while, per EY's account, splitting his time with running the family's cattle ranch. In 1991 he co-founded Animale with his sister Claudia Jatahy, investing what savings he had as a student (per BoF and ELLE; EY's own podcast page instead dates Animale's founding to 1999 — a second, unresolved discrepancy). He led Animale's 2010 merger with Farm Rio (founded 1997 by Kátia Barros and Marcello Bastos) to create Grupo Soma, becoming Diretor-Presidente — though the EY podcast page and a low-confidence YouTube transcript both put the group's formal "surgimento" closer to 2009. He took Soma public in July 2020 (proceeds reported as R$1.3bn contemporaneously by Exame and R$1.8bn in a later Bloomberg Línea retrospective — unreconciled), made ByNV his first post-IPO acquisition that October, then in April 2021 won a bidding contest against Arezzo for **Hering** (R$5.1bn, 30% cash / 70% stock, Cade-approved), calling the deal "na verdade uma fusão" given the two companies' relative size by then. He was named to the Business of Fashion 500 in 2023 (per BoF and ELLE, the only Brazilian on that year's list) and was EY's Entrepreneur of the Year Brazil in 2020. In February 2024 he engineered — alongside Alexandre Birman — the merger of Grupo Soma into Arezzo&Co, forming **Azzas 2154**; Birman became CEO of the combined company and Jatahy Chief Brand Officer, a demotion from the top chair he had held at Soma since 2010. In August 2025 he restructured again, ceding day-to-day operational command of the women's-fashion vertical to Ruy Kameyama ("A empresa ficou grande e não dá para um só fazer... eu realmente sozinho não consigo" — NeoFeed) to focus on brand/M&A strategy. That peace proved short-lived: by March 2025 investors were already calling the relationship with Birman a "bomba-relógio," a June 2025 "bandeira branca" reconciliation (mediated first by chairman Pedro Parente, then by Nicola Calicchio Neto) held less than a year, Kameyama resigned in April 2026, and in May 2026 Jatahy filed a pre-arbitral injunction against Birman's unilateral reorganization of the men's/women's fashion units — a judge froze the reorg and kept Jatahy in place, but the litigation (and dueling CAM-B3 arbitration filings) was ongoing as of this writing, coinciding with a >60% cumulative decline in Azzas 2154's share price since the 2024 merger. His judgment is worth borrowing specifically on **multi-brand portfolio construction, M&A discipline and sizing, and founder-preserving integration** — and, more uncomfortably, as a live case study in what happens when that same philosophy has to operate on him, not just through him.

## Core ideas
- **Fashion can be run for profit, not just for love of the craft.** His entry line to the business press, months after the IPO: "O negócio tem de ser bonito, bacana, mas primeiro tem de ser rentável." [The business has to be beautiful, cool — but first it has to be profitable.] He frames the "first wave" of Brazilian fashion-group consolidation (implicitly InBrands, Restoque) as failing because financial managers didn't understand fashion and creative founders confused vanity with brand preservation — Soma's edge, in his telling, is closing that gap. (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- **Soma is a brand accelerator, not a holding company or a private-equity shop.** "O Soma tem como conceito ser uma aceleradora de marcas: está no nosso DNA melhorar o reconhecimento, a receita e o lucro das nossas empresas." [Soma's concept is to be a brand accelerator: it's in our DNA to improve the recognition, revenue, and profit of our companies.] (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021) — the explicit contrast is with financial-engineering roll-ups that extract cost synergies instead of building the asset.
- **A brand is an extension of a creative founder's soul — you cannot manufacture that in-house.** Per an XP equity-research paraphrase of his own framing: "a criação é uma extensão de alma e criação das criadoras" [creation is an extension of the soul and creativity of the (female) creators/founders] — the stated reason Soma has never successfully launched an organic brand and instead only acquires. A separately-sourced (low-confidence ASR) YouTube interview has him saying nearly the same thing more bluntly: the group has tried building brands "do zero"/organically and "a gente não acredita" that path works. (XP equity research, abr/2021; YouTube/XP Expert, abr/2021 — ASR, treat as corroborating paraphrase, not verbatim)
- **Acquisition means sociedade (partnership), not subsidiary absorption.** Deals are structured partly in cash, partly in Soma stock, so founders remain economic partners: "As marcas que entram aqui não são subsidiárias de Farm, de Animale; fazem parte de uma sociedade. Não é à toa que o nome é Soma." [The brands that join here aren't subsidiaries of Farm or Animale; they're part of a partnership. It's no accident the name is Soma (= "sum").] (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **Integration order: invisible first, visible last, and never with a cost-cutting agenda.** "Normalmente, em uma aquisição, a primeira coisa que fazemos é trazer todo mundo da companhia. Áreas financeira, tributária, toda a contabilidade... Não interferimos em faixa de preço, modelo de negócios, canais de venda." [Normally, in an acquisition, the first thing we do is bring in everyone from the company — finance, tax, all the accounting... We don't interfere with price range, business model, sales channels.] He is explicit that this can be slow: "muitas vezes os resultados não aparecem em seis meses." (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **Discipline over market pressure — cash sitting idle beats a rushed deal.** "Não pretendemos ceder às pressões do mercado por aquisições desestruturadas... Prefiro ter 600 milhões, 700 milhões de reais parados a sair correndo para queimar caixa." [We don't intend to give in to market pressure for unstructured acquisitions... I'd rather have R$600-700 million sitting idle than rush out and burn cash.] (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- **ESG as a survival thesis, not a compliance line item.** Per the XP report's rendering of his position: "a empresa que não tem [agenda ESG], está fadada a acabar" [a company without an ESG agenda is doomed to end] — paired with "ESG não é só uma agenda importante para a companhia, como também é bom para o coração" [ESG isn't just an important agenda for the company — it's also good for the heart]. (XP equity research, abr/2021)

## Mental models
- **"Aceleradora de marcas" (brand accelerator).** Core operating identity: buy an already-strong, founder-led brand and systematically improve its recognition, revenue and profit — not a financial holding, not a cost-cutting PE-style rollup.
  - *Worked example:* the Hering acquisition (R$5.1bn, 2021) — explicit no-layoffs commitment, a planned "repaginação completa" over 30-36 months built on megastores plus cross-brand collabs (Fábula, Cris Barros, NV). "Não existe agenda de cortes e demissões. Nossa forma de fazer negócio é olhar as empresas adquiridas, entender a gestão e aproveitá-la." [There's no agenda of cuts and layoffs. Our way of doing business is to look at the acquired companies, understand their management, and make use of it.] (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- **Sweet-spot M&A sizing: ~R$300M revenue, scalable to R$700M-1bn.** A named, numeric acquisition filter, repeated near-verbatim across two years of interviews.
  - *Worked example:* "Uma marca muito pequena, até pelo nosso tamanho atual, acabaria ficando desprivilegiada dentro do nosso portfólio. O nosso sweet spot é pensar em negócios com receita ao redor de R$ 300 milhões, que a possamos levar para R$ 700 milhões, R$ 1 bilhão." [A brand that's too small would end up under-served in our portfolio. Our sweet spot is businesses with revenue around R$300M that we can take to R$700M-1bn.] Explicitly aimed at "achar a próxima NV" (finding the next NV, the group's highest-margin digital-native brand). (Exame INSIGHT, ago/2022)
- **Portfolio overlap ceiling (~20%).** A hard exclusion filter for M&A targets: no more than roughly 20% category/price/aesthetic overlap with an existing brand.
  - *Worked example:* used to justify pursuing a lower-priced women's-fashion brand to sit beneath Animale/A.Brand/Cris Barros in the price ladder — "Não haverá canibalização entre marcas, todas serão de classes, estéticas ou preços diferentes, com um máximo de 20% de interseção entre as que já existem." [There won't be cannibalization between brands — they'll all be different classes, aesthetics or prices, with a maximum 20% overlap with what already exists.] (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- **Visão matricial de portfólio (price × segment matrix).** Portfolio gaps are read off a two-axis map — fashion category/style against price point — and M&A targets are chosen to fill specific empty cells, not opportunistically.
  - *Worked example:* per the XP equity research note, he used this matrix to identify a fitness/athleisure gap and a mid-price feminine-fashion gap between Farm and Maria Filó — the latter reportedly aimed at the brand Shoulder, with unconsummated talks dating to 2015. (XP equity research, abr/2021)
- **Creative succession as a 3-5-year engineered process, not a sudden replacement — "contaminação da essência da marca."** Transitioning a brand's creative leadership is treated as a deliberate, multi-year handoff using "brand books" and other tools, never an abrupt swap.
  - *Worked example:* Farm and Animale are cited repeatedly as completed successful transitions, Maria Filó as in-progress: "O maior propósito do Soma é perenizar as suas marcas. E isso passa efetivamente por uma sucessão criativa." [Soma's greatest purpose is to make its brands last. And that genuinely requires creative succession.] (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **Multicanal as "no such thing as channels" — a single pooled operation.** Physical stores, digital, and multibrand wholesale are treated as one integrated stock/commission pool, not competing P&Ls, specifically to eliminate stockouts (which he calls the industry's biggest margin-destroyer).
  - *Worked example:* the XP equity-research note (higher confidence) records him reframing the model as "um canal só: o multicanal" — physical, digital and multibrand are not distinct channels but one, with all stock pooled expressly to end "a perda da venda por não ter um tamanho ou cor," and it captures his shorthand for the phone as a storefront: "A loja que está no seu bolso." The (low-confidence ASR) YouTube interview corroborates the mechanics — sellers paid the same commission regardless of which channel closes the sale, a multibrand partner able to draw on nearly all group stock. Corroborated again in the Hering context: "será mantida a rede de franqueados e multimarca ativa, com limpeza de conflitos que surgem quando se opera no digital, no físico e com lojas próprias." (XP equity research, abr/2021; Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021; YouTube — ASR, ancillary only)
- **Two-sided leadership split: "gestão de varejo" vs. "criativo."** As the group scaled past what one founder could run, he split the CEO function itself along a retail-management axis (delegated to a professional operator) and a brand-creative/expansion axis (kept for himself as CBO).
  - *Worked example:* the August 2025 reorg installed Ruy Kameyama (ex-CEO of BR Malls, a real-estate operator) as CEO of the unified fashion-and-lifestyle unit while Jatahy became Chief Brand Officer across all 15+ brands. "A empresa ficou grande e não dá para um só fazer. Tem que ter esses dois lados: um lado de inteligência de gestão de varejo e um lado mais criativo." [The company got big and one person alone can't do it. You need both sides: retail-management intelligence and a more creative side.] (NeoFeed, ago/2025)
- **"O rio corre pro mar" — complementarity as destiny.** A river-to-the-sea metaphor for framing a large, asymmetric merger as natural and inevitable rather than a hostile takeover.
  - *Worked example:* at the Feb-2024 announcement of the Arezzo-Soma merger, sharing the stage with Birman: "Nesse caso, é como costuma se dizer, o rio corre pro mar. Nossa complementaridade é muito grande e há um alinhamento muito grande entre mim e o Birman." [In this case, as they say, the river flows to the sea. Our complementarity is huge and there's a lot of alignment between me and Birman.] The same appearance shows him trading away his investor-relations role to Birman explicitly to free up personal time for M&A — a pattern he would repeat with Kameyama in 2025. (NeoFeed, fev/2024)
- **Long courtship as a de-risking discipline for M&A.** Every named acquisition target in the corpus was preceded by years of intermittent, unhurried conversation before signing — treated as the actual risk-mitigation mechanism, not diligence checklists.
  - *Worked example:* on Hering — "Nos conhecemos em 2013... Em 2015, a Hering estava interessada no Soma... Retomamos após o IPO... Fomos surpreendidos pela proposta não solicitada pela Hering." [We met in 2013... In 2015, Hering was interested in Soma... We resumed after the IPO... We were surprised by Hering's unsolicited proposal.] The same courtship pattern is cited (lower-confidence ASR) for Shein and a target referred to as "Shoulder," both still unconsummated years later. (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021; YouTube — ASR, corroborating pattern only)
- **Collection risk is engineered down with data + scored "test runway shows," not left to one creative's intuition.** He treats collection risk — which pieces to make, at what production grade — as the fashion sector's single biggest operational hazard, sharper still in women's fashion, and claims a specific remedy: algorithms and market research combined with small *desfiles-teste* (test runway shows) staged for a panel of professionals and multibrand buyers who score each piece; the production grade is then set by that score, and the panel itself is refreshed by which evaluators scored most accurately in prior collections.
  - *Worked example:* per the XP equity-research note, this scoring-and-feedback loop — not a single creative director's intuition — is what he volunteered as the group's operating "secret" for what XP's analysts flag as the sector's "maior dor de cabeça." It is the operational complement to the "aceleradora" thesis: creativity stays with the founders, but the bet-sizing on each collection is systematized and data-fed. (XP equity research, abr/2021)

## Decision heuristics
- Judge whether a deal was "too expensive" by subsequent execution, not by the announced price: "Não acho que tenha sido alto... o que determina isso vai ser a execução desse projeto." (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- Gate large M&A on macro visibility (interest rates), not just target attractiveness — the group publicly paused new deals in 2022-23 pending "melhor visibilidade" on rates. (Exame INSIGHT, ago/2022)
- Prefer holding idle cash to a forced deal under market pressure to "deploy capital." (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- Root, unsentimentally, for weaker fragmented competitors to struggle in downturns, since it thins the target pool toward him: "torço para que elas estejam perto de mim." (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- On integration, prioritize revenue/operating-result growth over cost synergies as the definition of a successful deal. (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- Run the accelerator as a two-way gate, not a hoard: brands that don't earn their place get discontinued or sold back, not carried indefinitely — even mid-turmoil the group closed Alme, Dzarm, Reserva Simples and Troc and resold Baw to its founders. This is the under-stated flip side of the "aceleradora de marcas" doctrine: it prunes as well as acquires. (Central do Varejo, mar/2025)
- Deliberately under-communicate upside pre-IPO/pre-milestone to avoid setting an expectation that later disappoints: withheld the Farm-US growth story from the 2020 IPO roadshow specifically "para não gerar expectativa e frustração no mercado." (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- When steamrolled by a partner's unilateral decision inside a shared structure, escalate to courts/arbitration rather than accept the fait accompli — his own 2026 move against Birman. (Mercado&Consumo, mai/2026; Money Times, mai/2026)
- On a genuine, irreconcilable governance deadlock, prefer structural separation (cisão) to indefinite forced cohabitation — reported willingness to accept a split of Azzas 2154 that Birman resisted. (NeoFeed, "como a briga... pode levar o Azzas à cisão", mai/2026)
- When confronted publicly with a hard question (the Animale labor-conditions scandal), lead with an emotional/ownership register ("um filho," "dor") before citing an operational fix, rather than leading with the numbers. (Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")

## Positions by decision type
- **Build vs. buy (new brands).** Buy, never build organically — "a gente não acredita" that an internally-fabricated brand can work; every brand in the portfolio came from an external founder/creative director whose "alma" was preserved. (XP equity research; YouTube — ASR corroboration)
- **Pricing an acquisition / was it too expensive.** Anchor on pre-deal fair value and defer the real verdict to execution, not the announcement-day price reaction: on Hering, "Era o preço de valor de mercado antes da pandemia... o tempo vai dizer." (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- **Post-acquisition integration / cost cutting.** Explicitly refuses a layoffs-and-synergy-extraction playbook; the group's own CFO later defended a R$2.9bn Hering write-down as tax-driven and "sem efeito caixa," not an admission the deal itself was wrong. (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021; Exame INSIGHT, mar/2024)
- **Internationalization.** Skeptical almost to the point of dismissive as a deliberate strategy — "só uma [marca brasileira] efetivamente tem um negócio robusto internacional, a Havaianas" — and frames Farm's US success as an unplanned surprise he didn't engineer, deliberately kept out of the IPO narrative to manage expectations. (Mercado&Consumo/InfoMoney, jul/2021)
- **Focus vs. diversify (category adjacency).** Firm limits: no luxury ("Não somos uma plataforma de luxo"), no beauty/decor beyond collabs, no footwear/accessories M&A because the supply chain know-how doesn't transfer ("A cadeia de fornecimento é diferente"). (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **Talent: promote from within vs. hire outside.** Strong default to internal promotion; outside executive hires are named exceptions, not policy (Nelson Camargo at Farm, Isabel Del Priore at Animale). (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **Scaling personal control.** When the company outgrew what one founder could run, he ceded operational command rather than trying to do more himself — twice: giving Birman the investor-relations interface in the 2024 merger announcement, then giving Kameyama the retail-operations chair in 2025 — in both cases explicitly to free himself for brand/M&A work. (NeoFeed, fev/2024; NeoFeed, ago/2025)
- **Partner conflict resolution under a shared/merged structure.** Escalate to the judiciary and arbitration when a co-founder unilaterally reorganizes a unit he controls, rather than accept it or negotiate informally first — the May 2026 injunction against Birman. (Mercado&Consumo, mai/2026)
- **Debt.** Aversion to leverage as a pre-condition for further M&A — "A gente não gosta de trabalhar com dívida," paired with the CFO's "cash is king" framing of the same discipline. (Exame INSIGHT, ago/2022)

## Voice & style
Jatahy talks in relational, almost familial registers even about hard financial mechanics — brands have an "alma," acquisitions are a "sociedade," a brand is "um filho," a big merger is a river finding the sea. He is fluent and confident narrating M&A history in long, sequential, date-by-date form ("Nos conhecemos em 2013... Em 2015... Retomamos após o IPO...") — a storyteller's cadence that turns a negotiation into a courtship narrative, not a transaction log. He is comfortable admitting personal limitation in a way many founder-CEOs aren't ("eu realmente sozinho não consigo"), and he reaches for consensus-and-alignment language reflexively when describing partnerships, even ones that later collapsed publicly ("alinhamento muito grande," "nem eu nem ele estávamos certos"). When pressed on a governance or labor controversy, his instinct is to answer in an emotional/ownership register before — sometimes instead of — an operational one. He is markedly more guarded and diplomatic discussing Alexandre Birman than discussing anyone else in the corpus, consistently downplaying friction in the moment ("nunca houve nada sério," "houve um exagero") in ways later reporting and his own subsequent litigation contradict — a pattern worth flagging to a reader rather than taking at face value in real time.

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Alexandre Birman — does founder autonomy survive inside a merger of equals?** The best-documented, highest-stakes disagreement in the corpus, because they built and then fractured Azzas 2154 together. Jatahy's declared model is decentralized: preserve each brand's "alma," don't impose a boss, let creative directors run their own operation once the invisible back-office is unified. Birman's model, per outside reporting picked up by both dossiers, is more centralizing — "de um lado, Alexandre Birman sempre foi associado a uma gestão mais disciplinada... do outro, Roberto Jatahy construiu reputação ligada à criatividade, branding e construção cultural de marcas" (Mercado&Consumo, mai/2026). When Birman unilaterally moved to reorganize the men's/women's units in 2026, Jatahy did not negotiate — he sued, framing it as protecting ~R$116-200M in already-realized integration efficiency, and a judge sided with him on an injunction. Jatahy optimizes for the founder/brand-side of the platform; Birman optimizes for centralized operating control. See Birman's own dossier for the mirror-image account.
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — discreet, non-luxury roll-up vs. LVMH's aggressive, exposed luxury consolidation.** Jatahy explicitly rejects the luxury frame for Soma ("Não somos uma plataforma de luxo") and is described by Exame's journalists as a deliberately "discreet executive" who "prefere mostrar a prometer" — implicitly contrasted in the same profile with Arnault's "agressividade nas aquisições e exposição na mídia." Where Arnault protects scarcity, price, and maison prestige at nearly any cost, Jatahy's stated model spans multiple price points and explicitly avoids categories (luxury, beauty, footwear/accessories) outside his group's operating know-how. (Exame, "Estilo bilionário")
- **vs. Rony Meisler — the founder whose autonomy Jatahy's philosophy was supposed to protect.** Meisler's Reserva became part of the Azzas 2154 group via the Arezzo side of the 2024 merger, then was caught directly in the Birman-Jatahy reorganization fight — the very brand Jatahy's May-2026 injunction fought to keep under continuity rather than see reassigned. Meisler and Reserva's other founders exited the company during the integration turmoil regardless. The disagreement is less about a stated position and more a structural one: Jatahy's public doctrine says a roll-up should let the founder's "alma" run the brand, but even the executive publicly committed to that doctrine could not, in practice, prevent Reserva's founding team from leaving once centralizing pressure from the other side of the merger took hold. See Meisler's own dossier for the founder-side account of that same integration.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — shared instincts on cash discipline, opposite instincts on integration.** Both prize idle cash over a forced deal and distrust debt-fueled growth: Jatahy's "prefiro ter 600, 700 milhões parados" echoes Buffett's fortress-balance-sheet doctrine. But Buffett's model is to buy rarely and then leave existing management almost entirely alone; Jatahy's model runs a much higher deal cadence (Hering, ByNV, NV, and a standing "sweet spot" numeric filter) and is far more actively involved in post-deal brand strategy (megastores, channel unification, creative succession planning) than Buffett's hands-off style would sanction.

## Blind spots
- **The autonomy-preserving model didn't protect its own author.** Jatahy's entire M&A doctrine is built on not imposing a boss on an acquired founder — yet when he became the subordinate co-founder in a merger he didn't fully control, he needed a court injunction to avoid exactly that treatment from Birman. The philosophy that worked when he held the controlling chair did not automatically generalize to when he didn't. (Money Times, mai/2026; Mercado&Consumo, mai/2026)
- **Personal-conduct critique from named market sources: "o Roberto não quer ter chefe."** Multiple investors quoted anonymously by NeoFeed attribute the Birman conflict specifically to Jatahy's own resistance to hierarchy/subordination, not just to legitimate strategic disagreement — a diagnosis made in March 2025, over a year before the 2026 injunction, suggesting market participants saw the pattern coming. A separate anonymous source goes further, calling him "muito orgulhoso e uma pessoa muito difícil... a razão pela qual nenhum executivo de ponta tem ficado no grupo," pointing to roughly 30 non-executive director departures and two rounds of retention packages since the merger as corroborating evidence. Independent retail-trade reporting names concrete exits as symptoms of the same tension — Rony Meisler (Reserva's founder) and Paulo Kruglensky, the executive charged with integrating the two groups, who left roughly six months into the job — a churn pattern visible over a year before the 2026 injunction. (NeoFeed, mar/2025; NeoFeed, mai/2026; Central do Varejo, mar/2025)
- **Broken integration timeline on the group's largest deal.** He publicly promised Hering would be "completamente repaginada em 30 a 36 meses" from mid-2021 — i.e., by roughly mid-2024. In October 2022 (16 months in), InfoMoney reported the company still couldn't deliver 27% of what it sold to multibrand retailers and franchises against a sector target of 10%, and explicitly named a capability gap: "Dono da Farm e da Animale, o Soma não tinha experiência com produtos de maior volume e baixo custo." By March 2024, the group booked a R$2.9bn accounting write-down on Hering's assets — larger than analysts expected — even as the CFO denied overpaying. (InfoMoney, out/2022; Exame INSIGHT, mar/2024)
- **A cyclical, not resolved, pattern with Birman.** March 2025 "quase divórcio" rumors → June 2025 public "bandeira branca" reconciliation → May 2026 injunction and dueling arbitration filings. Each reconciliation was publicly framed by Jatahy as durable ("nunca houve nada sério," "um precisa do outro"), and each one broke down within roughly a year. A reader should weight his real-time characterizations of the Birman relationship accordingly. (NeoFeed, mar/2025; NeoFeed, jun/2025; Mercado&Consumo, mai/2026)
- **The 2017 Animale labor-conditions question was answered emotionally, not structurally.** Asked directly about the Ministério do Trabalho's finding of slave-labor-like conditions among Bolivian immigrant seamstresses supplying Animale, his on-record 2020 response leads with "dor" and "um filho" and cites a R$10M/year traceability investment — but does not engage the structural accountability argument made by Repórter Brasil's Carlos Juliano Barros in the original 2017 investigation: that a brand able to guarantee product quality/design standards from its suppliers can equally guarantee labor conditions. (Exame, dez/2017; Exame, "a moda pode ser rentável")
- **Concentrated, founder-level deal-making with limited early board/minority buy-in.** Reporting on the run-up to the Arezzo-Soma merger noted institutional shareholders on both sides "não estão felizes com o acordo e não foram consultados sobre os termos," and that Soma's own board showed more internal resistance to the deal than Arezzo's — a governance pattern that, in hindsight, foreshadowed the founder-level conflict that would surface two years later. (NeoFeed, jan/2024)
