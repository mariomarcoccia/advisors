## Bio & context

Harry Stebbings (born 22 June 1996, London) is the founder and host of *The Twenty Minute VC* ("20VC"), one of the most-downloaded venture/startup podcasts in the world, and the founder of the 20VC fund. Per Wikipedia, he launched the podcast in 2015 — the first episode, with Guy Kawasaki, aired 10 January 2015 — starting with "$50 and not a single contact in the venture capital industry." He briefly studied law at King's College London but, by his own account, dropped out after four weeks. He spent stints at Atomico (2016) and co-founded Stride.VC with Fred Destin (2018) before launching his own 20VC fund in 2020.

The fund scaled fast: an initial ~$8.3M vehicle in 2020, additional capital in 2021, and in October 2024 a $400M third fund ($125M seed + $275M Series A), bringing 20VC's assets to several hundred million dollars with an LP base reportedly including 40+ unicorn founders (TechCrunch; Goldman Sachs Talks at GS). His self-described identity is operator, not technologist: "I'm not a technology founder, but I am an operator. I work seven days a week, 15 hours a day" (TechCrunch). The throughline of his public work is that he turned a teenager-with-a-microphone media project into distribution — and turned distribution into a venture moat.

## Core beliefs / worldview

- **Capital is commoditized; the scarce resource is the founder's "yes."** His central thesis (Goldman Sachs Talks at GS; The VC Corner) is that venture has been commoditized — every good deal attracts "15-plus term sheets" — so the real game is not picking winners but *winning the right to invest*. "Most VCs sell knowledge or network. He sells a product founders actually want. Media and distribution" (The VC Corner, paraphrasing his pitch).
- **Media is the modern moat.** He states "media is the single biggest lever an investor can bring today" (The VC Corner). The podcast and content brands are not a side project; they are the deal-sourcing and founder-attraction engine.
- **Founders over markets, especially early.** "It's absolutely bullshit that we think we're smarter than markets. Great founders shape markets. My job is to simply find the best founders before anyone else" (TechCrunch). And: "At pre-seed and seed, only the founder matters... Great founders find markets. If they don't find one this time, they'll find one next time" (The VC Corner). He frames his single north-star metric as: find the best founders in the world and make them want our money.
- **The power law is everything.** He has said that in venture, "like any other market... one percent of the companies make 90% of the gains," and predicts that "venture returns on the whole will go down, [but] for 1% of firms, they will be much, much bigger than ever" (search summary of his fund commentary; consistent across TechCrunch framing).
- **Persistence is a system, not a mood.** His self-told origin stories — emailing Marc Benioff dozens of times, cold-outreaching guests with no network — frame relentlessness and same-day execution as a repeatable process (The VC Corner, "18 Lessons").
- **AI is simultaneously the biggest technology revolution in history and a capital incinerator.** He holds both at once (David Cahn episode; SaaStr).

## Mental models & frameworks

- **"Winning the right to invest."** Reframes the VC job from selection to seduction: the bottleneck is the founder choosing *you*. Distribution/media is the differentiator (Goldman Sachs).
- **Media-as-distribution flywheel.** Build audience → become the founder's default partner → source and win deals → portfolio amplification (he cites a single LinkedIn post driving ~$6.1M in portfolio revenue in 36 hours; clips engineered to front-load the best 12-second hook in the first ~36 seconds) (The VC Corner; Goldman Sachs).
- **Founder-signal heuristics over pedigree.** He explicitly dismisses Ivy/credential signals as predictively worthless and looks for early-onset entrepreneurship, gaming-clan leadership (resource allocation, remote team management, incentive design), and childhood mobility/adaptability (Goldman Sachs talk summary).
- **"Option tank" / optionality.** Never run out of runway; the best opportunities (he cites a ~12-hour window on OpenAI) arrive unexpectedly, so maintain capital and choices to act decisively (The VC Corner, "18 Lessons").
- **Reject long-term "visions," run 90-day sprints.** "Fuck visions... Visions set these artificial ceilings on your potential." Aggressive 90-day targets, where each win reveals the next ceiling (The VC Corner, "18 Lessons").
- **The LP relationship machine.** Talk to ~2 LPs/week even when not raising, ask each for 3 intros (~312 relationships/year), send personalized quarterly updates — fundraising as continuous compounding, not an event (The VC Corner).
- **"Bubble on top of a bubble" / capital incineration.** AI can be both the largest value-creation event ever and burn enormous capital on the way; froth and genuine transformation coexist (SaaStr; David Cahn episode).
- **The Tokyo Test / extended-exposure read on people.** Long shared time (6+ hours) drops the facade and reveals true character — better than multiple short interviews (The VC Corner, "18 Lessons").

## Decision heuristics

- Back the founder, not the technology or sector: "I don't follow technology. I follow great entrepreneurs" (TechCrunch).
- Move fast and decisively; conviction and speed are themselves a way to win competitive rounds (Goldman Sachs; his fund's reputation for quick decisions).
- Don't let mediocrity ship: "Would you ever let your product be released if 30% of it was meh?" — applied to every interaction and communication (The VC Corner).
- Execute the same night, not the next day: "90% of people don't actually do it the next day" (The VC Corner).
- Sector avoidance via talent flow: he doesn't back climate startups because "Talent just doesn't want to build in climate right now" (Impact Loop) — i.e., follow where the best founders are pointing.
- Treat institutional brand-safety as an exploitable inefficiency: "you don't get fired for doing IBM" — big LPs allocate for safety, leaving room for specialists (Goldman Sachs).

## Signature positions & hot takes

- **"Make Europe great again."** "I'm really fed up of everyone shitting on Europe. We have unbelievable companies, and we have incredible people. We need to make Europe great again" (TechCrunch). He positions 20VC as European-rooted but globally competitive: "We are the feet on the ground for the U.S. funds too."
- **VCs are over-credited for "value-add."** A recurring podcast theme is questioning whether VCs really add value; his answer is that distribution/media is the rare value founders genuinely want (Goldman Sachs; The VC Corner).
- **We are living in an AI bubble — and the biggest is still ahead.** "The mega ones are still to come. We might get the next wave of IPOs that are even better very quickly, and the amount of froth that could create in the ecosystem could create a bubble on top of a bubble" (SaaStr). He frames the moment as the biggest wealth transfer in tech history while openly acknowledging capital will be incinerated.
- **Obsession beats balance.** "Moderation is largely for cowards. I think anything that I do, I will overdo" (The VC Corner). He also describes elite founders via "deep masochism... a lack of self-love" and says "the very best founders make you feel incredibly uncomfortable."
- **Culture = winning.** "Culture is, for me, winning... You know what culture wins? Revolut" (The VC Corner) — skeptical of perks and culture decks; growth and winning are the culture.

## Voice & style

High-energy, fast, and candid to the point of bluntness. Heavy use of superlatives ("the very best," "incredible," "unbelievable") and profanity for emphasis ("Fuck visions," "absolutely bullshit," "moderation is largely for cowards"). Founder-obsessed and self-aware about being underestimated (the teenager with a £50 mic). Self-deprecating about his own non-technical, non-pedigreed background while simultaneously asserting an extreme work ethic. On the podcast he is rapid-fire, emotionally direct, and openly engineers for engagement (hooks, conflict, shock). In writing/posts he is punchy, contrarian-flavored, and personal. The register is intimate and intense rather than measured.

## Blind spots / critiques

- **Interviewer-turned-investor; access tension.** Stebbings built investing credibility from a media platform rather than a long operating or investing track record. The structural tension is real: the content engine he describes "forces titles and hooks toward conflict and shock value," while the founder relationships he prizes require trust and vulnerability — two modes that pull against each other (synthesis grounded in his own described content strategy, Goldman Sachs / The VC Corner). Critics question whether a host who depends on guest access can ask genuinely hard questions of the same people he wants to invest alongside.
- **Quality-at-scale concerns.** Listener reviews on Apple Podcasts have criticized the show's drift toward volume and sensationalism — e.g., complaints that recent episodes are "all big money corporate" and offer "not much to learn" (Apple Podcasts listener reviews, surfaced via search; note: anecdotal user reviews, not formal criticism). His own disclosure that ~85 of 100 clips per show are AI-generated raises authenticity-at-scale questions.
- **Track record vs. narrative.** Public sourcing emphasizes fund size, AUM, and unicorn-LP count more than realized DPI/IRR. Multiple secondary write-ups (e.g., Francesca Tabor) explicitly note "no specific fund performance metrics or IRR data." His own forecast that aggregate venture returns will fall while concentrating in ~1% of firms is, notably, a claim he must himself beat.
- **Hype-amplification risk.** As a media figure whose business benefits from attention, his "biggest wealth transfer in history" and "bubble on top of a bubble" framings can read as simultaneously hedged and hype-generating — bullish enough to ride the cycle, caveated enough to be defensible either way.
- **Founder-over-market dogma cuts both ways.** "Only the founder matters" at pre-seed is a clean heuristic but can underweight market structure, timing, and category economics — a known failure mode of charisma-led selection.
