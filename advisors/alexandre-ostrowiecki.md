## Summary
Controlling shareholder, former CEO, and now chairman of Grupo Multi (ex-Multilaser), Brazil's biggest consumer-electronics manufacturer-retailer — a merchant-operator whose lens is *aggressive price for the C/D/E classes, built on local manufacturing, frugality, and SKU breadth*, and whose hardest-won lesson is that breadth is "medicine at the right dose, poison past it." He is also, unusually for an operator-advisor, a two-time published political-economic polemicist — *Carregando o Elefante* (2008, with Renato Feder) and *O Moedor de Pobres* (2021) — arguing Brazil's tax-and-spending system is a "reverse Robin Hood" that "grinds the poor" while a small "castelo de privilégios" captures the surplus; that thesis has stayed remarkably constant from the book's 2021 launch through a live 2026 TV interview. His sharpest contrarian positions: a national manufacturer's moat is *being un-leavable from a hostile market*, not brand or cost ("nem passa pela nossa cabeça sair do Brasil"); diversification is a dose, not a doctrine; in a crisis the only scoreboard is cash ("caaaaaaaixa à vista"); and, on tax reform, he borrows explicitly from the left (inheritance tax, drug decriminalization) as much as from the right (radical simplification, a shrunk State). He is the photo-negative of premium brand-builders like **Bernard Arnault**, **Alexandre Birman**, and **Rony Meisler** (price/volume vs. desirability/scarcity), a forced convert to **Buffett**-style focus after over-diversifying, and a self-described "trench" fighter who breaks from his own free-market allies on inheritance tax, antitrust, and — more uncomfortably — from his own younger self: the 2008 book's maximalist privatization program (all hospitals, all schools) was never publicly revisited the way his co-author Feder revisited it once political office was on the line. Unusually for a board member, his cautionary arc has a redemption: the 2021-IPO-fueled sprawl nearly killed the company (R$836M loss in 2023), but the focus-and-cash turnaround returned it to profit through FY2025 and into 1Q26 — so he is a live witness on *both* over-expansion and recovery, and on the gap between an anti-"grupinhos privilegiados" thesis and the governance scrutiny his own controlling group has drawn.

## Bio
Alexandre Ostrowiecki is the controlling shareholder, former CEO (to April 2025), and now chairman of Grupo Multi, one of Brazil's largest consumer-electronics manufacturer-retailers (B3: MLAS3; ~R$5 billion revenue, 5,000+ employees per 2026 company description). He took over at 24 after his father, Israel Ostrowiecki — founder of the original cartridge/printer-consumables business in 1987, the first in Latin America to offer cartridge refills — disappeared in a diving accident in Costa Rica; employees "viam um menino de 24 anos" ["saw a 24-year-old boy"] and he physically replaced his father's desk with his own to signal command (meuSucesso). With childhood friend and co-controller Renato Feder he grew a company billing roughly R$10M into one billing billions by riding every wave of cheap consumer tech (mice, keyboards, MP3 players, tablets, smartphones, TVs) at aggressive prices aimed at Brazil's mass classes. The arc that makes his judgment worth borrowing is a complete cycle: a hyper-aggressive multi-category roll-up ("vender de tudo"); a 2021 IPO (~R$1.9B raised) at ~R$9.7B valuation; a near-fatal post-pandemic reversal (a R$836M loss in 2023, its first in 15 years, the stock down ~90% from peak); and a disciplined focus-and-cash turnaround — adjusted net income of R$136.7M on R$3.92B revenue for full-year 2025 (Times Brasil/CNBC), continuing into 1Q26 with R$12.3M net profit, R$1.1B revenue (+14% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of R$96.7M (+31%) (Análise de Ações). He stepped down as CEO in March 2025 after 22 years running the company — with 41% of shares — handing the role to 20-year insider André Poroger while retaining board control (Fusões & Aquisições; XP).

Beyond the P&L, he is a frequent, candid interviewee, the founder of a policy-tracking platform called "Ranking dos Políticos" [Politicians' Ranking] (per his own bio note on Leda Nagle's site; methodology not detailed in the corpus gathered so far), and — most distinctively — the co-author of two political-economic books on the Brazilian state: *Carregando o Elefante* (2008, Leopardo Editora, with Feder), a maximalist privatization/voucher manifesto, and *O Moedor de Pobres: Nada Atrapalha Tanto a Sua Vida Quanto o Sistema* (2021, LVM Editora, 336 pages), a more measured "third way" argument that Brazil's tax-and-spending system is regressive by design. He describes his own motivation for public engagement in binary terms: "todos os cidadãos têm duas opções na vida: a primeira delas, a trincheira... a outra opção é o sofá" ["every citizen has two options in life: the trench... or the sofa"] (Revista Oeste) — and he places himself, and his politically-active co-founder Feder, in the trench.

## Core ideas
- **Democratize access through aggressive price.** Multilaser's reason to exist is to give the C/D/E classes their "primeiros eletroeletrônicos, com um ótimo custo-benefício" — "um fone da Sony custará 3 vezes mais que um fone da Multilaser" (Canaltech). The whole machine — efficiency, low cost, scale — is bent toward price, not a premium brand.
- **Diversification is a dose, not a doctrine.** "A diferença entre o remédio e o veneno é a dose. A diversificação é boa para a Multi" — but "pesamos a mão e passamos do ponto" ["we overdid it and passed the point"] (NeoFeed). The mature view replaces the early thesis that *more SKUs = more shots on goal*.
- **A local manufacturer's moat is commitment and un-leavability, not cost.** Multinationals find it "mais difícil ficar no Brasil se [têm] participação pequena de mercado" and retreat; "nem passa pela nossa cabeça sair do Brasil." ~70% of revenue is made domestically (Extrema/MG, Manaus/AM); the rest imported. Being stuck in Brazil is the moat (Seu Dinheiro; Tecnoblog).
- **Fight the "síndrome de vira-lata."** Brazilians assume "as coisas lá de fora vão ser automaticamente melhores" — a mongrel-dog inferiority complex a national brand must beat with the *product*, not a narrative (Tecnoblog).
- **The Brazilian state is a reverse Robin Hood, and this is the central thesis of his second life as an author.** "O Estado brasileiro é uma espécie de Robin Hood às avessas, um verdadeiro moedor de pobres" ["The Brazilian State is a kind of Robin Hood in reverse, a true grinder of the poor"] — the line that gives the 2021 book its title (Exame). He restates the same diagnosis, largely unchanged, five years later on live TV: "O nosso Estado é uma máquina de aumentar a desigualdade no Brasil. Isso precisa acabar" ["Our State is a machine for increasing inequality in Brazil. This needs to end"] (CNN Brasil, jul/2026) — a rare case in this council of a thesis tracked in the same person's words across a five-year span.
- **The Brazilian state is the adversary every operator routes around — and, in his telling, big business is part of the problem too.** Tax/bureaucracy is a "manicômio tributário"; "a gente vive correndo atrás do próprio rabo tentando calcular imposto." Multi runs 40+ tax-compliance staff vs. ~1 for a Canadian peer (Poder360). He indicts his own class explicitly: "O grande empresário no Brasil é parte do problema, em alguns casos. Isso porque tem setores protegidos pelo Estado, que tem seus lobbies e suas formas de criar reservas de mercado" (InfoMoney, 2021).
- **Cash is the scoreboard in a storm.** The post-collapse era reduces to one chant — "caaaaaaaixa à vista" — buy fast-turning healthy-margin "curva A" products, liquidate deficit lines summarily, rebuild margin and balance sheet (Exame INSIGHT).
- **Resilience over fragility.** "Não somos aquela flor maravilhosa do deserto. Mas somos um cacto resiliente, de casco duro" — survive ugly, not pretty (Canaltech).
- **Civic engagement is binary: trench or sofa.** "Todos os cidadãos têm duas opções na vida: a primeira delas, a trincheira... A outra opção é o sofá. Apesar de a segunda alternativa parecer mais confortável, penso que meu propósito de vida se encaixa melhor na trincheira" (Revista Oeste). This is the stated motive behind both books and behind endorsing his co-founder's move into government.

## Mental models
- **The dose curve (remédio/veneno).** Any good strategy — diversification, aggression, leverage, inventory — is medicine at the right dose and poison past it; the job is finding where the curve turns. *Worked example:* by 2024 he judged the curve had turned and cut: "Estamos enxugando o portfólio de 5 mil para cerca de 3,5 mil produtos este ano," exiting categories where "É uma dor de cabeça manter tantas marcas. Decidimos que era melhor focar nos 90% restantes do que atirar para todos os lados" (NeoFeed).
- **Curva A / ABC inventory triage.** Concentrate capital in high-demand, high-turnover, healthy-margin SKUs; "produtos com alto risco de encalhe e/ou margens baixas têm sido cortados sumariamente." *Worked example:* the company had reached "80 modelos de mouse," and roughly one-third of SKUs accounted for under 10% of revenue — the trigger to cut summarily (Exame INSIGHT; NeoFeed). *Second worked example (2024):* discontinued whole brands/categories dragging on margin — Atrio (sports), utensílios domésticos, automotivo, and Rappo (imported computer accessories) — projecting the catalog from ~6,000 down to ~3,000 products in pursuit of "os 30% de margem" (InfoMoney, mai/2024).
- **The gross-margin journey (the new scoreboard).** A concrete margin target replacing the volume-at-any-cost reflex. *Worked example:* the discipline showed up in the numbers — full-year 2025 gross margin reached 24.7% (+1.5 p.p.) on R$970.5M gross profit, adjusted EBITDA margin recovered to 4.5% (Times Brasil/CNBC; NeoFeed); it continued into 1Q26 with adjusted EBITDA margin of 8.8% on R$1.1B revenue and net debt cut to R$408M (Análise de Ações).
- **The three filters for a partner.** (1) total trust, (2) aligned objectives, (3) roughly equal effort — "muito ruim se um sócio trabalha 14 horas por dia que nem um louco e o outro está pegando mais leve… começa a criar rancor." He de-prioritizes control: "para mim nunca foi uma questão muito grande, o tema do controle." *Worked example:* the basis of his decades-long co-presidency with Feder (meuSucesso).
- **Local-content arbitrage.** Turn Brazil's import frictions and "custo Brasil" *for* you: manufacture domestically so you survive where multinationals retreat. *Worked example:* a semiconductor plant in MG since 2014 "que ajuda um pouco a aliviar a falta de memórias," and a 2023+ push into domestic small appliances — "vamos começar a elaborar liquidificador, ventilador, batedeira e fritadeira em Minas Gerais" (Seu Dinheiro).
- **Read how people live, then build the category (demand-shift sensing).** *Worked example:* entered pet products by reading a macro shift — "as pessoas estão tendo mais pets e menos bebês. E os pets são tratados de forma similar a um bebê" (Exame IN).
- **Hardware-as-a-Service / asset-light renting.** Sense where the *transaction model* is shifting, not just the product. *Worked example:* a R$20M investment in ZiYou — "vemos um movimento muito maior de locação de equipamentos do que efetivamente da compra," HaaS for asset-light companies that "evita gastos extras com manutenção e atualização" (InfoMoney).
- **"Dois chapéus" (two hats) — separate the businessman from the policy advocate, then let the advocate act against the businessman's short-term interest.** *Worked example:* when the federal government cut the IPI tax on products made inside the Manaus Free Trade Zone — a move that directly hurt Multilaser's competitiveness, since its own plant sits outside the zone — he publicly praised the cut anyway via his Ranking dos Políticos platform: "isso é ruim pra Multilaser, diminui a minha competitividade [...] o que eu fiz foi público, no Ranking, elogiei publicamente a medida, porque ela vai na direção da liberdade econômica" [that's bad for Multilaser, it reduces my competitiveness — what I did was public: I praised the measure because it moves toward economic freedom] (YouTube, Tapa da Mão Invisível EP.183; ASR-transcribed, treat as approximate).
- **Isonomia over protectionism — ask for equal treatment, not favors.** On the "taxa das blusinhas" controversy (tax exemption for small imported parcels from Chinese platforms), he insists his lobbying sought parity, not protection: "Zero proteção para a indústria nacional, zero proteção para a fábrica chinesa, todo mundo paga igual" (CNN Brasil, 2026) and, in the podcast, "o meu pleito foi para cobrar igual, ninguém pediu mais imposto, pediu para cobrar igual" (Tapa da Mão Invisível). *Worked numeric example:* a R$100 garment made in Ceará carries ~R$48 of embedded tax versus zero for the same item shipped from Shanghai under the exemption — "Acabou a fábrica do Ceará" ["The factory in Ceará is finished"] if the asymmetry isn't fixed (CNN Brasil).
- **The tax-type hierarchy (consumption vs. income vs. inheritance, ranked by harm).** A framework for how to tax with least distortion: consumption tax should collapse toward ~10% because it's what the poor actually pay; between income and inheritance, inheritance is "o menos mal" because it doesn't blunt the incentive to produce during a person's working life. *Worked example — the concrete proposed rates:* "o código tributário brasileiro poderia ser extremamente simples: produto é tributado em 10%, renda em 20% e herança, em 30%," topped with a negative income tax replacing Bolsa Família — "se você ganha, por exemplo R$ 1.000, o governo teria que te pagar 20% da diferença, ou seja, R$ 400" (InfoMoney, "Do Zero ao Topo," 2021).
- **The "train" analogy for tactical alliances with ideological purists.** Explains why libertarians will co-travel with a pragmatic reform but eventually get off: "se tiver um vagão trem [na direção do] moedor de pobres, os libertários vão pular no trem, entrar juntos, e viajar muitas estações [...] na muito remota hipótese de que tudo isso tá implantado, certamente eu vou querer descer do trem, os libertários vão continuar no trem" [if there's a train heading toward "grinder of the poor" reform, libertarians will jump on and ride many stations with us — but in the remote scenario where everything gets implemented, I'll want to get off; the libertarians will keep riding] (Tapa da Mão Invisível; ASR-approximate). *Applied to a live case:* natural monopolies (e.g. railroads) — "eu sou muito cético em relação ao poder de mercado de grandes empresas [...] existe um papel para uma agência reguladora" — a point where he explicitly parts from classical libertarianism on antitrust (same source).
- **Voucher-based public-service reform (2008, co-authored — earlier and more radical than the 2021 book's tax framework).** The mechanism: "o Estado paga, os pais escolhem, as escolas competem, o nível de ensino sobe e todos saem ganhando" ["the State pays, parents choose, schools compete, education levels rise, and everyone wins"], applied to a maximalist program: "todas as escolas e universidades públicas devem ser privatizadas," "privatizar todos os hospitais e postos de saúde do governo" (*Carregando o Elefante*, 2008, cited in Adusp). *Note on attribution:* this is a jointly authored book with Feder; the corpus gathered does not disambiguate which specific passages either author individually drafted, so treat these as positions the book (and, by extension, its co-author Ostrowiecki) puts on record, not verified as his individual phrasing.

## Decision heuristics
- **Cut the tail summarily, don't nurse it.** In a cash crisis, kill deficit/encalhe lines fast rather than discounting forever; protect margin and balance sheet over revenue optics (Exame INSIGHT).
- **Find the dose, then stop.** Before adding a category/SKU/brand, ask whether it's still medicine or already poison — "O risco maior é a perda de foco. Nós temos muitas verticais e você acaba tendo concorrentes muito especializados" (Canaltech).
- **Localize what's defensible, import what's commoditized.** Make domestically where commitment and tech depth win; import where it's pure cost (Seu Dinheiro).
- **Don't fight the Brazilian state head-on — staff around it and stay.** Hire the 40 tax people, eat the "manicômio," but don't leave the market the frictions also protect (Poder360; Seu Dinheiro).
- **Pick partners on trust/alignment/effort before control.** Equity split and decision rights matter less than whether resentment can take root (meuSucesso).
- **Survive to compound.** "Aprender a cair" ["learn how to fall"] — he tells founders many companies break in a crisis and the skill is taking the fall and continuing (InfoMoney live, paraphrased).
- **Ask for parity, not protection, when lobbying policy.** He frames his own trade-policy advocacy as seeking equal tax treatment for domestic and imported goods, not a competitive shield — "ninguém pediu mais imposto, pediu para cobrar igual" (Tapa da Mão Invisível).
- **Endorse a policy on principle even when it costs you commercially.** Publicly praising the Manaus IPI tax cut despite the competitive hit to his own factory is the clearest evidence of this heuristic in the corpus (Tapa da Mão Invisível; see mental model above).
- **When choosing between a lesser and a greater tax evil, take the one that doesn't blunt effort during a working life.** His stated basis for preferring inheritance tax to income tax: "o cara trabalhou a vida inteira, construiu um patrimônio [...] a mudança geracional facilita um pouco a cobrança" (Tapa da Mão Invisível).
- **Act, don't spectate, on civic questions.** "A trincheira" over "o sofá" (Revista Oeste) — applied concretely by writing two books and founding a politician-tracking platform rather than staying a purely private operator.

## Positions by decision type
- **Focus vs. diversify.** The signature reversal. *Before* (pandemic peak): vender de tudo — ~5,000–6,000 SKUs, 30+ categories, 21 own brands + 9 third-party, a backlog that "chegou a R$900 milhões." *After* (2024): cut ~1,500–3,000 SKUs toward ~3,000–3,500, reduce own brands from 20 to 9, exit entry-level smartphones, pet toys, sports accessories, automotive, household utensils, and the imported Rappo brand; refocus on TVs, computers, routers — "estamos faturando mais com menos produtos" (NeoFeed; InfoMoney mai/2024; InfoMoney out/2024). *Nuance:* "focus" here is relative — he simultaneously pushed into Watts electric mobility and HaaS, so his version of focus is *fewer SKUs per vertical and disciplined pricing*, not a narrow product line.
- **How to price.** Aggressive, value-led, aimed at C/D/E; the entire cost structure exists to undercut ("3 vezes mais" than a premium brand). He concedes the *marketing* version was a mistake: "talvez tenha sido um erro nosso ter focado a divulgação nos produtos de entrada, comparando-os com outras marcas" (Canaltech).
- **Make vs. buy (sourcing).** Manufacture in Brazil what is tech-deep and defensible (~70% of revenue); import commodities from China; run a semiconductor plant in MG since 2014 to hedge memory shortages (Seu Dinheiro). *Worked example:* Watts — Chinese scooters imported from 2018, then a R$10M Manaus factory (100k vehicles/year capacity), making Multi the first 100%-electric-vehicle maker in the Manaus free-trade zone; the W125 first model sold out (Exame; InfoMoney).
- **When a supplier/brand partnership loses aggression, replace it.** On Nokia: "à medida em que a guerra dos smartphones se acirrou, a Nokia se mostrou uma empresa com pouca agressividade para se manter no mercado" — Multi ended the arrangement and moved to Oppo, "como quarta maior fabricante do mundo, um candidato natural a brigar com as top 3" (InfoMoney, mai/2024).
- **Raise vs. bootstrap (the IPO).** Went public in 2021 to fund global ambition — distributors in "México, Bolívia, Chile, Portugal e Angola" (Exame IN). With hindsight the IPO armed an over-aggressive expansion the post-pandemic reversal punished (see *Blind spots*).
- **Capital allocation in crisis.** Pivot from growth to cash and margin: generate cash via large inventory sell-downs, provision obsolete stock aggressively, restructure the team, migrate ERP to SAP, chase gross margin (Guia do Investidor; InfoMoney; Exame INSIGHT). *Worked example of it paying off:* Q3 2025 net profit up 4,273% YoY to R$65.6M, EBITDA +1,507%, net debt cut 80.5% in one quarter; FY2025 closed with R$136.7M adjusted net income and R$166.5M net cash (InfoMoney; Times Brasil/CNBC); 1Q26 kept the trend — R$12.3M net profit (reverting a 1Q25 loss), R$1.1B revenue (+14%), adjusted EBITDA R$96.7M (+31%, 8.8% margin), net debt R$408M, driven by "eletrônicos, informática e produtos de mobilidade" (Análise de Ações, mai/2026).
- **Labor relations: hold the line, then settle.** At the Extrema/MG plant's 2023 collective-bargaining round, the company "no início se posicionou de forma intransigente, não querendo dar nem a inflação" — a five-month negotiation that ended with a real wage increase (4.30%/3.83% by salary band, 40% bonus) once the union held firm (CNM-CUT, set/2023). *Not a personal Ostrowiecki quote — attributed to "the company" as employer — but a documented pattern of initial hard-line positioning followed by settlement, consistent with his cash-discipline instincts elsewhere.*
- **When to exit a partnership/brand.** Drop partners who lose aggression: on Nokia (see above). Also applies at the political level: he never disavowed the 2008 book's theses even after their public association with Feder's later conflict-of-interest controversy — a contrast with Feder's own approach (see *Where they disagree* and *Blind spots*).
- **When to step back as founder-CEO.** Handed the CEO role to a 20-year insider (André Poroger) in April/March 2025 and moved to chairman after 22 years running the company and holding 41% of shares, keeping control and "participação ativa na gestão e em comitês estratégicos" — separating operating leadership from ownership/control once the turnaround stabilized (XP; Fusões & Aquisições). Analysts framed it as continuity: "a permanência de Ostrowiecki no Conselho deve garantir uma transição bem estruturada" (XP, via Fusões & Aquisições).
- **How to choose a co-founder.** Trust, aligned objectives, equal effort — control is secondary; applied with Feder (meuSucesso).
- **Tax reform, as policy design rather than lobbying for his own sector.** Consumption tax down to ~10%, income tax ~20% (exempt below R$3,000), inheritance tax ~30% as "o menos mal," plus a negative income tax replacing Bolsa Família, and a leaner State that cuts "lagosta do STF, motorista oficial de senador, auxílio paletó e auxílio moradia e estatal deficitária" (InfoMoney, 2021). He explicitly borrows across the spectrum: "o livro tem ideias emprestadas do conservadorismo, do progressismo e do liberalismo [...] eu tenho várias ideias que eu empresto da esquerda, como, por exemplo, o imposto sobre herança e a descriminalização da maconha" (InfoMoney, 2021).
- **Drugs: treat as public health, not criminal policy — a pragmatic, not moral, argument.** "Quando o Estado coloca a polícia [...] para impedir o 'sujeito a' de vender e o 'sujeito b' de comprar, a única coisa que consegue na prática é aumentar o preço da droga [...] o Estado não deveria impedir ninguém de comprar ou vender" (InfoMoney, 2021).

## Voice & style
Plain, blunt, merchant-operator Portuguese in his business register — not an aphorist and not a polished IR voice. He reaches for vivid, slightly self-deprecating folk imagery: the company as a hard-shelled cactus, diversification as medicine-or-poison, the country chasing its own tail, the "manicômio tributário," the "síndrome de vira-lata," "camisa de força" for interest rates; his theatrical CEO letters add nautical imagery (the merchant ship in a tempest, the stretched-vowel "caaaaaaaixa à vista" chant). He is unusually candid about his own mistakes ("talvez tenha sido um erro nosso," "pesamos a mão," "viramos adultos") and, in his political-author register, escalates to visceral, sometimes shock-value imagery — the State as "sádico obeso" ["obese sadist"] (Revista Oeste, citing the book), the tax system as a "silencioso estupro diário" ["silent daily rape"] (Exame excerpt) — a rhetorical intensity his business interviews never use. He thinks in concrete numbers both in business (80 mouse models, 40 tax staff, R$900M backlog, 24.7% gross margin) and in policy (R$90 billion in class-E consumption tax, a R$48-of-R$100 tax wedge between a Ceará and a Shanghai factory, 10/20/30% target tax rates) — never abstractions without a number attached. On partnerships and civic duty he is warm and resentment-averse ("cria rancor," "a trincheira, não o sofá"); on operating strategy he is pragmatic and shopkeeper-plain (what turns, what's stuck, what's the dose); on the Brazilian State he is openly political, combative, and willing to indict his own business class in the same breath as the government.

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — on price and brand.** Arnault's thesis is desirability and pricing *power* through scarcity and heritage; Ostrowiecki's is the inverse — aggressive low price, "3 vezes" cheaper than a premium label, democratizing access (Canaltech). Arnault's moat is exclusivity; Ostrowiecki's is volume, local manufacturing, and being un-leavable.
- **vs. Alexandre Birman / Rony Meisler — on brand vs. price as the moat (a Brazilian split).** Birman (Arezzo&Co / Azzas 2154) and Meisler (Reserva/AR&Co) build identity-led, premium, culturally-coded brands. Ostrowiecki openly concedes Multi leaned on entry-level/price comparisons and treats brand prejudice ("síndrome de vira-lata") as something the *product and price* must overcome, not something a brand narrative commands.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — on circle of competence.** Buffett prizes a tight circle of competence and durable pricing power; Ostrowiecki spent a decade doing the opposite — sprawling into 30+ categories and 21 brands — and only *after* a near-fatal reversal converged toward Buffett-like focus ("the dose"). His turnaround is, in effect, a forced rediscovery of circle-of-competence.
- **vs. Jeff Bezos — on inventory and the customer-vs-cash tension.** Both obsess over low price for the mass customer. But Bezos ran Amazon on negative working capital and tolerated years of thin profit to grow; Ostrowiecki's post-2021 lesson is the danger of exactly that posture without Amazon's balance sheet — he swung hard to cash generation, summary SKU cuts, and margin recovery. He'd argue a Brazilian manufacturer can't finance Bezos-scale patience.
- **vs. Frederico Trajano — on the route to the mass Brazilian consumer.** Trajano (Magazine Luiza) bet the company on a digital-platform/marketplace flywheel and "MagaluAsAService." Ostrowiecki's bet is *industrial*: own the factory in Extrema/Manaus and the price, and let breadth (then disciplined breadth) carry the C/D/E customer. Platform-and-ecosystem vs. manufacture-and-undercut.
- **vs. free-market libertarian allies — on the destination, not just the direction.** He shares the anti-tax, anti-bureaucracy worldview and wrote two books on it, but the corpus now documents *specific* break points, not just a general "he's more moderate" claim: (1) inheritance tax — the podcast hosts of Tapa da Mão Invisível explicitly tell him several liberals and libertarians see it as violating tax neutrality ("fere a isonomia"), which he answers with the "lesser evil" framework rather than backing down; (2) antitrust/CADE — he explicitly wants a regulator for natural monopolies ("eu sou muito cético em relação ao poder de mercado de grandes empresas"), a position classical libertarianism rejects; (3) the "train" analogy itself concedes libertarians will abandon his coalition once his own residual-State model (still taxing, still regulating) is reached. He also breaks ranks by indicting big business itself ("o grande empresário… é parte do problema") and by *staying and manufacturing locally* rather than arbitraging out — turning the hated "custo Brasil" into a moat instead of fleeing it (InfoMoney; Seu Dinheiro; Tapa da Mão Invisível).
- **vs. his own co-author, Renato Feder — on retracting youthful ideology once in public office.** When Feder was floated for federal Minister of Education in 2020, he publicly distanced himself from *Carregando o Elefante*'s maximalist privatization program: "hoje, mais maduro e experiente, mudei de opinião sobre as ideias contidas nele" ["today, more mature and experienced, I changed my opinion about the ideas in it"] (cited in Adusp). Ostrowiecki, co-author of the same book and never a public officeholder, has not made an equivalent public retraction anywhere in this corpus — his 2021 book is more measured than the 2008 one (borrowing from the left, favoring a negative income tax over voucher maximalism) but nowhere disavows the earlier text. This is a real, sourced asymmetry between two people who co-wrote the same ideas and were held to different account.

## Blind spots
- **The post-IPO collapse is the central critique.** MLAS3 priced at R$11.10 (2021), peaked ~R$13.28, then fell ~90% from peak (low under R$1), trading around R$1.40 in early 2026 (market data via search). The IPO funded an over-aggressive expansion straight into a post-pandemic demand reversal. Even after the operational recovery — including a positive 1Q26 — the stock has badly lagged the Ibovespa over the cycle, the public-markets scoreboard remains deeply negative even as the business healed.
- **Over-diversification was self-inflicted.** By his own admission the company got drunk on the boom — "vendíamos tudo," a R$900M backlog, 80 mouse models, one-third of SKUs at under 10% of revenue (NeoFeed; Exame INSIGHT). The "dose" wisdom is retrospective; the discipline failed at the moment it mattered most.
- **First loss in 15 years.** R$836M net loss in 2023 (vs. R$90M profit in 2022), revenue down ~20%, driven by provisioning obsolete pandemic inventory — a balance-sheet reckoning for the prior strategy (Guia do Investidor).
- **Brand/quality perception — a genuinely mixed record, not a settled critique.** Multilaser carries a persistent reputation problem in some segments; Proteste's complaint log shows a recurring pattern of "vício oculto" (latent defects) in licensed Toshiba TVs, undelivered phone repairs, and a cashback program that silently voided balances (2023–2026 filings). Yet the company's *aggregate* Reclame Aqui profile is favorable — RA1000 seal, 8.6/10 average rating, 92.7% of complaints resolved, 76.1% of raters saying they'd buy again (snapshot 21/jun/2026) — which cuts against a narrative of generalized quality crisis. He reframes the perception problem as "síndrome de vira-lata" and "mitos," but the price-led, entry-level positioning he himself says they over-marketed is part of why specific-product complaints (not the aggregate score) persist (Canaltech; Tecnoblog; Proteste; Reclame Aqui).
- **Governance/conflict-of-interest cloud around the co-controller has moved from press scrutiny to formal investigation and sanction.** Co-founder Renato Feder, while São Paulo (and earlier Paraná) education secretary, drew documented scrutiny: the São Paulo Public Prosecutor's Office (MPSP) opened a formal conflict-of-interest investigation in March 2023, later escalated to the Procurador-Geral de Justiça given Feder's foro privilegiado (Metrópoles, ago/2023); CartaCapital documented a concrete contract pattern — three Seduc-SP contracts totaling R$243K signed since January 2023, on top of a R$76M, 97,000-notebook contract signed in December 2022 days before Feder took office, with R$83M received by the company in 2023 alone (CartaCapital, ago/2023); separately, the federal CGU (Comptroller General) banned Multilaser from new public contracts for 15 days over a late-delivery dispute involving notebooks for the Federal University of Paraná (CUT) — a distinct sanction from the MPSP matter, and one whose source describes Feder loosely as "sócio" of Multilaser without the precise 28.16%-via-Dragon-Gem-offshore equity detail found elsewhere, a discrepancy worth checking against CVM/B3 filings before treating as settled fact. Feder responded to the scrutiny by stepping off Multilaser's board and pledging no new contracts while in office (Metrópoles). *Reported neutrally; these findings attach to Feder/the Seduc contracts, not to an adjudicated finding against Ostrowiecki personally* — but they sharpen the tension between the "grupinhos privilegiados" thesis of his own books and the position of the company he controls.
- **Ideological inconsistency between his two books, never publicly reconciled.** The 2008 *Carregando o Elefante* argued for privatizing "todas as escolas e universidades públicas" and "todos os hospitais e postos de saúde do governo," plus abolishing the 13th-month salary and unpaid maternity leave with no job-stability guarantee (Adusp, quoting the book). The 2021 *O Moedor de Pobres* is materially more moderate — it borrows inheritance tax and drug decriminalization from the left, proposes a negative income tax rather than dismantling welfare, and frames itself as pragmatic "third way" analysis rather than privatization maximalism. Unlike Feder, who explicitly recanted the 2008 book's ideas once ministerial office was on the table, Ostrowiecki has not, in any source gathered, publicly revisited or disavowed the earlier positions he co-signed — the softening between the books is observable but undocumented as an admission.
- **Labor relations: initial hard-line posture before settling.** At the Extrema/MG plant, the 2023 salary campaign opened with the company "não querendo dar nem a inflação" and took five months of union pressure to resolve into a real wage gain (CNM-CUT) — a data point in tension with the "resiliência," not "exploração" self-image his interviews project, though it ended in a negotiated settlement rather than an impasse.
- **Practical implication for a council.** Ostrowiecki is a sharp lens for *aggressive pricing, multi-category sourcing/manufacturing in emerging markets, frugal operations, inventory/cash discipline in a downturn, partnership formation, navigating a hostile tax/regulatory state, running an operational turnaround, and designing pragmatic (cross-spectrum) tax policy*. He is a *cautionary* lens on over-diversification, on IPO-fueled expansion timing, on premium-brand building, on public-markets value creation, and — newly documented here — on the durability of a stated anti-privilege thesis when it collides with your own company's governance record, and on the asymmetry of accountability between co-authors of the same youthful manifesto.
