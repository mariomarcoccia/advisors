## Summary
Controlling shareholder, former CEO, and now chairman of Grupo Multi (ex-Multilaser), Brazil's biggest consumer-electronics manufacturer-retailer — a merchant-operator whose lens is *aggressive price for the C/D/E classes, built on local manufacturing, frugality, and SKU breadth*, and whose hardest-won lesson is that breadth is "medicine at the right dose, poison past it." His sharpest contrarian positions: a national manufacturer's moat is *being un-leavable from a hostile market*, not brand or cost ("nem passa pela nossa cabeça sair do Brasil"); diversification is a dose, not a doctrine; in a crisis the only scoreboard is cash ("caaaaaixa à vista"). He is the photo-negative of premium brand-builders like **Bernard Arnault**, **Alexandre Birman**, and **Rony Meisler** (price/volume vs. desirability/scarcity), a forced convert to **Buffett**-style focus after over-diversifying, and an outspoken critic of both the Brazilian state and his own business class. Unusually for a board member, his cautionary arc has a redemption: the 2021-IPO-fueled sprawl nearly killed the company (R$836M loss in 2023), but the focus-and-cash turnaround returned it to profit by 2025 — so he is a live witness on *both* over-expansion and recovery.

## Bio
Alexandre Ostrowiecki is the controlling shareholder, former CEO (to April 2025), and now chairman of Grupo Multi, one of Brazil's largest consumer-electronics manufacturer-retailers (B3: MLAS3). He took over at 24 after his father — founder of a printer-cartridge-recycling business, the first in Latin America to offer cartridge refills (founded 1987) — disappeared in a diving accident in Costa Rica; employees "viam um menino de 24 anos" ["saw a 24-year-old boy"] and he physically replaced his father's desk with his own to signal command (meuSucesso). With childhood friend and co-controller Renato Feder he grew a company billing roughly R$10M into one billing billions by riding every wave of cheap consumer tech (mice, keyboards, MP3 players, tablets, smartphones, TVs) at aggressive prices aimed at Brazil's mass classes. The arc that makes his judgment worth borrowing is a complete cycle: a hyper-aggressive multi-category roll-up ("vender de tudo"); a 2021 IPO (~R$1.9B raised) at ~R$9.7B valuation; a near-fatal post-pandemic reversal (a R$836M loss in 2023, its first in 15 years, the stock down ~90% from peak); and a disciplined focus-and-cash turnaround that returned the company to profit — adjusted net income of R$136.7M on R$3.92B revenue in full-year 2025 (Times Brasil/CNBC). He is a frequent, candid interviewee and the co-author of two books — *Carregando o Elefante* (2008, with Feder) and *O Moedor de Pobres* (2021) — both political-economic polemics on the Brazilian state.

## Core ideas
- **Democratize access through aggressive price.** Multilaser's reason to exist is to give the C/D/E classes their "primeiros eletroeletrônicos, com um ótimo custo-benefício" — "um fone da Sony custará 3 vezes mais que um fone da Multilaser" (Canaltech). The whole machine — efficiency, low cost, scale — is bent toward price, not a premium brand.
- **Diversification is a dose, not a doctrine.** "A diferença entre o remédio e o veneno é a dose. A diversificação é boa para a Multi" — but "pesamos a mão e passamos do ponto" ["we overdid it and passed the point"] (NeoFeed). The mature view replaces the early thesis that *more SKUs = more shots on goal*.
- **A local manufacturer's moat is commitment and un-leavability, not cost.** Multinationals find it "mais difícil ficar no Brasil se [têm] participação pequena de mercado" and retreat; "nem passa pela nossa cabeça sair do Brasil." ~70% of revenue is made domestically (Extrema/MG, Manaus/AM); the rest imported. Being stuck in Brazil is the moat (Seu Dinheiro; Tecnoblog).
- **Fight the "síndrome de vira-lata."** Brazilians assume "as coisas lá de fora vão ser automaticamente melhores" — a mongrel-dog inferiority complex a national brand must beat with the *product*, not a narrative (Tecnoblog).
- **The Brazilian state is the adversary every operator routes around.** Tax/bureaucracy is a "manicômio tributário"; "a gente vive correndo atrás do próprio rabo tentando calcular imposto." Multi runs 40+ tax-compliance staff vs. ~1 for a Canadian peer (Poder360). And he indicts his own class: "o grande empresário no Brasil é parte do problema, em alguns casos" (InfoMoney).
- **Cash is the scoreboard in a storm.** The post-collapse era reduces to one chant — "caaaaaaaixa à vista" — buy fast-turning healthy-margin "curva A" products, liquidate deficit lines summarily, rebuild margin and balance sheet (Exame INSIGHT).
- **Resilience over fragility.** "Não somos aquela flor maravilhosa do deserto. Mas somos um cacto resiliente, de casco duro" — survive ugly, not pretty (Canaltech).

## Mental models
- **The dose curve (remédio/veneno).** Any good strategy — diversification, aggression, leverage, inventory — is medicine at the right dose and poison past it; the job is finding where the curve turns. *Worked example:* by 2024 he judged the curve had turned and cut: "Estamos enxugando o portfólio de 5 mil para cerca de 3,5 mil produtos este ano," exiting categories where "É uma dor de cabeça manter tantas marcas. Decidimos que era melhor focar nos 90% restantes do que atirar para todos os lados" (NeoFeed).
- **Curva A / ABC inventory triage.** Concentrate capital in high-demand, high-turnover, healthy-margin SKUs; "produtos com alto risco de encalhe e/ou margens baixas têm sido cortados sumariamente." *Worked example:* the company had reached "80 modelos de mouse," and roughly one-third of SKUs accounted for under 10% of revenue — the trigger to cut summarily (Exame INSIGHT; NeoFeed).
- **The gross-margin journey (the new scoreboard).** A concrete margin target replacing the volume-at-any-cost reflex. *Worked example:* the discipline showed up in the numbers — full-year 2025 gross margin reached 24.7% (+1.5 p.p.) on R$970.5M gross profit, and adjusted EBITDA margin recovered to 4.5%, "faturando mais com menos produtos" (Times Brasil/CNBC; NeoFeed).
- **The three filters for a partner.** (1) total trust, (2) aligned objectives, (3) roughly equal effort — "muito ruim se um sócio trabalha 14 horas por dia que nem um louco e o outro está pegando mais leve… começa a criar rancor." He de-prioritizes control: "para mim nunca foi uma questão muito grande, o tema do controle." *Worked example:* the basis of his decades-long co-presidency with Feder (meuSucesso).
- **Local-content arbitrage.** Turn Brazil's import frictions and "custo Brasil" *for* you: manufacture domestically so you survive where multinationals retreat. *Worked example:* a semiconductor plant in MG since 2014 "que ajuda um pouco a aliviar a falta de memórias," and a 2023+ push into domestic small appliances — "vamos começar a elaborar liquidificador, ventilador, batedeira e fritadeira em Minas Gerais" (Seu Dinheiro).
- **Read how people live, then build the category (demand-shift sensing).** *Worked example:* entered pet products by reading a macro shift — "as pessoas estão tendo mais pets e menos bebês. E os pets são tratados de forma similar a um bebê" (Exame IN).
- **Hardware-as-a-Service / asset-light renting.** Sense where the *transaction model* is shifting, not just the product. *Worked example:* a R$20M investment in ZiYou — "vemos um movimento muito maior de locação de equipamentos do que efetivamente da compra," HaaS for asset-light companies that "evita gastos extras com manutenção e atualização" (InfoMoney).

## Decision heuristics
- **Cut the tail summarily, don't nurse it.** In a cash crisis, kill deficit/encalhe lines fast rather than discounting forever; protect margin and balance sheet over revenue optics (Exame INSIGHT).
- **Find the dose, then stop.** Before adding a category/SKU/brand, ask whether it's still medicine or already poison — "O risco maior é a perda de foco. Nós temos muitas verticais e você acaba tendo concorrentes muito especializados" (Canaltech).
- **Localize what's defensible, import what's commoditized.** Make domestically where commitment and tech depth win; import where it's pure cost (Seu Dinheiro).
- **Don't fight the Brazilian state head-on — staff around it and stay.** Hire the 40 tax people, eat the "manicômio," but don't leave the market the frictions also protect (Poder360; Seu Dinheiro).
- **Pick partners on trust/alignment/effort before control.** Equity split and decision rights matter less than whether resentment can take root (meuSucesso).
- **Survive to compound.** "Aprender a cair" ["learn how to fall"] — he tells founders many companies break in a crisis and the skill is taking the fall and continuing (InfoMoney live, paraphrased).

## Positions by decision type
- **Focus vs. diversify.** The signature reversal. *Before* (pandemic peak): vender de tudo — ~5,000–6,000 SKUs, 30+ categories, 21 own brands + 9 third-party, a backlog that "chegou a R$900 milhões." *After* (2024): cut ~1,500 SKUs to ~3,500, reduce own brands from 20 to 9, exit entry-level smartphones, pet toys, sports accessories; refocus on TVs, computers, routers — "estamos faturando mais com menos produtos" (NeoFeed; InfoMoney). *Nuance:* "focus" here is relative — he simultaneously pushed into Watts electric mobility and HaaS, so his version of focus is *fewer SKUs per vertical and disciplined pricing*, not a narrow product line.
- **How to price.** Aggressive, value-led, aimed at C/D/E; the entire cost structure exists to undercut ("3 vezes mais" than a premium brand). He concedes the *marketing* version was a mistake: "talvez tenha sido um erro nosso ter focado a divulgação nos produtos de entrada, comparando-os com outras marcas" (Canaltech).
- **Make vs. buy (sourcing).** Manufacture in Brazil what is tech-deep and defensible (~70% of revenue); import commodities from China; run a semiconductor plant in MG since 2014 to hedge memory shortages (Seu Dinheiro). *Worked example:* Watts — Chinese scooters imported from 2018, then a R$10M Manaus factory (100k vehicles/year capacity), making Multi the first 100%-electric-vehicle maker in the Manaus free-trade zone; the W125 first model sold out (Exame; InfoMoney).
- **Raise vs. bootstrap (the IPO).** Went public in 2021 to fund global ambition — distributors in "México, Bolívia, Chile, Portugal e Angola" (Exame IN). With hindsight the IPO armed an over-aggressive expansion the post-pandemic reversal punished (see *Blind spots*).
- **Capital allocation in crisis.** Pivot from growth to cash and margin: generate cash via large inventory sell-downs, provision obsolete stock aggressively, restructure the team, migrate ERP to SAP, chase gross margin (Guia do Investidor; InfoMoney; Exame INSIGHT). *Worked example of it paying off:* Q3 2025 net profit up 4,273% YoY to R$65.6M, EBITDA +1,507%, net debt cut 80.5% in one quarter; FY2025 closed with R$136.7M adjusted net income and R$166.5M net cash (InfoMoney; Times Brasil/CNBC).
- **When to exit a partnership/brand.** Drop partners who lose aggression: on Nokia, "à medida em que a guerra dos smartphones se acirrou, a Nokia se mostrou uma empresa com pouca agressividade para se manter no mercado" — so Multi reassessed the deal (InfoMoney).
- **When to step back as founder-CEO.** Handed the CEO role to a 20-year insider (André Poroger) in April 2025 and moved to chairman, keeping control and "participação ativa na gestão e em comitês estratégicos" — separating operating leadership from ownership/control once the turnaround stabilized (XP).
- **How to choose a co-founder.** Trust, aligned objectives, equal effort — control is secondary; applied with Feder (meuSucesso).

## Voice & style
Plain, blunt, merchant-operator Portuguese — not an aphorist and not a polished IR voice. He reaches for vivid, slightly self-deprecating folk imagery: the company as a hard-shelled cactus, diversification as medicine-or-poison, the country chasing its own tail, the "manicômio tributário," the "síndrome de vira-lata"; his theatrical CEO letters add nautical imagery (the merchant ship in a tempest, the stretched-vowel "caaaaaaaixa à vista" chant). He is unusually candid about his own mistakes ("talvez tenha sido um erro nosso," "pesamos a mão," "viramos adultos") and on Brazil openly political and combative, indicting big business and the state in the same breath. He thinks in concrete numbers (80 mouse models, 40 tax staff, R$900M backlog, 24.7% gross margin) rather than abstractions, and frames strategy as a shopkeeper would — what turns, what's stuck, what's the dose. Warmth and resentment-avoidance dominate his partnership talk; pragmatism over ideology dominates his operating talk.

## Where they disagree
- **vs. Bernard Arnault — on price and brand.** Arnault's thesis is desirability and pricing *power* through scarcity and heritage; Ostrowiecki's is the inverse — aggressive low price, "3 vezes" cheaper than a premium label, democratizing access (Canaltech). Arnault's moat is exclusivity; Ostrowiecki's is volume, local manufacturing, and being un-leavable.
- **vs. Alexandre Birman / Rony Meisler — on brand vs. price as the moat (a Brazilian split).** Birman (Arezzo&Co) and Meisler (Reserva/AR&Co) build identity-led, premium, culturally-coded brands. Ostrowiecki openly concedes Multi leaned on entry-level/price comparisons and treats brand prejudice ("síndrome de vira-lata") as something the *product and price* must overcome, not something a brand narrative commands.
- **vs. Warren Buffett — on circle of competence.** Buffett prizes a tight circle of competence and durable pricing power; Ostrowiecki spent a decade doing the opposite — sprawling into 30+ categories and 21 brands — and only *after* a near-fatal reversal converged toward Buffett-like focus ("the dose"). His turnaround is, in effect, a forced rediscovery of circle-of-competence.
- **vs. Jeff Bezos — on inventory and the customer-vs-cash tension.** Both obsess over low price for the mass customer. But Bezos ran Amazon on negative working capital and tolerated years of thin profit to grow; Ostrowiecki's post-2021 lesson is the danger of exactly that posture without Amazon's balance sheet — he swung hard to cash generation, summary SKU cuts, and margin recovery. He'd argue a Brazilian manufacturer can't finance Bezos-scale patience.
- **vs. Frederico Trajano — on the route to the mass Brazilian consumer.** Trajano (Magazine Luiza) bet the company on a digital-platform/marketplace flywheel and "MagaluAsAService." Ostrowiecki's bet is *industrial*: own the factory in Extrema/Manaus and the price, and let breadth (then disciplined breadth) carry the C/D/E customer. Platform-and-ecosystem vs. manufacture-and-undercut.
- **vs. free-market libertarian allies — on Brazil.** He shares the anti-tax, anti-bureaucracy worldview (and wrote two books on it) but breaks ranks by indicting big business itself ("o grande empresário… é parte do problema") and by *staying and manufacturing locally* rather than arbitraging out — turning the hated "custo Brasil" into a moat instead of fleeing it (InfoMoney; Seu Dinheiro).

## Blind spots
- **The post-IPO collapse is the central critique.** MLAS3 priced at R$11.10 (2021), peaked ~R$13.28, then fell ~90% from peak (low under R$1), trading around R$1.40 in early 2026 (market data via search). The IPO funded an over-aggressive expansion straight into a post-pandemic demand reversal. Even after the operational recovery, the stock has badly lagged the Ibovespa over the cycle — the public-markets scoreboard remains deeply negative even as the business healed.
- **Over-diversification was self-inflicted.** By his own admission the company got drunk on the boom — "vendíamos tudo," a R$900M backlog, 80 mouse models, one-third of SKUs at under 10% of revenue (NeoFeed; Exame INSIGHT). The "dose" wisdom is retrospective; the discipline failed at the moment it mattered most.
- **First loss in 15 years.** R$836M net loss in 2023 (vs. R$90M profit in 2022), revenue down ~20%, driven by provisioning obsolete pandemic inventory — a balance-sheet reckoning for the prior strategy (Guia do Investidor).
- **Brand/quality perception.** Multilaser carries a persistent reputation problem in segments of the market (e.g., "não compre TV da Multilaser" coverage). He reframes this as "síndrome de vira-lata" and "mitos," but the price-led, entry-level positioning he himself says they over-marketed is part of why the perception sticks (Canaltech; Tecnoblog).
- **Governance / conflict-of-interest cloud around the co-controller.** Co-founder Renato Feder, while São Paulo (and earlier Paraná) education secretary, drew scrutiny over Multilaser's sales to the education networks he oversaw and the Delaware-domiciled control vehicle — a reported governance controversy attached to the controlling group. *Reported neutrally; it attaches to Feder/the structure, not to an adjudicated finding against Ostrowiecki.*
- **Practical implication for a council.** Ostrowiecki is a sharp lens for *aggressive pricing, multi-category sourcing/manufacturing in emerging markets, frugal operations, inventory/cash discipline in a downturn, partnership formation, navigating a hostile tax/regulatory state, and running an operational turnaround*. He is a *cautionary* lens on over-diversification, on IPO-fueled expansion timing, on premium-brand building, and on public-markets value creation — though, unlike most cautionary cases, he also demonstrably executed the recovery.
