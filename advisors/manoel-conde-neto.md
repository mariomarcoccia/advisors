## Summary
Manoel Conde Neto is the founder, chairman and president of Farma Conde, a São Paulo-coast pharmacy chain he and his wife Claudia built from a rented garage in Ubatuba (1993) into a ~500-store, ~8,000-employee, R$1.7-billion-revenue regional operator across SP and MG. His operating voice is numbers-first and benchmark-driven — though a single long-form profile (Metrópole, Jul 2024) shows he also carries a personal, faith-rooted success creed ("Fé, trabalho, lealdade com a concorrência e foco, são os ingredientes para atingir um objetivo") and an explicit low-cost-access mission ("nosso propósito é levar medicamento e saúde para todo mundo, num custo mais baixo"). On the business itself he runs an explicit capital-allocation rule ("nossa atuação começa na unidade madura, não na nova"), reads Amazon's disruption of CVS as a forcing function to modernize or die, and is betting the company's next decade on vertical integration — turning a 15%-of-revenue private-label business into a 50%-of-revenue in-house pharmaceutical manufacturer. He is also, on the public record, a convicted figure: in August 2023 a São José dos Campos court found him guilty of money laundering, falsidade ideológica and tax crimes tied to a medicine tax-evasion scheme (Operação Monte Cristo), a case in which his own 2021 testimony — "o mercado não é ingênuo... se o remédio custa R$8 e está custando R$4, é porque não foi recolhida a substituição tributária" — reads as a market-rationality justification for the very fraud he was convicted of enabling, with his sentence commuted via a plea/collaboration agreement. The tension is sharpest against his own stated creed: the same man who lists "lealdade com a concorrência" (fair play toward competitors) as a core value, and whose declared purpose is the *lowest* price, was convicted of a scheme whose entire mechanism was undercutting competitors on price through unpaid tax. He sits opposite operators who lead with culture or purpose (Jonas Marques Neto's psychology-first Pague Menos, Luiz Seabra's philosophy-first Natura, Miguel Krigsner's franchisee-protection ethic at O Boticário) and opposite Warren Buffett's "reputation is the asset you cannot rebuild" — Conde's own words are the clearest documented counter-case on this council for how a market-rationalizing frame can blur into a justification for fraud.

## Bio
Manoel Conde Neto describes himself as a merchant before anything else — by his own account he was selling garlic and lemon at the street market from age nine ("Sempre fui comerciante. Vendia alho e limão na feira, com 9 anos de idade. Continuo vendedor, só que agora vendedor de remédios" [I was always a merchant. I sold garlic and lemon at the market at nine years old. I'm still a salesman, just now a salesman of medicine]) — and only later took the legal-and-public-service route he frames as a search for stability rather than a calling ("Eu fiz concurso público para ter uma vida mais tranquila, mas voltei pro comércio que é a minha raiz" [I took the public-service exam to have a calmer life, but I went back to commerce, which is my root]). He worked in the banking and legal fields before spending roughly a decade as a police delegate (delegado de polícia) in Ubatuba, on São Paulo's northern coast — a posting he remembers as hard because of local crime ("Era uma cidade de praia, divisa com o Rio de Janeiro, então tinha muito crime, criminalidade alta. Foi difícil" [It was a beach town, bordering Rio de Janeiro, so there was a lot of crime, high criminality. It was hard]). It was in that period that his wife Claudia Conde — 25, newly graduated in pharmacy, with a six-month-old baby and unable to find work at local pharmacies because of her pregnancy — decided to open her own drugstore in 1993. The couple sold both cars they owned and rented a garage to house it, running the first "office" out of the *edícula* of their own home while a grandmother watched the baby; Claudia did everything herself at the start — "abria o estabelecimento, realizava as compras, recebia mercadoria, aplicava injeção" [opened the store, did the buying, received merchandise, gave injections]. Manoel joined the business and eventually left the police force once the chain approached two dozen stores, taking on the role of president while Claudia became vice-president leading community health outreach and their daughter Giovana grew into the marketing VP role. By January 2024 the chain had passed 360 points of sale; by 2025 it counted more than 500 owned stores plus 50 licensed units across São Paulo and Minas Gerais, close to 8,000 employees, and reported 2024 revenue of R$1.7 billion (with a stated internal target, as of January 2024, of R$1.5 billion for that year — see *Provenance notes* on the resulting figure discrepancy). The company is headquartered in São José dos Campos, where it lends its name to the Farma Conde Arena, the city's largest sports/events venue, run under a concession model with the municipality. Running alongside the growth story is a criminal one: in August 2023 Conde Neto was personally convicted (4 years, 8 months, converted to community service) for money laundering and tax crimes connected to a medicine tax-evasion scheme dating to the 2012–2017 period, a case the company had publicly distanced itself from in a 2020 statement asserting it was "em dia com suas obrigações tributárias" three years before his own conviction. He is worth reading less as a philosopher-founder and more as a hard-numbers regional operator — SKU counts, percentage targets, capital-allocation ordering — whose business judgment and legal record sit in direct, documented tension.

## Core ideas
The handful of convictions that recur across the sourced material — decision applications live in *Positions by decision type*; the quotable register in *Signature quotes*.

- **Consolidate the mature store before opening the new one — never invert the order.** Even while running an aggressive expansion pipeline (40 new drugstores plus a target of 250 franchisees by 2025), his stated priority sequence puts operational maturity of existing units ahead of growth: "Nossa atuação começa na unidade madura, não na nova. Jamais podemos inverter as prioridades." [Our approach starts with the mature unit, not the new one. We can never invert the priorities.] (Sincofarma SP)
- **Modernize or be disrupted — read internationally, apply locally.** He treats Amazon's encroachment on CVS in the U.S. drugstore market as a live predictive model for what happens to any pharmacy chain that doesn't invest in technology: "Basta observar o exemplo da Amazon no Exterior, que vem ganhando mercado sobre players tradicionais, como a CVS. Quem não se modernizar não terá vez." [Just look at the example of Amazon abroad, which has been gaining market share over traditional players like CVS. Whoever doesn't modernize won't have a chance.] (Sincofarma SP)
- **Vertical integration into manufacturing as the next growth engine.** Private label already funds part of the business; his stated ambition is to roughly triple its share of revenue by moving into medicine manufacturing itself, not just cosmetics/supplements: "O laboratório irá mudar a realidade da Farma Conde. Atualmente, nossa marca própria responde por 15% do faturamento. Com os medicamentos, queremos levar essa porcentagem a 50%." [The lab will change Farma Conde's reality. Currently our private label accounts for 15% of revenue. With medicines, we want to bring that percentage to 50%.] (Sincofarma SP)
- **Own physical capacity ahead of demand, against the cloud-first default.** The company invested R$25 million in on-premise data infrastructure sized for four times current store demand, explicitly rejecting the industry norm of scaling gradually on the cloud — a position the reporting frames as company philosophy rather than just an IT department's preference, since it appears alongside Conde Neto's own modernization rhetoric in the same piece. (Sincofarma SP — Alexandre Rosati, IT director, in the same visit where Conde Neto set the modernization agenda)
- **Franchising and owned stores as a single, hybrid growth engine — not a philosophy about the franchisee's stake.** Unlike some Brazilian franchisors, the documented pitch to franchisees is transactional and financial — negotiating power with suppliers, specialized training, revenue upside — rather than an ethical framework about protecting the franchisee's capital: "Estamos construindo parcerias sólidas e proporcionando aos nossos franqueados a possibilidade de crescimento sustentável e rentável." [We are building solid partnerships and giving our franchisees the possibility of sustainable and profitable growth.] (Mercado&Consumo)
- **Store investment can be justified by symbolic/community return, not just financial return.** The flagship two-story format opened in a residential condominium in his and Claudia's hometown of Ubatuba is explicitly framed as a debt of gratitude rather than a real-estate calculation: "Itamambuca é um presente para Ubatuba, que é historicamente a nossa casa. Queremos retribuir o carinho que a cidade sempre nos deu." [Itamambuca is a gift for Ubatuba, which is historically our home. We want to give back the affection the city has always given us.] (Mercado&Consumo)
- **A personal, faith-rooted formula for success — the one place his voice turns from operator to creed.** In the single long-form profile in this corpus (Metrópole, Jul 2024) he names an explicit four-part formula and frames his own role as custodial rather than sovereign: "Minha mãe e meu pai sempre tiveram fé em Deus e Jesus Cristo. Eu acredito que aqui (neste plano espiritual) sou passageiro e não piloto. Fé, trabalho, lealdade com a concorrência e foco, são os ingredientes para atingir um objetivo. É ter fé que tudo vai dar certo." [My mother and father always had faith in God and Jesus Christ. I believe that here (on this spiritual plane) I'm a passenger and not the pilot. Faith, work, loyalty toward the competition and focus are the ingredients to reach a goal. It's having faith that everything will work out.] The stated mission is likewise plain and access-first: "Nosso propósito é levar medicamento e saúde para todo mundo, num custo mais baixo." [Our purpose is to bring medicine and health to everyone, at a lower cost.] Read against his 2023 conviction, both the "lealdade com a concorrência" value and the lowest-cost mission carry a documented irony flagged in *Blind spots*. (Metrópole)
- **Market pricing is read as a diagnostic signal — a conviction that, on the record, doubled as a justification for fraud.** In sworn 2021 testimony (accessed by Fantástico/TV Globo and cited in the reporting on his conviction), he frames anomalous below-cost drug pricing as proof of an unrecorded tax mechanism somewhere in the chain — a piece of market literacy that, in context, functions as a rationalization for the scheme he was later convicted of participating in: "O mercado não é inocente, não é ingênuo, como eu também não sou ingênuo. Se o remédio custa R$8 e está custando R$4, é porque não foi recolhida a substituição tributária." [The market isn't innocent, it isn't naive, just as I'm not naive either. If the medicine costs R$8 and it's being sold for R$4, it's because the tax substitution wasn't collected.] (Panorama Farmacêutico)

## Mental models
Reusable lenses documented in the sourced material, each with a worked example.

- **"Unidade madura antes da nova" (mature-unit-first capital allocation).** Gist: whenever expansion and consolidation compete for capital or management attention, consolidation of what already exists wins the ordering, even when the expansion pipeline is large. *Worked example:* in the same 2024 interview where he confirms a pipeline of 40 new drugstores and a target of 250 new franchisees, he states the priority explicitly as mature-unit-first, "jamais podemos inverter as prioridades" — the growth plan and the discipline coexist, but the order is fixed. (Sincofarma SP)
- **Competitive benchmark-by-analogy (Amazon vs. CVS).** Gist: import a disruption pattern from a more mature foreign market as a predictive model for the local one, then size the internal investment budget to it. *Worked example:* the Amazon/CVS comparison is the stated rationale behind the R$100 million private-label lab, the R$25 million on-premise data infrastructure buildout, and stated interest in AI and quantum-computing partnerships — modernization spend justified as defense against a Brazilian equivalent of the same disruption. (Sincofarma SP)
- **Anticipatory infrastructure capacity, contrarian to "cloud-first."** Gist: size physical capacity for future demand up front instead of scaling incrementally as the market default does. *Worked example:* rather than "subir os dados na nuvem e ir aumentando a capacidade digital gradualmente," Farma Conde invested R$25 million to build on-premise infrastructure sized to handle four times its current store demand before it's needed — a deliberate inversion of the industry-standard scaling pattern. (Sincofarma SP)
- **Private label as a margin and independence lever.** Gist: instead of treating store-brand goods as a minor cosmetics/supplements sideline, use them as the mechanism for both margin capture and supply-chain independence, then scale the model into the regulated, higher-stakes category (medicines) once the infrastructure exists. *Worked example:* the R$100 million laboratory build (2,400 m², with a further 20,000 m² already earmarked for expansion) is funded specifically to take private label from 15% to a targeted 50% of revenue once Anvisa authorization for pharmaceutical manufacturing is in place. (Sincofarma SP; Exame)

## Decision heuristics
How he documents deciding under uncertainty and competing priorities.

- **Order capital and attention by operational maturity, not opportunity size.** Existing, mature stores get consolidated before new capital opens new ones — a fixed sequencing rule stated even when the expansion pipeline is large. (Sincofarma SP)
- **Keep geographic focus, but don't foreclose optionality — and, on this evidence, the optionality wins over time.** In January 2024 the documented company line (via operations director Fabiano Morais, in the same reporting Conde Neto's own quotes appear in) was discipline on the São Paulo metro region as the priority "onde entendemos que ainda temos muito espaço para explorar," while declining to rule out other regions — focus as a default, not a hard boundary. Six months later Conde Neto himself, in his own voice, converts that hedge into concrete out-of-region intent: from a base "em 123 municípios, em São Paulo e Minas Gerais," he states "Vamos para o Rio de Janeiro, Mato Grosso e Amazonas" — naming specific new states rather than a vague openness. The heuristic in practice: hold focus while the core market still has room, but name the next frontier explicitly once the pipeline supports it. (Sincofarma SP; Metrópole)
- **Let symbolic/community return sit alongside financial return in site selection.** The Ubatuba flagship-format decision is framed as a debt to be repaid to the town where the company was founded, not purely a real-estate calculation — a heuristic of "symbolic ROI" that appears to complement, not replace, the financial case for a location. (Mercado&Consumo)
- **When facing a multi-year criminal investigation, cooperate rather than litigate to the end.** The documented sequence — collaboration with the Ministério Público beginning in the 2012–2017 investigation window, a signed plea/collaboration agreement (colaboração premiada) homologated by the court, and a defense statement citing that cooperation as grounds to expect judicial pardon — reflects an explicit trade-off: reduced/converted sentence and continued business operation in exchange for admitted cooperation and reputational exposure, rather than contesting the case to the end. (Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **In a crisis, separate "this phase" from "the earlier phase" rather than deny everything.** The company's 2020 public statement — issued the same day an anti-fraud operation made headlines — denies involvement in the *current* phase of the investigation while explicitly admitting to having been a target of an *earlier* phase (2017), and frames that earlier episode as resolved and cooperative. This temporal-separation move is a documented crisis-communication pattern, not a personal Conde Neto quote (the statement speaks in the company's institutional voice). (Jornal do Povo Marília)

## Positions by decision type
Concrete, documented situations and what he did; inferences are flagged as such.

- **Raise vs. bootstrap.** Bootstrapped from nothing: the founding capital was the proceeds of selling the couple's two cars, and the first "office" was the *edícula* of their own home — no outside capital documented at founding or since. (Exame)
- **Focus vs. diversify (vertical integration into manufacturing).** Rather than staying a pure retailer, the company is diversifying upstream into medicine manufacturing (pending/obtained Anvisa authorization) on top of an existing cosmetics/supplements private-label lab — an explicit bet that owning production, not just distribution, is where the next decade of margin comes from. (Sincofarma SP; Exame)
- **Expansion vs. consolidation.** Documented, explicit priority: consolidate mature units first, even while running a large simultaneous pipeline (40 new stores, target of 250 franchisees by 2025) — these are not treated as mutually exclusive, but the *order* is fixed and stated publicly. (Sincofarma SP; Mercado&Consumo)
- **How to read below-cost pricing in a regulated category.** Documented position, given under oath: anomalous below-cost drug pricing is not treated as ordinary competitive behavior but as near-certain evidence that tax substitution somewhere in the chain wasn't paid — a market-literacy claim that, in the context it was given (testimony connected to his own tax-fraud conviction), functions simultaneously as an explanation of the very scheme he was convicted for. Flagged in *Blind spots* rather than presented as clean business wisdom. (Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **Facing prosecution: cooperate vs. fight.** Documented choice to sign a colaboração premiada (plea/collaboration agreement) rather than litigate the tax-evasion and money-laundering case to a final verdict — trading admitted cooperation and reputational exposure for a converted sentence and, per his defense's public statement, the expectation of judicial pardon. (Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **Store-format investment: financial case vs. symbolic case.** In at least one documented instance (the Itamambuca/Ubatuba flagship), the stated rationale for a differentiated, more expensive two-story format is explicitly the town's role as the company's birthplace — a symbolic-return criterion layered on top of, not instead of, the ordinary financial case for the location. (Mercado&Consumo)
- **Pivot vs. persevere.** Not documented in the sourced material — no interview or profile in this corpus surfaces a moment where he describes abandoning or fundamentally reworking a strategy. Left as an open gap rather than inferred (see *Provenance notes*).
- **When to fire / when to sell.** Not documented in the sourced material — no source touches personnel decisions, an exit, or a sale consideration. Left as an open gap rather than inferred (see *Provenance notes*).

## Voice & style
Where he speaks in his own words (the Sincofarma SP visit-piece, the Mercado&Consumo press coverage, and — most extensively — the Metrópole profile give first-person quotes), his default register is operational and number-forward, not aspirational or philosophical: percentages, SKU counts, reais figures, and named foreign benchmarks (Amazon, CVS) do the persuasive work, not adjectives. His characteristic rhetorical move is the *analogy-as-forecast* — pointing at a disruption that already happened in a more mature market (Amazon over CVS) and treating it as the predictive model for what will happen locally if the company doesn't spend ahead of it. He states growth targets as flat declaratives with a specific number attached ("queremos levar esse nível para 15 mil SKUs," "levar essa porcentagem a 50%," "totalizando R$1,5 bilhão em 2024") rather than ranges or hedges — a founder comfortable being held to a stated figure. The one register where the numbers drop away is hometown loyalty: describing the Ubatuba flagship store, he shifts to a debt-of-gratitude framing ("presente," "retribuir o carinho") not found anywhere else in the sourced material — the only moment the corpus shows him speaking in an emotional rather than operational register. His sworn 2021 testimony shows a third, harder-edged register: blunt market-insider framing ("o mercado não é inocente, não é ingênuo, como eu também não sou ingênuo") used to explain — and, in context, appears to rationalize — a fraud mechanism, rather than distance himself from it. The Metrópole profile adds a fourth, distinct register the shorter trade pieces never surface: a personal, faith-and-origin creed. Here he speaks of himself as "comerciante" since age nine, casts his own agency as custodial ("sou passageiro e não piloto"), names an explicit value formula ("Fé, trabalho, lealdade com a concorrência e foco"), gives a plain mission statement ("nosso propósito é levar medicamento e saúde para todo mundo, num custo mais baixo"), and lands on an unguarded humanism ("gosto de gente. Gosto de animais também"). This corrects what a corpus limited to the trade pieces would suggest: he *does* have a purpose-and-values register in his own voice — but it is personal, religious, and origin-rooted rather than the abstract corporate-philosophy register of a founder-philosopher, and it appears only in the one long-form profile that gave him room for it.

## Where they disagree
How this figure diverges from other named council members. Largely inferred from each side's documented record, since no source shows Conde Neto naming or directly responding to any of these figures — flagged accordingly.

- **vs. Jonas Marques Neto (Pague Menos) — numbers-first vs. people-first, in the same industry.** Jonas Marques Neto's documented operating principle at a national pharmacy chain is to talk to people before looking at numbers ("a primeira coisa que eu quis fazer foi falar com as pessoas," rather than the CFO's dashboards) and to trace organizational dysfunction to a psychology-room framework (the gap between "self ideal" and "self real"). Conde Neto's own documented voice never reaches for a comparable people/culture register — his stated priorities are capital-allocation order, SKU counts, and percentage targets. Same industry, same national-vs-regional competitive question each is implicitly answering, opposite registers for how to run the organization. (Extrapolation from each figure's documented public voice; no direct exchange between them is sourced.)
- **vs. Warren Buffett — reputation as the untouchable asset vs. a documented rationalization of market fraud.** Buffett's bright line, under maximum pressure at Salomon, was "lose a shred of reputation for the firm, and I will be ruthless," with the front-page test as the operating discipline. Conde Neto's own 2021 testimony — explaining below-cost drug pricing as proof of an unrecorded tax mechanism — reads, in the context of his own later conviction for exactly that mechanism, as the clearest documented case on this council of a founder's market-literacy framing sliding into a justification for the fraud itself, rather than a boundary against it. This is the sharpest, most direct tension in the corpus — not an inference, since both men's own words are on the record. (Panorama Farmacêutico; cf. Buffett's Salomon testimony)
- **vs. Miguel Krigsner (O Boticário) — what a franchisor owes the franchisee.** Krigsner's documented moral spine is explicit and ethical: "é preciso levar em conta, em primeiro lugar, o respeito ao capital do próximo," with a stated conviction that any deal favoring only one side "morre na praia." Conde Neto's documented franchise pitch, by contrast, is transactional and financial only — negotiating power, training, revenue upside — with no sourced statement about an obligation to protect the franchisee's capital as Krigsner frames it. Both scaled on franchisee capital; only one has a documented ethical framework for that relationship. (Mercado&Consumo; cf. Krigsner dossier)
- **vs. Luiz Seabra (Natura) — operator's numbers vs. founder-philosopher's values.** Seabra frames the business almost entirely in relational and philosophical terms ("a vida é um encadeamento de relações"), treating profit as a byproduct of purpose. Conde Neto's default operating voice is the inverse emphasis: SKU counts, percentage targets, capital sequencing, benchmark-by-analogy. He is not, on the fuller record, purpose-mute — the Metrópole profile gives him a genuine creed and mission ("nosso propósito é levar medicamento e saúde para todo mundo, num custo mais baixo") — but that register is personal, religious and origin-rooted (faith, work ethic, hometown gratitude), not a Seabra-style relational *philosophy of the business itself*. The contrast is therefore not values vs. no-values but two incompatible theories of what a founder's purpose language is *about*: for Seabra it is the operating system of the company; for Conde Neto it sits beside the numbers, personal rather than structural. ## Blind spots
Real, sourced limitations — not invented weaknesses.

- **A personal criminal conviction the business press does not mention.** In August 2023 a São José dos Campos court convicted him of money laundering, falsidade ideológica and tax crimes tied to a scheme (Operação Monte Cristo) alleged to have netted him at least R$50 million personally, part of a fraud investigators say cost Brazilian tax authorities more than R$10 billion. None of the four post-conviction business/profile pieces in this corpus (Exame, Sincofarma SP, Mercado&Consumo, and the Jul 2024 Metrópole profile) mention the case at all — he is covered purely as a growth-story founder in material published after the August 2023 conviction, and the Metrópole piece in particular runs a laudatory personal-values profile eleven months after the verdict without a word on it. That is a documented editorial blind spot in the trade and regional press covering him, not a claim about his character beyond the court record itself. (Panorama Farmacêutico; Exame; Sincofarma SP; Mercado&Consumo; Metrópole)
- **His own words are the clearest evidence against the "market is not naive" framing as clean business wisdom.** The same sentence that could read, out of context, as shrewd market literacy — "o mercado não é inocente, não é ingênuo" — was given as testimony connected to his own conviction for the fraud it describes. Any use of this quote as a general heuristic must carry that context; presented alone, it risks laundering a fraud rationalization as insight. (Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **His stated personal values collide with his own conviction.** In the Metrópole profile he names his formula for success as "Fé, trabalho, lealdade com a concorrência e foco" and his mission as bringing medicine "para todo mundo, num custo mais baixo." Both sit in direct, documented tension with the August 2023 conviction: the fraud he was found guilty of enabling worked precisely by undercutting competitors on price through unpaid tax substitution — i.e., the opposite of "loyalty toward the competition," and a lowest-cost achieved by evasion rather than efficiency. This is not an inference about hidden motive; it is the plain juxtaposition of his own stated value ("lealdade com a concorrência," Jul 2024) with the court's finding (Aug 2023) and his own 2021 testimony describing the mechanism. Any use of his values language must carry that record. (Metrópole; Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **A documented gap between compliance messaging and the legal outcome.** In October 2020, the company's own public statement asserted it was "em dia com suas obrigações tributárias" and had undergone governance improvements confirmed by internal and external audits. Three years later, the founder himself was personally convicted of money laundering and tax crimes tied to conduct from the 2012–2017 period — the exact window the 2020 statement was implicitly trying to close the book on. This is a documented factual tension between the company's compliance narrative and its founder's legal outcome, not a speculative one. (Jornal do Povo Marília; Panorama Farmacêutico)
- **Publicly reported revenue figures are internally inconsistent.** Across the sourced material: R$960 million (2021, per SBVC ranking cited in the Aug/2023 piece), R$1.23 billion (year prior to the Aug/2023 piece, i.e. roughly 2022), R$1.35 billion (cited by the company itself in the Jan/2024 Sincofarma SP piece as the prior year's — 2023 — figure), his own stated target of R$1.5 billion for 2024 (Jan/2024, same piece), and R$1.7 billion actually reported for 2024 (Apr/2025, Exame). These are plausibly a mix of different fiscal-year bases and reporting methodologies rather than a single contradiction, but the corpus does not reconcile them, and no source explains the gap between the founder's own R$1.5bn target and the R$1.7bn actually reported. Recorded as an open ambiguity rather than resolved. (Panorama Farmacêutico; Sincofarma SP; Exame)
